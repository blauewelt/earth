Dependency-Cone Explorer

Which past inputs can matter for a forecast at one ocean pixel? Every driver reaches only as far as its signal speed carries it and only as far back as its memory lasts — so the region a network should look at is a union of wedges, not a fixed cylinder.

How to read this

Pick a target and a forecast step Δt. Each driver p is a wedge in the (lag, distance) plane: an input observed ℓ before now sits Δt+ℓ before the target, so it can matter only inside |Δx| ≤ vp·(Δt+ℓ) and only while Δt+ℓ ≤ τp. Reach never falls below the driver's spatial correlation length Lcorr. The cylinder gives every lag the same 89-slot disc of radius 4 444 km; the cone gives each lag only the slots its union reach needs — panel C counts the difference. Roll shows why a one-step model still sees far: composing the stencil h times widens the effective cone by one Δt per step.

Caveats

  • v is the fastest relevant signal speed (waves, adjustment), not the mean flow.
  • τ is predictability, not causality — the driver may still act, its state is just no longer inferable.
  • Real cones are tilted: upstream, westward (Rossby), along boundaries (Kelvin). The anisotropy toggle is a sketch, not a model.
  • Monthly averaging turns anything with τ < Δt into forcing: it enters at lag 0 and is not itself predictable.
  • A fast driver acting through a slow medium (air T → heat flux → mixed layer) inherits the medium's memory and, at old lags, its advection speed.
  • These are ablations to run, not results. Parameters are order-of-magnitude illustrations.
