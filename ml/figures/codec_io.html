What goes into the codec, and what comes out

One overview of the input a stage-1 codec reads, the embedding it emits, what an FSQ bottleneck changes about that output — and the fitted ladder (E-048) built on top of plain FSQ.

The pipeline, with amounts

What an FSQ codec outputs — exactly

Your memory is right in spirit: FSQ makes the output discrete. But in the FSQ paper (Mentzer et al. 2023) the bottleneck's literal output is still a vector, not an integer: each of d dimensions is bounded (⌈L/2⌉·tanh(z)) and rounded to one of L fixed values, giving the quantized vector ẑ. The "tokens" are an enumeration view of that vector: because each dimension takes one of L values, ẑ is equivalently a tuple of d small integers, and the paper notes a bijection from the tuple to a single index in {1, …, ∏Lᵢ} — that index is what a downstream token model (MaskGIT, autoregressive LM) consumes. The paper keeps the product small on purpose: d < 10, every Lᵢ ≥ 5, codebook 2¹⁰–2¹⁶ (their recommended sets: [8,6,5] for 2⁸, [8,5,5,5] for 2¹⁰, [8,8,8,5,5,5] for 2¹⁶).

Our implementation, and the three equivalent readings of its output

The E-046 codec (--fsq-levels 8, d_z 32) applies exactly the paper's map inside to_z's output and emits the quantized continuous vector — the [T, P, 32] float array keeps its shape and dtype, the values just sit on the lattice. Integer codes are never materialized. Three readings of the same output:

  • As a vector: z_q ∈ lattice ⊂ R³², bounded at ±R per dimension.
  • As digits: 32 base-8 digits per pixel-bin — 3 bits × 32 = 96 bits. If you ever want literal tokens for an AR model, this is the token unit: 32 tokens of vocab 8 (or group 4 dims → 8 tokens of vocab 4096).
  • As one codebook index: 8³² ≈ 7.9 × 10²⁸ — not usable as a vocabulary. Our config is deliberately far off the paper's regime (32 dims vs their <10): we use FSQ as a bounded, discretized substrate for a continuous-input head, not to build a token vocabulary.

One distinction worth keeping crisp because the paper (ours) leans on it: where the lattice sits decides what it is. The same InputQuant map bolted onto the head's inputs (A9, ratio 0.4916 — the first arm to beat the pentad control) is "input quantization"; moved inside the codec's bottleneck it is FSQ proper (E-046/E-048), and only there does it change what the codec's output is.

What we built on top of plain FSQ: the fitted ladder

Plain FSQ fixes the lattice by decree: uniform spacing, saturation radius R = 2 ("let the encoder learn the scale in"). E-048 measured what that costs and replaced both decisions with a fit against the observed distribution of each z-dimension's pre-quantization activations:

The fit, per z-dimension (ml/fsq_ladder.py, --fsq-ladder auto)

  • Sample the distribution. Capture the run's own pre-quantization activations at scheduled steps (--fsq-auto-step 50,200,2000 — refit because the scale drifts; the last fit is what the checkpoint carries).
  • Fit the form. Uniform ladder (levels evenly spaced, bins companded by tanh) vs geometric ladder (magnitudes a, ca, c²a, … — bins uniform in log|v|), bases c ∈ {1.5, 2, 3, 4}.
  • Fit the scale too (the part that was missing): saturation radius R_d searched over candidates derived from the sample — 2σ_d, and p90 / p99 / max of |v_d| at ×½ and ×1.
  • Ties go to the default. The fit only moves a dimension where it measures a strict quantization-MSE gain, so auto can never silently differ from the shipped behaviour.
  • The fit is part of the model. Recorded in the checkpoint as one string (u:<R> / e<base>:<R> per dim); loaders rebuild from it and never refit. Legacy strings without :<R> reproduce archived codecs bit-identically.
  • One pre-registered caveat: at even L the uniform map is biased (+0.233 mean at L=8), which by itself favours the geometric form — the odd-L (L=7) contrast is registered to separate "exp wins because it compands" from "exp wins because it's unbiased".

Voxtral TTS, as the reference point

Close — the 37 is right, the 16 levels isn't. Per 12.5 Hz frame the Voxtral codec emits 37 tokens: 1 semantic token from an ordinary VQ codebook of 8,192 (13 bits) plus 36 acoustic dimensions, each FSQ-quantized to 21 uniform levels through a tanh bound (~4.4 bits/dim, not 4) — ≈2.14 kbps in total. Notably its AR model generates only the semantic token stream; the 36 FSQ dims are filled in by a flow-matching model. So Voxtral treats the FSQ dims exactly the way our stage-2 head does: as a discretized vector to condition on, not as a flattened vocabulary.

So what should the output be?

Three defensible answers, in increasing distance from what is running now:

  • Keep the quantized vector (current design): the head stays a continuous-input transformer, FSQ is purely an information restriction. Then the open E-048 question is the one that matters — give the latent an intrinsic bound (normalize before the lattice so the fit has a stationary target), or accept the sign code and size the lattice for it. The fitted ladder is the instrument either way.
  • Emit per-dimension digits as tokens (the Voxtral shape): 32 tokens of vocab 8 per pixel-bin. Buys an AR/masked-token training objective over the state; costs 32× the sequence length unless dims are grouped (4 dims → vocab 4096, 8 tokens).
  • The paper-faithful token: shrink to d≈6–8 dims at L∈[5,8] so one pixel-bin is one token from a 2¹²–2¹⁶ vocabulary. That is a much harsher bottleneck than d_z 32 — a different experiment about how much state a bin really carries, not a drop-in variant.
