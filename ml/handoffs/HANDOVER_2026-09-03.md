## 6 · Yesterday's failure taxonomy — four deaths, all fixed at the root

Read this before diagnosing anything that dies early; three of the four recur.

1. **Full disk, three times** (#530 Germany, #532 Virginia, and #518 the week
   before). A `Train` step writes a 31.6 GiB writable scratch copy of the
   tensor; `scripts/disk_hygiene.sh` was called with a 16 GB want, sized for
   the Z pull. Both boxes passed the check and died 30–60 s into the write
   with `Bus error` (exit 135). **Fixed (`4dab012`):** hygiene now raises its
   want to the largest `*_X.npy` in the cache + 4 GB, frees the tensor build
   inputs (`rg/`, `wind_daily/`, `truth/`, `glorys_pentad/`) as a tier when a
   decoded `X.npy` is present, and **exits 2** when it cannot get there — the
   workflow turns that into a refusal BEFORE the Train step instead of a death
   inside it (§5.16).
2. **The stage-2 held-out monitor OOMed a 24 GB card** (#529, E-064b). The
   deterministic monitor ran all 4,096 windows in ONE forward — a single
   10.2 GiB allocation at d_z 32 × K 144 × stencil 145 — and died at its first
   monitor. The run went **green with no `temporal.json`**, §7's classic
   signature. **Fixed (`3069d91`):** the pass is chunked at 512 like the FGN
   branch already was (row-wise forward ⇒ bit-identical result). The token arm
   survived only because its input is 5× smaller.
3. **A run cancelled on a mis-read pace** (#525, the d_z-8 parent). I read
   "≥ 0.45 s/step" from a step-5,000 push 45 minutes after the Train start and
   cancelled it as a token-expiry risk. That window also contained a 397 s
   probe and the scratch decode, so 0.46 was an UPPER bound mis-read as a
   floor; the fleet-watch session had already withdrawn the deadline in
   OVERVIEW ("it is training, do NOT cancel") and I had not pulled `main`
   before acting. Cost ~$1.3 and 1.5 h. **Rule re-learned: pull before acting
   on a written deadline; a pace reading that contains a probe is an upper
   bound.**
4. **A shared cache that is not keyed on what changed** (found, not suffered).
   `std_stats.npz` and the embed-cache `Z` name are keyed on the codec weights
   and the tensor sha — neither sees the anomaly statistics, which the holdout
   years determine. A warm box would have handed a terminal-holdout run the
   climatology of the 2009/2017/2023 codec. **Fixed (`7d9bd95`, `72eade6`):**
   the flat `std_stats.npz` is reserved for the legacy year set, every other
   codec gets `std_stats__hold-<blocks>.npz`, and the embed cache carries a
   `_hold-<blocks>` suffix in BOTH `temporal.embed_cache_path` and
   `embed_cache_sync.cache_name` whenever the effective years differ from the
   codec's.

## 9 · Open questions worth a session's attention

1. **Is the token's one-step win real skill or a smoother target?** A
   quantized latent may be easier to predict AND easier for persistence to
   predict; the ratio is computed inside each z-space. The roll (decoded, data
   space) is the arbiter, and it is cheap.
2. **Why does the d_z-8 codec reconstruct worse than the d_z-6 one?** Check
   run-480's schedule before calling it a width effect.
3. **The heads are uncalibrated by construction** — every one of them emits
   too much amplitude at too little correlation, and the whole MSSS deficit is
   that. Nobody has tried fitting the amplitude on training-year starts and
   applying it to held-out ones. It is arithmetic, not GPU.
4. **The LIM is the reference and nobody has tried it in embedding space.**
   Same fit, same battery, z instead of the standardised field.
5. **Who stopped the Virginia boxes at 20:42Z?** If a fleet-watch rule stops
   boxes on a schedule, it must read the JOBS endpoint first — a box with a
   live job is not idle burn.
