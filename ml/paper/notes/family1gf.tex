\begin{abstract}
Family 1.gf (``gf'' for \emph{global, fine}) is a proposed \emph{family} --- a numbered set of datasets built to one storage design and read by the programme's forecasting model --- of observation \emph{stores} (a store is one dataset, published as a small set of arrays) covering the whole
globe at a granularity of \textbf{ten kilometres or finer in space and five
days or finer in time}, built on the storage contract of family 10, the programme's granularity-aware dataset set (one
registry --- the JSON file that lists every store; three tiers --- gridded fields (G), point measurements (P) and image tiles (T); one token --- the unit the model reads, carrying a value, its offset in space and time from the place being predicted, and its footprint, the area and time span the value averages over). It
extends family 10's five point stores --- 10.1's (family 10's first build) drifters (free-floating surface buoys), tropical moorings,
ship CO$_2$ and 2.03 billion along-track sea-level samples (satellite altimeter heights along the ground track), and 10.2's (its second build)
fishing-effort store (E-081, the experiment that built it: the fishing fleet as a distributed sensor) --- with the fine-scale
measurements the programme's four prediction goals (El Ni\~no, the ocean
carbon sink, the AMOC --- the Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation, the Atlantic's northward heat-carrying flow --- and sea-surface temperature, SST) can each be argued to
need: daily 4\,km ocean colour --- merged across the older sensors, and
hyperspectral from PACE (NASA's 2024 ocean-colour satellite, whose
instrument resolves the whole visible spectrum and with it which kinds of
phytoplankton are there) --- daily 5\,km and 2\,km sea-surface temperature
from the instruments rather than the analyses fitted to them, 6\,km sea-ice
concentration, 2\,km swath sea level from SWOT (the Surface Water and Ocean Topography radar satellite, which maps sea level across two 50\,km-wide strips), 4\,km cloud-top temperature
every 30 minutes, ship reports back to 1662, every non-Argo ocean profile since
1772, interior carbon bottles, biogeochemical floats, open-ocean moorings, and
satellite column CO$_2$ (the CO$_2$ averaged through the whole depth of the atmosphere). Every candidate was checked against its producer on
2026-09-16: resolution, cadence, record, access, volume and licence. Three
things the research settled change the design: no ocean-wind, sea-surface
salinity or microwave-SST (sea-surface temperature from microwave radiometers, which see through cloud) product exists at ten kilometres, so wind is an
\emph{exception} to decide and the other two are out; the daily 4\,km and
5\,km fields are 10--30\,\% valid and 0.2--1.5\,TB each even compressed, so
the gridded tier gains a sharded, tiled, compressed layout for grids finer
than $0.25^\circ$; and several sources have ended or changed recently (Pathfinder SST, NOAA's 4\,km infrared record,
stopped at 2023, IMERG --- NASA's merged satellite precipitation --- in its V07 Final run at 2025-09, and the sea-ice line of NSIDC (the US National Snow and Ice Data Center) from AMSR2 (a Japanese microwave radiometer) at 2025-08),
so the family is built from the successors. The note gives the ledger, the
storage design, the per-store row and byte arithmetic, a three-phase build
ladder (phase A $\approx 0.5$\,TB, eight stores, keyless or hosted-runner
credentials only --- hosted runners are GitHub's own build machines), the falsifier (the test that can show a store is not worth keeping) that decides whether each store earned its
place, and the decisions left to Chris.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope: which measurements qualify (finer than 10\,km and 5 days), and what that rule leaves out}

\paragraph{The rule.} A source qualifies for family 1.gf if it is a
\emph{global} (or global-ocean) \textbf{observation} whose spatial support is
$\le 10$\,km --- a point measurement counts --- and whose temporal support is
$\le 5$ days (a pentad or finer). A derived product is admitted only where it
carries raw observations that are otherwise inaccessible (a merge of sensors
whose individual level-2 files (per-orbit retrievals on the instrument's own swath) are impractical, a bias-corrected collation
whose inputs are the observations). Monthly or coarser products enter only
where something the goals need would otherwise be missing, and each such
exception is named as one in \S6.

\paragraph{Why this rule, in the programme's own terms.} Family 7.2's (the published global input tensor: every $0.25^\circ$ cell, pole to pole, 1982--2024) grids
are $0.25^\circ$ and $1^\circ$, five-daily or monthly; family 10.1 added the point
observations but no fine \emph{fields}. E-078 (the design study that gave family 10 one schema for every granularity) measured why the grid is the wrong
contract for fine data --- a coarse value copied into fine cells is one
measurement presented as many, a fine value averaged into a coarse cell throws
away what the finer source was bought for --- and E-077's (the experiment that added ocean colour to family 7.1) ocean colour is the
deliberate exception, box-averaged to $0.25^\circ$ to get a channel onto the
grid quickly. Family 1.gf is the fine half of that ledger, stored at its own
resolution so the token's footprint fields can say what each value averaged.

\paragraph{What is already held and therefore not repeated.} OISST (NOAA's daily Optimum Interpolation SST analysis) and its
sea-ice fraction at $0.25^\circ$ (family 7.2, \code{g025}, its $0.25^\circ$ ocean-surface group); NCEP (the US National Centers for Environmental Prediction reanalysis) at $1^\circ$
(\code{g100}, family 7.2's $1^\circ$ atmosphere-and-land group); GLORYS-derived (Copernicus' ocean reanalysis) interior fields and the monthly climatology of Argo, the global array of profiling floats
(\code{rg100}, the $1^\circ$ monthly ocean-interior group); OC-CCI (ESA's merged ocean-colour record) box-averaged to $0.25^\circ$ (\code{oc025}, the $0.25^\circ$ ocean-colour group); Argo core
profiles (family 8, the Argo temperature and salinity store); drifters, tropical moorings, SOCAT (the Surface Ocean CO$_2$ Atlas of ship measurements) and all-mission
along-track sea level (family 10.1); and the Global Fishing Watch daily
0.1$^\circ$ per-vessel fishing effort (family 10.2, E-081 --- 11\,km, so it
sits just outside this family's rule and stays where it is). Two of those are the coarse form of a
store below --- \code{oc025} of \code{oc4k} (this family's daily 4\,km ocean-colour store), \code{g025}'s sea ice of
\code{seaice\_asi} (this family's daily 6\,km sea-ice store) --- and both stay: the fine store is a new group, not a
replacement, so a consumer that reads only tier G is unchanged.

\paragraph{Excluded by the rule, after checking the finest product that exists.}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep1pt
\item \textbf{Sea-surface salinity.} Every product is $\ge 25$\,km on the grid
and $\ge 40$\,km in effective resolution (RSS SMAP v6 --- Remote Sensing Systems' salinity from NASA's Soil Moisture Active Passive satellite --- ``40\,km'', ESA CCI SSS v5.5 --- the salinity record of ESA's Climate Change Initiative ---
at 25\,km grid / 50\,km stated, SMOS --- ESA's Soil Moisture and Ocean Salinity satellite --- 25\,km grid / 40--50\,km footprint). Out.
\item \textbf{Passive-microwave SST.} AMSR2 is a $0.25^\circ$ grid from a
$\approx 50$\,km footprint. Out; the infrared L3 products (level 3: measurements binned onto a fixed grid, not gap-filled) below are the
fine-scale SST.
\item \textbf{Ocean wind from scatterometers} (radars that infer wind from the roughness of the sea surface). The finest gridded product is
$0.125^\circ$ ($\approx 13.9$\,km, Copernicus --- the EU's Earth-observation programme ---\allowbreak \code{WIND\_GLO\_PHY\_L3\_MY\_012\_005});
the finest swath product is 12.5\,km (EUMETSAT OSI SAF ASCAT L2: the Ocean and Sea Ice facility of Europe's weather-satellite agency, processing ASCAT, the scatterometer on the MetOp satellites; CC BY 4.0, free with attribution).
Neither passes. Wind is the one forcing the ocean stores cannot do without,
so it is carried as \textbf{exception E1} in \S6 rather than dropped silently.
\item \textbf{Gap-filled analyses}: OSTIA (Met Office) and MUR (NASA JPL) SST, DUACS (the gridded altimetry of CNES and Copernicus) sea level,
GLORYS, SMAP L4 (level 4: model-assimilated) soil moisture, the IMERG family's model-like blending. They
are derived; their inputs are what the stores below hold. IMERG is the one
carried as an exception (E2), because the raw swaths of GPM (the Global Precipitation Measurement mission) are 40\,TB and cover
the globe only every few days.
\end{itemize}

\section{Storage: what the family-10 design already gives, and the one layout it lacks --- compressed tiles for grids finer than $0.25^\circ$}

\code{family10.json} (\code{epoch} 1982-01-01, \code{pentad\_days} 5 --- a pentad is the five-day bin every time is filed under ---, the

\code{log2\_dt} (the base-2 logarithms of a value's spatial and temporal support, relative to 27.83\,km and 5 days), per-group \code{tier}, \code{cadence}, \code{C},
\code{channels}, \code{footprint}, \code{layout}, \code{prefix}, files with
\code{sha256} (a checksum), per-year counts, QC policy, \code{degraded} (the list of any check a build had to relax); \code{C} is the number of channels). The tier letters stay G, P and T so that a consumer's dispatch is
unchanged; a fine grid is tier G with \code{layout: "sharded"} (\S4's
layout) rather than \code{"binmajor"} (one array per group ordered by five-day bin), and the reader named for tier G
handles both by reading that field. Six per-group fields are new and all
are load-bearing: \code{licence} with a \code{redistribution} value of
\code{yes}, \code{attribution} or \code{no} and a \code{derived\_works} value
of \code{free}, \code{non-commercial} or \code{restricted}, from the
licence's own clause; \code{distribution}, \code{public} or \code{private},
the track of \S4's two-track rule, \code{private} whenever redistribution is
\code{no}; \code{frames\_per\_bin} and
\code{frame\_seconds} for gridded groups; \code{dtype} per group (float16, a two-byte number,
for measured fields, uint8 for classes and for brightness temperature stored
as K$-160$); and \code{verdict}, empty until \S7's falsifier has run. A
sharded group also names its \code{tile\_grid.json} and
\code{shard\_index.npy}. Family 10.2's registry --- the
current one, which inherits 10.1's four stores by reference and adds
\code{fishing} (E-081) --- gains a one-line \code{siblings} list naming this
file, family 1.0.tf's (land and coast at the same scale) and family 0.9.tf's (land and coast through Google's AlphaEarth embedding), so a consumer that already dispatches on \code{tier}
discovers both families from the one entry point it knows. Nothing in family
10.1 or 10.2 is rewritten.

\paragraph{Tier P (points, profiles and tracks), with one widening.} Every point store below ships the nine arrays of
family 10.1 at schema 2 --- \code{bin} int16, \code{time\_s} int32 seconds,
\code{lat}, \code{lon} float32, \code{values} float16 $[N, C]$ in raw units,
\code{platform} int64 (the hashed instrument identity), \code{qc} uint8 (quality code), \code{fp} float16 $[N, 2]$ (the two footprint fields),
\code{bin\_offsets} int64 (where each bin's rows start) --- sorted by \code{(bin, time\_s)}, negative bins
before 1982, longitude in $[-180, 180)$ enforced in float32, one row costing
$27 + 2C$ bytes. Profiles put the vertical axis into $C$ exactly as family 8
does for Argo: the 16 Roemmich--Gilson pressures per variable (the standard
levels of the Roemmich--Gilson Argo climatology, 2.5 to 2,000 dbar), NaN
where the cast did not reach. The one widening: an int32 second since 1982
reaches back only to 1913-12-13, and \code{icoads} begins in 1662 and
\code{wod} in 1772. A store whose record starts before 1914 declares
\textbf{schema 3}, identical except that \code{time\_s} is int64; a row then
costs $31 + 2C$ bytes, \code{bin} (int16, $\pm 449$ years) is unchanged, and
\code{ml/family10\_store.py} (the store reader) gains one dtype branch. Stores inside the int32
range stay at schema 2.

\paragraph{Tier G-fine (gridded fields finer than $0.25^\circ$), the new layout.} Family 7.2's tier G is one bin-major
\code{.npy} (NumPy array file) per group, read by one HTTP range (a request for one byte span of a file) of one bin: 14.5\,MB at
$0.25^\circ$ for seven channels. At $0.05^\circ$ one daily frame of one
channel is 51.8\,MB and a day of \code{sst\_acspo02} is 324\,MB per channel;
a cone (the set of past measurements the model reads around the place and time it predicts, the \emph{anchor}) anchored at one point needs a few hundred kilometres of it, and the
daily fields are 10--30\,\% valid, the rest cloud, night or land. A flat
uncompressed array would cost 5--10 times the bytes it carries and force every
anchor to read a whole globe. So groups on a grid finer than $0.25^\circ$ use
a \textbf{sharded, tiled, compressed} layout:
\begin{itemize}\itemsep1pt\sloppy
\item One \textbf{shard} per (group, bin): \code{<prefix>/\allowbreak <yyyy>/\allowbreak <bin>.shard}.
Inside it, $F$ \textbf{frames} per bin (\code{frames\_per\_bin} = 1 for a
pentad field, 5 for daily, 10 for day/night, 40 for three-hourly, 240 for
half-hourly), each frame cut into \textbf{tiles} of $256 \times 256$ pixels
of the group's native grid ($12.8^\circ$ at $0.05^\circ$; $10.7^\circ$ at
$1/24^\circ$; 1,600\,km on the 6.25\,km polar grid), each tile all $C$
channels as float16 in raw units, NaN (not-a-number) for unobserved, zstd-compressed (Zstandard, a fast lossless compressor).
\item One \textbf{index} per group: \code{shard\_index.npy}, int64
$[n_{\mathrm{bins}}, F, n_{\mathrm{tiles}}, 2]$ of (byte offset, byte length)
inside the bin's shard, plus \code{tile\_grid.json} (rows, columns, the
lat/lon of every tile's corner). An empty tile (all NaN --- open ocean for a
land product, the tropics for sea ice) has length 0 and is never stored. Tiles are float16 unless the group's \code{dtype} says otherwise.
\item A \textbf{read} is one HTTP range per (bin, frame, tile) the cone
touches: 20--130\,KB compressed for a tile with 20\,\% valid pixels, measured
by the probe (\S7) before anything is built. The bin's whole frame is
\code{n\_tiles} ranges; a cone of 1,400\,km reach touches four to nine tiles at $0.05^\circ$ and up to about 36 at $0.02^\circ$.
\item The limits of the Hub (Hugging Face's dataset hosting) shape this: under 100\,k files per repository and
10\,k per folder, so shards are per bin (about 3,450 for a group reaching back to 1979) in year
folders, never per tile; files under 200\,GB, which no shard approaches.
\end{itemize}
The frame carries its own timestamp (\code{frame\_seconds} in the registry:
86,400 for daily, 10,800 for three-hourly; for day/night passes the local
overpass time is a channel), and the token's $\Delta t$ is measured from the
end of the anchor's bin to the frame's time, as tier P does. A gridded value is
one token at the pixel centre with \code{log2\_fp} = $\log_2(\text{pixel km} /
27.83)$ and \code{log2\_dt} = $\log_2(\text{frame days} / 5)$; nothing is
copied into neighbouring pixels. On the floor: family 10.1 writes
$\log_2 f_p = -4$ for a point instrument, and this note keeps that as the
\emph{label} for ``no areal support''; a pixel with a real support carries
its true value even below $-4$ (a 1\,km pixel is $-4.8$, a 300\,m pixel
$-6.5$), and likewise a real temporal support (an hour, $-6.9$) is written
as it is while an instantaneous sample keeps the $-4$ label. E-078 \S2
quoted OC-CCI's 4\,km as $-2.8$; the grid is $1/24^\circ = 4.64$\,km, so
$-2.58$ is the value used here.

\paragraph{Which tier a fine field belongs in is geometry first, bytes
second.} A row of tier P costs $27 + 2C$ bytes; a stored pixel of a
compressed G-fine tile costs about $2C \times 1.2$ bytes and an unobserved
pixel almost nothing, so on bytes alone a compressed grid beats rows at any
valid fraction (an \emph{uncompressed} grid would lose below
$v = 2C/(27 + 2C)$, 7\,\% at $C = 1$). What decides is whether the source
\emph{has} a fixed grid: SWOT's 2\,km cells lie along two swaths whose
position changes every pass, so there is no raster to tile and \code{swot} is
a P store; a 4\,km chlorophyll field at 10--15\,\% valid is on one grid every
day and is G-fine, and a cone over the tropics then reads a few tiles instead
of millions of rows. The probe measures $v$ per source; the registry records
the tier and the layout.

\paragraph{Tier T (image tiles) is not used by this family.} Nothing global at
$\le 10$\,km is imagery in the tile sense --- the 300\,m ocean-colour and
Sentinel (the EU Copernicus satellites) swaths are coastal or terrestrial and belong to family 1.0.tf (and, as a published embedding in place of the raw imagery, family 0.9.tf). This
departs from E-078 \S3, which placed 300\,m--1\,km radiometry in tier T: a
\emph{gridded level-3} product at those scales is a raster on one fixed grid
and fits the sharded tier-G layout, whereas tier T is for swath imagery whose
footprint moves every pass. The one
candidate that approaches tier-T volume, \code{irtb} at 30 minutes, is handled
by scope (\S5) rather than by a token cache.

\paragraph{Two-machine builds, unchanged.} Credentials --- Earthdata login,
Copernicus Marine (the free accounts of NASA and of the EU marine service) --- live in repository secrets and reach only hosted runners;
rented boxes (machines hired by the hour) are keyless and assemble from Hub parts, exactly the
\code{slatrack} pattern, the way family 10.1 built its 67\,GB sea-level store (six hosted lanes fetch and push per-year parts with
\code{done.json}, a completion marker, written last after a restore-hash; a keyless verified box
pulls, assembles by streaming, publishes with restore-verification). Every
fetch lane refuses a listing that came back empty or a download that came back
short and records any relaxation in \code{degraded}; there is no
``download failed, skip'' path, per Chris's 2026-09-14 rule.

\paragraph{Two tracks: public and private stores.} Not every source that
passes the rule may be republished, and the families no longer refuse
those. Chris, 2026-09-16: a dataset that forbids redistribution is used by a
single party; the Hub was only ever the easiest way to import a dataset and
hand it to other agents; a private dataset can be built and trained on
without redistributing it. So a group's \code{distribution} is \code{public}
--- the public repository \code{chfrank/\allowbreak earth-tensors}, read
anonymously by anyone, the default wherever the licence allows --- or
\code{private}: the same arrays, layout and \code{store.json} in a private
Hugging Face dataset repository (\code{chfrank/\allowbreak earth-tensors-private},
the pick, because every reader and build script is unchanged; an
S3-compatible bucket with pre-signed URLs (time-limited download links) is the alternative if the private
volume passes a terabyte or the Hub's private-storage pricing bites) that
only this project's own jobs can read. The public registry lists private
groups too, with their \code{prefix}, sizes, checks and \code{verdict}, so
the design is public even where the bytes are not, and a consumer without a
token skips them by the field rather than by a failed fetch. Four
consequences. A rented box reads a private store with a fine-grained
read-only token scoped to that one repository, handed to the job through a
repository secret and revoked when the job ends --- the one credential a box
holds beyond the job's own, admissible because it outlives nothing
(\code{ml/CLAUDE.md} \S6, the project's security rules for rented machines; pre-signed URLs would be the keyless form). A
private store is processed on a box and never kept on one: pulled for the
job, deleted by the job's hygiene, never on a stopped box's disk, since the
host has root. What may leave is the model and the numbers: a trained
checkpoint, evaluation scores and aggregate statistics may be published where
the licence's derived-works clause allows (\code{derived\_works} records it;
a \code{restricted} source confines even the checkpoint to the private
track), the data never. And a private store is a cost to reproducibility:
nobody outside the project can check it, so a published result that rests on
one names it, and a public source is preferred wherever one carries the same
observations --- which is why every CC0, CC BY (Creative Commons, attribution required) and Copernicus source in these
families is on the public track.

\paragraph{Three arithmetic notes.} \code{swot}'s 16 billion rows are eight
times \code{slatrack}'s, because a swath is a grid and a nadir track is a
line; at 33 bytes a row the store would be about 0.5\,TB. The alternatives ---
keep the 250\,m unsmoothed product (25\,TB native) or subsample the 2\,km
grid --- are respectively impossible and against the rule, so \code{swot} is
built as a P store in phase B, once the Hub conversation about terabyte
repositories has happened. \code{irtb} is the largest raw source in the ledger
and its scientific value for El Ni\~no is in the tropics, so phase B takes
30$^\circ$S--30$^\circ$N three-hourly (0.7\,TB) and the full half-hourly
60$^\circ$S--60$^\circ$N record is a phase-C decision; the frame count, not
the pixel count, is what the design has to hold. \code{sst\_cci05} is built
by the family's own builder from the per-sensor L3C files, choosing the
highest quality level per pixel per pass --- a super-collation, which is what
NOAA's ACSPO L3S does at 0.02$^\circ$ from 2000; the CCI route is taken first
because it reaches 1979 and is CC BY 4.0, and \code{sst\_acspo02} follows for
the 2\,km detail.

\paragraph{Why \code{pace4k} is the 4\,km daily bins and not the 1.2\,km
swath.} PACE's level-2 swath at 1.2\,km is about a terabyte a year of cells
--- $2.5 \times 10^8$ ocean cells a day, a quarter of them clear --- for a
product whose value is the \emph{spectrum} and the \emph{community} rather
than the last kilometre of detail, and the 4\,km daily bins keep both. The
swath is therefore a phase-C row, \code{pace1k}, to be built only if a
verdict says the finer grain matters; the 4\,km store is phase A. This is
the one daily \emph{binned} product admitted beside \code{oc4k}, and it is
admitted because OC-CCI v6 does not merge PACE, so nothing else in the
family carries the spectrum. Two honest notes on what it is. Its three
community channels come from MOANA, which NASA labels \emph{level 4} rather
than level 3 (measured in the catalogue on 2026-09-20), so those three are a
retrieval of a retrieval and are stored with the rest but flagged in
\code{qc}; and its apparent visible wavelength comes from the optical
product while chlorophyll and carbon come from the biogeochemical one, three
files a day joined on one grid by the builder. Marine species records are
not here: they ride \code{gbif}, family 1.0.tf's store of dated, located
occurrence records, which is global and holds the sea as well as the land.

\section{The build ladder, the probe, and the falsifier --- the order of building, the one-month test fetch, and the test each store must pass to be kept}

\paragraph{Phase A --- nine stores, $\approx 0.6$\,TB, keyless or
hosted-runner credentials only.} \code{seaice\_asi}, \code{oc4k},
\code{pace4k}, \code{icoads}, \code{wod}, \code{glodap}, \code{bgcargo},
\code{oceansites},
\code{xco2}, plus the registry and the \code{siblings} line in family 10.2's.
Seven of the nine fetch without any account; \code{xco2} and \code{pace4k}
need an Earthdata login on a hosted runner (GES DISC for the first, OB.DAAC
--- already verified --- for the second); \code{oc4k} is the one G-fine build large enough to
need a box for assembly. Expected cost, from the 10.1 precedent (a \emph{verified} box is a rented machine whose host the marketplace has vetted): the P stores
\$0 on hosted runners; \code{oc4k} one verified box for a few hours, \$2--5;
the whole phase under \$10 if nothing is re-fetched.

\paragraph{Phase B --- the terabyte stores.} \code{swot}, \code{sst\_cci05},
\code{irtb} (tropics, three-hourly), \code{precip01}, \code{swh},
\code{deep\_arrays}, and the six Rrs channels of \code{oc4k}. $\approx 3.5$\,TB;
\$20--40 in box time at 10.1's measured rates, dominated by upload; the Hub
should be asked about a terabyte-scale public repository before this phase
(the docs say ``contact us for large repos'').

\paragraph{Phase C --- decisions, not builds, until A and B have verdicts.}
\code{sst\_acspo02}, \code{wind12}, the full \code{irtb}, and \code{pace1k},
PACE's 1.2\,km level-2 swath ($\approx 1$\,TB a year) if the 4\,km bins of
\code{pace4k} turn out to be too coarse for the carbon read-out.

\paragraph{The probe, before any build.} For each source, one month
(January 2015, as E-079 --- family 10's first data pull --- used) fetched on a hosted runner through the real
adapter, measuring: rows per day or valid fraction per frame; compressed
bytes per tile at the chosen tile size; listing completeness against the
producer's own count; fetch throughput; and for G-fine, the byte length of a
cone-sized read. Family 10 learned the cost of building on the spec's numbers
--- a time column 84 times coarser than the sampling, 908 rows in the wrong
pentad --- so \textbf{no store is dispatched on a size that has not been
measured}, and the probe's numbers replace this note's in the registry.

\paragraph{The falsifier per store.} A store earns its place if, at anchors
where it has coverage, adding its tokens to the cone lowers the held-out
masked-token loss (the model's error at predicting tokens hidden from it) on the goal's read-out (\S2's mapping: the Ni\~no-3.4 --- the standard El Ni\~no index box in the central Pacific ---
target for El Ni\~no, the \code{socat}-scored $f$CO$_2$ and \code{oc025}
colour targets for carbon, the transport measured by RAPID (the 26.5$^\circ$N overturning array) and the corridor targets for AMOC,
the OISST target for SST) against the same anchors, same seed, without it ---
three seeds (three independent training runs), because a new tier buys its own replication (\code{ml/CLAUDE.md}
\S3b, the programme's replication rule). Parity means the coarse tensor already carried what the fine store
adds at pentad cadence, and the store is then justified only as a fine
\emph{target} (E-078 \S5), which is a different and cheaper claim. The
verdicts are written per store into the registry's \code{verdict} field so a
consumer can see which fine stores the programme found to matter.

\paragraph{Verification, as for 10.1.} Every array's sha256 against
\code{store.json}; $N$ from file sizes; \code{bin} monotone and equal to
\code{floor\_divide(time\_s, 432000)} (432,000 seconds are five days); the CSR (compressed sparse row) index reproduced; and, new for
G-fine, every shard's index entries decompressing to tiles of the declared
shape with the declared NaN fraction, and per-year frame counts equal to the
producer's listing (a day absent upstream is a zero-length frame with a
counter, never a silent gap).
