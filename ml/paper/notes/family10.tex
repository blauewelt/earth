\title{Family 10: one schema for every granularity\\
\large a storage and sampling design in which no source is resampled to a common grid}
\author{blauewelt/earth --- design note}
\date{13 September 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
The programme's input tensors so far share one contract: every source is
brought onto a $0.25^\circ$, five-day grid before the model sees it. That
contract copies coarse values into fine cells as if they were independent
measurements, averages fine sources down to the grid and discards what they
were bought for, and represents a sparse observing system as a grid that is
mostly empty. Family 10 replaces it with three commitments. Every source is
stored at its own resolution and cadence in one of three tiers by density:
gridded dense fields at their native grid, points and profiles in a
pentad-sorted observation store, and fine imagery as a catalogue plus the
cached tokens of a small per-tile codec. A single token schema --- the value,
its offset from the anchor in space and time, and its \emph{footprint} (the
spatial and temporal support it averaged, in log units) --- is produced by
each tier's reader, so the encoder never learns which file a value came from,
only what it is, where it is, and how much it averaged. And the decoder is
queried at a location and a footprint, so targets can be at any granularity
too. Families 7.1 and 8 become the first tier-G and tier-P groups of family
10 unchanged. We state the schema, the tiers, the registry that binds them,
the local codec, the storage arithmetic, and four staged experiments each
with a falsifier.
\end{abstract}

\section{Why a common grid is the wrong contract}

Three properties of the existing tensors motivate the design; each is a
measurement rather than a preference.

\paragraph{A coarse value copied into fine cells is one measurement
presented as many.} Family 7 serves a reanalysis value on a $1.9^\circ$
Gaussian cell, stored at $1^\circ$, to the sixteen $0.25^\circ$ cells inside
it and to the nine cells of a lag-0 patch, as sixteen or nine tokens that
happen to agree. The model cannot distinguish nine readings of $0.31$ from
one reading copied nine times, and those are different amounts of evidence.

\paragraph{A fine value averaged into a coarse cell discards what the finer
source adds.} Family 7.1 keeps one thirty-sixth of the pixels of a 4\,km
colour product and adds a coverage channel to say how much was discarded.
Kilometre-scale radiometry is of order $3.5 \times 10^{14}$ values; it
cannot be a tensor, and ``therefore average it'' is the wrong conclusion.

\paragraph{Distance is a feature that a grid hides.} With the same five
nearest Argo profiles, optimal interpolation beats climatology by 21\,\% on
temperature where the gridded monthly product, read at the nearest cell,
gave a learned codec 3\,\% (E-076b). The information was in where and when
the profiles were taken relative to the point being predicted; the grid had
removed it.

The contract cannot therefore be ``everything on one grid''. It has to be
``everything at its own granularity, and the token says what that
granularity is''.

\section{The token}

Every observation, from every tier, becomes one token:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{token} = \big(\,\mathbf{v}[C],\ \mathbf{m}[C],\ \Delta x,\ \Delta y,\ \Delta t,\ \log_2 f_p,\ \log_2 f_t,\ n_R,\ s,\ c\,\big)
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{v}$ is the measurement, standardised per channel, with mask
$\mathbf{m}$ marking which of its $C$ components were measured;
$(\Delta x, \Delta y)$ the offset from the anchor in kilometres east and
north and $\Delta t$ the age in days, measured back from the end of the
anchor's bin; $n_R$ the number of such observations within the search
bounds; $s$ and $c$ learned embeddings of the source class and the channel.
The two footprint fields are
\begin{equation}
\log_2 f_p = \log_2\!\frac{\text{footprint in km}}{27.83\,\text{km}}, \qquad
\log_2 f_t = \log_2\!\frac{\text{support in days}}{5\,\text{days}},
\end{equation}
the spatial and temporal support in units of a $0.25^\circ$ cell and a
pentad, clamped to $\pm 12$. Table~\ref{tab:fp} places the programme's
sources on that scale. For a dense group both fields are constants and cost
nothing; for a sparse store they are per row.

The offsets are the correction to the first property above: a $1^\circ$
value is one token located at the cell's centre, seen from sixteen different
offsets by the sixteen anchors inside it, and a fine anchor's lag-0 patch
holds one such token rather than nine. The footprint fields are what the
offsets cannot carry: a value 30\,km away from a $1.9^\circ$ average and one
30\,km away from a point profile say different things about the anchor,
because the coarse value is a smoother, lower-variance quantity. This is
the choice AlphaEarth makes on the encoder side, conditioning each source's
decoder on the sensor's own metadata rather than resampling to a common grid
first \cite{alphaearth}.

Three properties make the tiers one family. \emph{One time axis:} every
tier is indexed by the pentad bin from the 1982-01-01 epoch; tier-P rows keep
their timestamp inside the bin so $\Delta t$ is fractional; a tier-G group
carries its own first bin or, for a monthly product, its own list of live
bins. \emph{One registry:} a \code{family.json} at the family's root lists
every group with its tier, its grid or store schema, its cadence, its channel
names and units, its footprint constants, its first bin, its file names with
hashes, its sources and the builder commit; consumers dispatch on the tier,
so a new group is a new entry rather than a new code path. \emph{One sampler
contract:} $\mathrm{gather}(\text{anchor}, \text{family}) \to \text{tokens}$
--- for each group, its reader returns tokens in the schema of \S2 under that
group's cone family (the reach-versus-lag rule of E-071: atmosphere,
currents and sea-surface height, temperature and mixed layer, land, the
depth column) with a fixed slot count per group, so batches stay
rectangular. The existing cone sampler is this contract for tier G, and the
Argo store's nearest-neighbour search is it for tier P; tier T reuses tier
P's.

\section{The local codec}

A $64 \times 64$-pixel tile at 10\,m is 4,096 pixels of 13 bands; a
$0.25^\circ$ cell holds about 7,700 such tiles. They cannot be dots. The
local codec is the programme's per-pixel masked-autoencoder codec
generalised to a tile: input one tile-date --- all bands, with the sensor's
own metadata (resolution, incidence angle for radar, sun angle for optical)
as conditioning --- and output $k = 4$--$8$ tokens of 64 dimensions, trained
by masked-pixel reconstruction plus a same-place-different-date term so that
the token carries state rather than texture. Tokens are cached once per
tile-date and reused by every anchor whose cone contains the tile. The codec
has snapshot semantics; the cone codec supplies the time axis by reading the
cached tokens of many dates. The hierarchy is: raw observations
$\rightarrow$ local codec (per tile-date, cached) $\rightarrow$ cone codec
(per anchor, over local tokens, coarse dots and point observations)
$\rightarrow$ task heads. AlphaEarth's space--time pyramid does this within
one image \cite{alphaearth}; here the pyramid runs across sources and time
scales, and the set encoder is Perceiver-style cross-attention from a fixed
number of latents to the token set \cite{perceiver}, so cost is linear in
the number of tokens and a ragged input --- a profile here, a swath there, a
tile in the corner --- needs no padding.

\paragraph{Storage arithmetic.} Over land and coasts ($\approx 1.5 \times
10^{8}$\,km$^2$) there are $\approx 3.7 \times 10^{8}$ tiles of 640\,m; at
$\approx 70$ clear dates per year, 6 tokens of 64 float16 dimensions, that
is $\approx 20$\,TB of tokens per year --- streamable as a tier-P store but
not small. Tiles of 256 pixels (2.56\,km) cut it sixteenfold to
$\approx 1.3$\,TB per year and remain a hundred times finer in area than a
$0.25^\circ$ cell; the tile size is a parameter the first experiment
measures. The catalogue is metadata only, of order 10\,GB per year. Raw
imagery is streamed once to train the codec and once to encode, and is never
kept.

\paragraph{Radar.} NISAR's L-band backscatter at 15\,m on a 12-day repeat,
through cloud and at night, is the right snapshot input for soil moisture,
flooding, ice and vegetation structure, and complements Sentinel-1's C band;
its record begins in late 2025, so it enters as a local-codec input and
cannot contribute to a 43-year forecast axis. Sentinel-1 (2014--) and
Sentinel-2 (2015--) carry the record. One codec conditioned on sensor
metadata serves both radars with different source embeddings, which is the
reason the codec is conditioned rather than trained per sensor.

\section{Outputs at any granularity}

The target side has the same problem as the input side. The recipe already
has a decoder queried at a location (E-072); it gains the two footprint
fields of the token. ``The mean chlorophyll of this $0.25^\circ$ cell over
this pentad'' and ``this 300\,m pixel on this day'' are then two queries of
one embedding, each scored against the observation that actually has that
support. A masked-token objective over the schema of \S2 covers every tier
at once: withhold a token --- a grid cell, a profile, a cached tile token ---
and predict its value from the rest, at its own footprint. Family 8's two
objectives, masked-profile reconstruction and next-pentad prediction, are
the tier-P instances of it. Every read-out remains a head on the frozen
representation plus the rolled forecast, and is scored against the null
ladder --- climatology, damped persistence, a linear inverse model in pixel
and in embedding space --- the programme's protocol requires.

\section{What is built first}

Four steps, each with its falsifier stated before it is run.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The footprint fields on the tensor that exists.} Read family
7's coarse groups as offset tokens --- one token at the coarse cell's
centre, its $(\Delta x, \Delta y)$ to the anchor, $\log_2 f_p = +2.9$ for the
reanalysis and $+2$ for the monthly Argo field --- instead of nearest-cell
copies, and add the footprint fields to every sparse token. One cone-codec
arm against its twin on the same anchors and seed. Falsifier: no change in
held-out per-family loss, in which case the model does not use the fields at
this scale and the tile tier must be argued on its own evidence. Cost: a
sampler change and about one dollar of compute.
\item \textbf{The registry and the tier dispatch.} Write \code{family.json}
for families 7.1 and 8 as one family, make the samplers and the front end
dispatch on the tier, retire the per-family metadata conventions. No model.
Falsifier: a test that gathers one anchor through the registry and
reproduces the existing sampler's token bytes exactly.
\item \textbf{The first local codec, coastal.} Sentinel-2 level-2A 10\,m
tiles over about three hundred coastal $0.25^\circ$ cells where colour,
sea-surface temperature and Argo all have coverage; train the tile codec,
cache its tokens as a tier-P store, add them to the cone; three seeds, since
a new tier buys its own replication. Verdict: the masked-token loss on the
colour and temperature targets at those anchors with and without the tile
tokens. Falsifier: parity, in which case at pentad cadence the coarse fields
already carry what 10\,m adds and the fine tier is for fine targets, not for
the forecast. This is the one compute-bound step.
\item \textbf{NISAR and Sentinel-1 through the same codec} with a radar
source embedding, over sea-ice-edge and flood-prone cells where the
day-and-night, through-cloud property matters. After step 3.
\end{enumerate}

\section{What does not change}

The pentad bin as the unit of time; the holdout protocol (2009, 2017 and
2023 as development years; train through 2020, test 2021--2024); the cone
families and reaches; family 7.1's files, which become tier-G groups of
family 10 unchanged; family 8's store, which becomes its first tier-P group
unchanged. The design adds a registry and a tier and removes nothing.
