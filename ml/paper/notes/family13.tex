\begin{abstract}
Family 1.3 is a proposed \emph{family} --- a numbered set of datasets built to one storage design and read by the
programme's forecasting model --- made of family 1.2 \textbf{inherited by reference} (listed in the new family's
registry, the JSON file that names every store, with no byte copied) plus a small number of new \emph{stores} (a
store is one dataset, published as a small set of arrays) whose job is to say \textbf{where floating plastic is in
the ocean, and how much}. Plastic is not one of the programme's four prediction goals (El Ni\~no, the ocean carbon
sink, the Atlantic overturning circulation and sea-surface temperature), so the family adds a \textbf{fifth
read-out} --- floating plastic concentration at a place and a time --- and the stores that measure it, and is kept
separate so that family 1.2's registry and its verdicts stay untouched. Every candidate source was measured by
request on 2026-10-11 rather than transcribed from its documentation. The finding that shapes everything:
\textbf{plastic in the open ocean is measured almost only by nets, not by satellites.} A net towed at the surface
catches pieces down to a third of a millimetre; a satellite pixel sees a 10\,m or 1\,km square of sea, and the
densest open-ocean microplastic covers about 0.005\,\% of the surface (Hu 2021). So the first store,
\code{mplastic}, is the nets --- built from one producer per cruise, because the published compilations are mostly
the same tows compiled again and again; storing pieces per km$^2$ \emph{as caught}, because the per-volume figures
rest on a net depth each compiler assumed; and storing an empty net as the observation it is. The satellite stores
are tested against the nets and against human-labelled pixels before anything large is built: Sentinel-2 does not
image the open ocean, except in one 2021 campaign over the Great Pacific Garbage Patch that coincides with The Ocean
Cleanup's aerial surveys, and a one-hour probe there is expected to find nothing above noise. The note gives the
ledger of sources, the stores, nine decisions, the build ladder, the test that can show each store is not worth
keeping, and the open items. Nothing has been built, dispatched, rented or uploaded.
\end{abstract}

\section{What family 1.3 is, and the fifth read-out}

\paragraph{Family 1.2, the parent.} Family 1.2 is the set of global observation stores at 10\,km and 5 days or
finer --- family 1.gf: ocean colour, satellite sea-surface temperature, ship reports, floats, bottles, moorings,
the swaths of SWOT (the Surface Water and Ocean Topography satellite, which maps sea level across wide strips) and
more --- plus the upper-air temperature, humidity and wind of ERA5, the European weather reanalysis (E-085, the
experiment that added it). It is published on the project's public Hugging Face dataset
\code{chfrank/earth-tensors}.

\paragraph{Family 1.3.} Family 1.2, inherited by reference, plus a small number of new stores whose job is to say
where floating plastic is, and how much. Chris asked on 2026-10-08 for this family to be drafted --- family 1.2
unchanged, plus stores that could identify plastic in the ocean --- and on 2026-10-09 for The Ocean Cleanup's
survey to be shown on blauewelt.org first; it shipped that day as the globe's ``Floating plastic, North Pacific''
layer.

\paragraph{Why a new family and not a new store in 1.2.} Plastic is not one of the programme's four prediction
goals. Family 1.3 adds a \textbf{fifth read-out} (a quantity the model is asked to predict or report): floating
plastic concentration at a place and a time, and the stores that measure it. Keeping it a separate family keeps
1.2's registry and its verdicts --- the recorded result of each store's keep-or-drop test --- untouched.

\paragraph{The finding that shapes everything.} Plastic in the open ocean is measured almost only by nets, not by
satellites. A net towed at the surface catches pieces down to a third of a millimetre; a satellite pixel sees a
10\,m or 1\,km square of sea, and the densest open-ocean microplastic covers about 0.005\,\% of the surface
(Hu 2021), far below what any optical sensor can see. Satellites see plastic only where it is piled up ---
windrows (lines where wind and currents gather floating material), river plumes, harbours --- and even then mixed
with seaweed and foam. So the first store is the nets, and the satellite stores are tested against them before
anything large is built.

\paragraph{Three facts about these sources decide the design.}
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{They are mostly the SAME tows, compiled again and again.} The cruises of SEA (the Sea Education
Association), The Ocean Cleanup's 2015 expedition and Eriksen's 5 Gyres tows sit in NCEI, AOMI and Eriksen 2023
alike, and the SEA Pacific tows are in AOMI twice (2,527 submitted by SEA, 2,130 imported via NOAA). 12,160 of
AOMI's 18,860 rows match NCEI on date and position; 7,758 of Eriksen 2023's 12,546 do; 644 of the 683 Ocean
Cleanup 2015--16 events have an NCEI row on the same day and place. A store that concatenated the compilations
would count the same tow three times.
\item \textbf{``Pieces/m$^3$'' for a surface net tow is not a measurement.} A neuston or manta net (a surface net
that skims the top of the sea) skims the top few tens of centimetres and measures pieces per AREA; NCEI converts to
per VOLUME by dividing by a net depth the compiler assumed, and the assumption differs by study. Matched against The
Ocean Cleanup's own pieces/km$^2$, the implied depths are 0.25\,m for Law 2014, 0.08\,m for Eriksen 2014,
0.155--0.25\,m for Goldstein, and 0.075\,m for The Ocean Cleanup's 2015 tows; Isobe 2021 uses 0.5\,m for the same
SEA tows. Values from different studies are not comparable until that division is undone.
\item \textbf{Zeros are real and common.} 4,958 of NCEI's 16,988 water samples, 5,566 of AOMI's, 1,506 of
Lebreton's compiled tows: the net came up empty. A zero is an observation and is stored as one.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{The SEA conflict --- found while checking, settled the same day.} SEA's 2,529 Pacific tows
(Law et al.\ 2014) exist in two published copies: NCEI accession 0211008 (also carried by NCEI's harmonised table
as 2,530 rows) and the copy inside Lebreton 2018's compilation. They agree on every year-month count and on the
first $\sim 46$ rows, then diverge: only 399 positions match, although both are coherent ship tracks (median step
between consecutive tows 92\,km and 100\,km). Two further copies broke the tie. SEA's own submission to AOMI
(2,485 Pacific tows, 2001-10 $\to$ 2012-12) matches Lebreton's copy at \textbf{all 2,485} year-month-position keys
and NCEI's at 355; Eriksen 2023 (10,170 SEA rows carrying ship, cruise and station numbers) matches Lebreton's at
2,485 of 2,487 and NCEI's at 357. \textbf{NCEI accession 0211008 has the wrong positions; Lebreton's copy --- the
one the globe's ``earlier surveys'' option draws --- is right.} In September 2005, for example, NCEI places the
tows off Hawaii ($15^\circ$\,N, $160^\circ$\,W) where the other three place them off San Diego ($32.8^\circ$\,N,
$117.5^\circ$\,W). Only the Pacific accession is affected: NCEI's 6,135 SEA Atlantic tows (Law et al.\ 2010) match
SEA's own AOMI submission at 5,808 of 5,851 positions.

\textbf{The values are a separate, open question.} Where the positions agree, the published Law 2014 value equals
SEA's own AOMI value (pieces/km$^2$, $<5$\,mm) for 1,440 of 2,487 tows and is within 5\,\% for 20 more; for the
other 1,027 SEA's resubmitted value is larger, typically about three times (median ratio 3.0, 10th--90th
percentile 1.02--5.85). That looks like a later reprocessing by SEA, not a transcription error, and nothing in the
files says which version SEA now stands by. Decision D9 records the choice.

\paragraph{Sentinel-2's coverage.} The one satellite with the bands the debris indices need (red edge, near
infrared, shortwave infrared) at 10--20\,m does not image the open ocean: its plan stops at coastal waters, islands
and closed seas, and the measured reach offshore is about 250\,km and only where a swath clips land. Over the Great
Pacific Garbage Patch it imaged one campaign in one summer, 2021.

\paragraph{Level-2A clips over water.} The anonymously served, already-corrected product cannot be used as it is:
on a clear tile more than 100\,km off Portugal, 11\,\% of B08, 27\,\% of B11 and 8\,\% of B06 pixels sit at the
lowest value --- the very bands the floating debris index differences. Any debris store starts from level-1C and
corrects with ACOLITE, as the debris papers do.

\paragraph{The labelled sets.} MARIDA, MADOS, the Plastic Litter Project and FloatingObjects are human labels or
known targets on Sentinel-2 scenes, all near coasts. They are what any satellite debris method is tested on, and
the first three are CC BY 4.0.

\paragraph{PACE and CYGNSS.} PACE's water-leaving reflectance stops at 719\,nm, its only shortwave-infrared above
level 1 is the surface-reflectance product, and no band sits on plastic's 1,732\,nm absorption. CYGNSS's
microplastic index is a roughness anomaly fitted to a plastic model, at $1^\circ$ and 30 days, and natural
surfactants and the tropical rain band confound it.

\paragraph{The 2021 co-location window --- where a satellite and the nets meet in the open gyre.} The Ocean
Cleanup's North Pacific missions of 2021 (in Lebreton 2024) flew 22 aerial surveys (34.8--35.4$^\circ$\,N,
144.4--145.9$^\circ$\,W, every few days from 2021-08-05 to 08-31) and towed 6 manta nets in August, inside the area
and the weeks of the only Sentinel-2 campaign over the patch: a $0.7^\circ$ box around the surveys has 167 level-2A
items on 20 dates in August--September 2021, including 2021-08-11, 08-16, 08-24, 08-26 and 08-31 --- days with
surveys. It is the one place on Earth where an open-gyre plastic measurement and a 20\,m multispectral image are
days apart, and it is cheap to look at (\S\ref{sec:ladder}, step 2).

\paragraph{The indices, as their papers define them.} $R_n$ is the reflectance in Sentinel-2 band $n$ and
$\lambda_n$ that band's centre wavelength (band 4 is red, 6 red edge, 8 near infrared, 11 shortwave infrared).
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{FDI}, the floating debris index (Biermann et al.\ 2020), brightens where something floats on the
water: it is band 8 minus a baseline drawn from bands 6 and 11,
\[
\mathrm{FDI} = R_8 - R'_8, \qquad
R'_8 = R_6 + \left(R_{11} - R_6\right)\,\frac{\lambda_8 - \lambda_4}{\lambda_{11} - \lambda_4}\times 10 ,
\]
with the centre wavelengths of each satellite: the factor $10\,(\lambda_8 - \lambda_4)/(\lambda_{11} - \lambda_4)$
is 1.7722 for Sentinel-2A and 1.7770 for Sentinel-2B.
\item \textbf{FAI}, the floating algae index (Hu 2009):
\[
\mathrm{FAI} = R_8 - \left[ R_4 + \left(R_{11} - R_4\right)\frac{\lambda_8 - \lambda_4}{\lambda_{11} - \lambda_4}\right].
\]
\item \textbf{NDVI}, the normalised difference vegetation index, and \textbf{PI}, the plastic index
(Themistocleous et al.\ 2020):
\[
\mathrm{NDVI} = \frac{R_8 - R_4}{R_8 + R_4}, \qquad \mathrm{PI} = \frac{R_8}{R_8 + R_4}.
\]
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Footprints.} Every value reaches the model with a footprint, the area and time span it stands for. A
manta tow sweeps $\sim 0.9$\,m $\times$ 1--15\,km: its token's area is the swept area ($\approx 0.001$--0.02\,km$^2$)
and its length the tow line; a pump sample is a point (\code{log2\_fp} $= -4$, the family's label for a point); a
Sentinel-2 pixel is 20\,m.

\section{Decisions D1--D9 --- this note's picks, each reversible}
\label{sec:decisions}

\begin{enumerate}\itemsep2pt
\item[\textbf{D1}] \textbf{The first store is the nets.} \code{mplastic} is built first; every satellite store is
gated on a test against it or against labelled pixels.
\item[\textbf{D2}] \textbf{One producer per cruise, the original producer first.} Precedence: The Ocean Cleanup
(its own cruises, its own size classes) $\to$ NCEI (each study's own accession where one exists, else the
harmonised table) $\to$ EMODnet (European cruises) $\to$ Isobe 2021 (rows not in the above, once its data licence is
confirmed). A row from a later source is dropped when an earlier one holds the same day within $0.01^\circ$, and
its drop is counted in the de-duplication ledger, never silently. Compilations are sources of rows nobody else
published, not of copies.
\item[\textbf{D3}] \textbf{Store what the net measured.} For surface net tows the stored quantity is pieces/km$^2$
(and g/km$^2$ where weighed) \textbf{as caught}; NCEI's pieces/m$^3$ is multiplied back by the depth the compiler
assumed, recorded per study in a committed table with the match that measured it. Pumps and grab samples are
volumetric and stay in pieces/m$^3$, in their own channel. The producer's wind-mixing correction is kept beside the
raw value, never in place of it.
\item[\textbf{D4}] \textbf{AOMI stays out of phase A}: non-commercial, mixed per-record licences, 64\,\% already in
NCEI, and 79 rows with latitude and longitude swapped. The probe measures what it would add; if it is worth it, it
goes on the \emph{private} track (held for this project's own use, never republished).
\item[\textbf{D5}] \textbf{Eriksen 2023 stays out until its licence is clear} (there is no licence in the
repository). It was one of the two further copies that settled the SEA conflict, a use that needs no redistribution.
\item[\textbf{D6}] \textbf{No satellite debris store is built at scale in this family's first pass.} Sentinel-2
cannot see the open ocean, PACE has no band on plastic's absorption, and CYGNSS's index is fitted to a model. What
IS built is cheap: the labelled pixels (\code{s2labels}) and one probe at the 2021 co-location window. A coastal
Sentinel-2 debris store is a phase-C decision with a cost estimate, taken on the probe's result.
\item[\textbf{D7}] \textbf{CYGNSS's microplastic index is rejected} under the family's rule --- raw observations
first, a derived product only where it carries observations nothing else does: it is a roughness anomaly fitted to
a plastic model, at $1^\circ$ and 30 days. The roughness itself (mean-square slope) is a phase-C candidate.
\item[\textbf{D8}] \textbf{Family code \code{13}, version \code{1.3}, root \code{tensors/\allowbreak family1\_3/}, registry
\code{tensors/\allowbreak family1\_3/\allowbreak family13.json}.} It inherits 1.2, which inherits 1.gf: the registry's
\code{inherits\_block} lists the RESOLVED chain (1.gf's 13 stores under their own paths and 1.2's four), so a reader
opens one parent list, not a chain. \code{ml/build\_family1\_registry.py} needs that small extension; today it
resolves one parent.
\item[\textbf{D9}] \textbf{SEA's Pacific tows come from Lebreton 2018's copy, with the published Law et al.\ 2014
values.} NCEI's accession 0211008 and its 2,530 ``Law et al.\ 2014'' rows are dropped by name (their positions are
wrong); SEA's Atlantic tows (Law et al.\ 2010) stay with NCEI. The ledger records, per SEA Pacific tow, whether SEA's
later AOMI value differs, so the choice can be reversed without rebuilding anything else. Whether SEA's resubmitted
values supersede the published ones is a question for SEA.
\end{enumerate}

\section{The ladder --- the order of building}
\label{sec:ladder}

\begin{enumerate}\itemsep2pt
\item[\textbf{0}] \textbf{Settle the SEA conflict} (\S3) --- \textbf{done 2026-10-11}: SEA's own AOMI submission
and Eriksen 2023 both agree with Lebreton's copy and not with NCEI's accession 0211008. The globe layer keeps
Lebreton's copy; \code{mplastic} drops NCEI's SEA Pacific rows (D9). Left open: which values SEA now stands by.
\item[\textbf{1}] \textbf{Phase A, on a hosted runner (one of GitHub's own build machines), \$0.} Adapters for
\code{mplastic}, \code{mobjects} and \code{s2labels} against the family-10 point-store builder; a probe that runs
the whole of each (they are small enough that the probe IS the build); the per-study depth table committed with the
match that measured each depth; the de-duplication ledger (rows read, rows dropped as copies per pair of sources,
rows kept). Publish to \code{tensors/family1\_3/}, registry \code{family13.json} inheriting the resolved 1.2 chain.
\item[\textbf{2}] \textbf{The co-location probe --- one hour, \$0.} For each 2021 aerial survey and manta tow in
the patch with a Sentinel-2 acquisition within $\pm 1$ day, fetch the level-1C tiles over the survey line, correct
with ACOLITE, compute FDI, and compare FDI's distribution under the line (and its upper percentiles) with the same
tile's open water away from it, and with the survey's megaplastic count. \textbf{Expected: nothing above noise} ---
the densest patch holds at most $\sim 13$ objects over 50\,cm per km$^2$ (Lebreton 2018's highest mosaic), of the
order of 0.001\,\% of the surface. A clear signal would be a finding worth a paragraph; no signal closes
\code{s2fdi\_gpgp} for good, with a measured reason.
\item[\textbf{3}] \textbf{Phase B} only on step 2's result: \code{s2fdi\_gpgp}, if it showed signal.
\item[\textbf{4}] \textbf{Phase C} decisions, each with its own probe first: \code{s2fdi\_coast}, \code{pace\_swir},
\code{cygnss\_mss}, \code{beachlitter}, and AOMI's unique remainder on the private track.
\end{enumerate}

\section{The falsifiers --- what would show each store is not worth keeping}

\paragraph{\code{mplastic} as a read-out.} The family is worth having only if a model reading 1.3's inputs
predicts held-out tows --- left out by CRUISE, so a cruise's neighbouring tows cannot leak --- better than two
baselines: the nearest tows in space and time ($k$ nearest neighbours, $k = 5$), and the published climatological
map (Isobe 2021's level-3 grid). Score: the error in $\log_{10}(1 + \text{pieces/km}^2)$ and, separately, whether an
empty net is predicted empty. If the model does not beat the neighbours, the inputs carry nothing the tows' own
geography does not, and the read-out is shelved.

\paragraph{\code{mobjects}.} Kept as the aircraft's own record; falsified only if its counts do not reproduce
Lebreton 2018's published megaplastic concentrations per mosaic --- a check made when the store is baked.

\paragraph{\code{s2labels}.} The baseline is an index threshold --- FDI above its 95th percentile over clear water
in MARIDA's training split --- scored on MARIDA's own test split. The store earns its place if a model reading the
11 bands plus the inherited ocean colour beats it on debris-versus-not F1 (a score that balances missed debris
against false alarms). If the threshold already matches the model, the labels are a test set, not a training store.

\paragraph{\code{s2fdi\_gpgp}.} The co-location probe of \S\ref{sec:ladder}, step 2.

\section*{Terms used above, in one line each}
\footnotesize
\textbf{Family} a numbered set of datasets built to one storage design and read by the programme's forecasting
model; \textbf{store} one dataset of a family; \textbf{registry} the JSON file listing every store of a family.
\textbf{Family 1.gf / 1.2 / 1.3} the global observation stores at 10\,km and 5 days or finer; 1.gf plus ERA5's
upper air; 1.2 plus the plastic stores. \textbf{Inherited by reference / \code{inherits\_block} / resolved chain}
a parent's stores listed in the child's registry without copying a byte; the registry field holding that list; the
list written out in full so a reader opens one parent list, not a chain. \textbf{Read-out} a quantity the model is
asked to predict or report. \textbf{Verdict} a store's recorded keep-or-drop result. \textbf{Tier P / G / G-fine}
points, profiles and tracks; gridded fields; grids finer than $0.25^\circ$ stored as compressed tiles.
\textbf{Footprint / \code{log2\_fp} / token} the area and time span a value stands for; its area as a base-2
logarithm, $-4$ being the label for a point; the unit the model reads. \textbf{\code{qc} / \code{platform}} a point
store's quality-or-class column and its instrument (here cruise) column. \textbf{Phase A / B / C} the cheap first
build, the stores taken on a probe's result, and decisions taken later. \textbf{Probe} a test run of a store's real
fetch code before any full build. \textbf{Falsifier} the test, fixed before building, that can show a store is not
worth keeping. \textbf{Hosted runner} one of GitHub's own build machines. \textbf{Public / private track} a store
anyone may read, or one held for this project's own use because its licence forbids republishing.
\textbf{Adapter} the per-source fetch-and-convert code. \textbf{Hugging Face / \code{chfrank/earth-tensors}} the
dataset host and the project's public repository there. \textbf{E-085} the experiment that added ERA5 to family 1.2.
\textbf{ERA5} the European weather reanalysis, a weather model run over past decades and continually corrected towards
observations. \textbf{SWOT / \code{sig0\_karin}} the Surface Water and Ocean Topography satellite and its radar
backscatter. \textbf{PACE / OCI} NASA's Plankton, Aerosol, Cloud, ocean Ecosystem satellite (2024) and its Ocean
Color Instrument, which resolves the whole visible spectrum (hyperspectral). \textbf{Ocean colour / \code{kd\_490}}
the colour of light leaving the sea, from which chlorophyll is estimated; the attenuation of light at 490\,nm.
\textbf{Altimeter} a radar satellite measuring sea height and wave height straight below it. \textbf{Wind-mixing
correction} the producer's scaling up of a net's catch for light pieces that waves pushed below the net.
\textbf{Confusers} turbid water, blooms, \emph{Sargassum} (a floating brown seaweed) and foam, which a debris
index can mistake for plastic. \textbf{Windrows} lines where wind and currents gather floating material.
\textbf{Manta / neuston net} surface nets that skim the top few tens of centimetres of the sea; \textbf{large net}
a bigger net towed for larger pieces; \textbf{pump / grab sample} water pumped or scooped and filtered, a per-volume
measurement. \textbf{Mesh} the size of a net's openings ($\mu$m, micrometres). \textbf{Microplastic /
megaplastic} the smallest size class (here 0.05--0.5\,cm) and objects over 50\,cm. \textbf{Nurdles} plastic
pellets, the raw material of plastic goods, counted on beaches. \textbf{Compilation} a table gathered from many
studies rather than measured by its publisher. \textbf{Accession / DOI} an archive's catalogue number; a
permanent identifier for a dataset. \textbf{CC0 / CC BY 4.0 / non-commercial / Apache-2.0} public domain; free with
attribution; free only for non-commercial use; a permissive software licence. \textbf{NOAA / NCEI} the US National
Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration and its National Centers for Environmental Information, the US national
environmental archive. \textbf{ArcGIS FeatureServer / MapServer / Hub} web services that answer queries for map records.
\textbf{CSV} comma-separated values, a plain-text table. \textbf{figshare / Zenodo / Springer supplement / GitHub
/ Google Drive} the public file hosts the sources come from. \textbf{The Ocean Cleanup} the organisation whose surveys produced Lebreton 2018 and 2024;
\textbf{cleanup-system catches} what its collecting systems brought up. \textbf{EMODnet}
the European Marine Observation and Data Network. \textbf{AOMI} Japan's Atlas of Ocean Microplastics; \textbf{Isobe
levels 0--3} its source dataset's stages: 0 and 1 per volume, 2 per area and wind-corrected, 3 a gridded map. \textbf{SEA / 5 Gyres} the Sea Education
Association, whose cruises towed the Pacific nets of Law 2014, and 5 Gyres, the organisation behind Eriksen's
tows. \textbf{NOAA MDMAP} NOAA's Marine Debris Monitoring and Assessment Project of beach surveys.
\textbf{Great Pacific Garbage Patch} the area of the North Pacific gyre where floating plastic gathers.
\textbf{Sentinel-2 / MSI / S2A, S2B} the EU Copernicus optical satellites and their MultiSpectral Instrument; the
two satellites. \textbf{Red edge / NIR / SWIR} light just beyond red, near-infrared, shortwave-infrared.
\textbf{nm} nanometre, the unit of wavelength. \textbf{Level 1C / 2A} Sentinel-2's top-of-atmosphere product and its
surface product after atmospheric correction; \textbf{level 2 / 3 / 4} elsewhere: swath measurements, gridded maps,
model-filled products. \textbf{Atmospheric correction / Sen2Cor / ACOLITE} removing the air's contribution to what a
satellite sees; ESA's land-tuned corrector; an open-source corrector tuned for water. \textbf{Item / tile / scene}
one catalogued Sentinel-2 image file, its fixed grid square, one acquisition. \textbf{COG / JPEG 2000}
image formats; a cloud-optimised GeoTIFF can be read in byte ranges. \textbf{AWS / requester-pays} Amazon Web Services'
storage; a bucket whose downloads are billed to the reader. \textbf{HTTP 200 / 206 / 302 / 401} whole file served;
byte range served; redirect; login refused. \textbf{CORS \code{*}} a header allowing any website to read the file.
\textbf{Copernicus / ESA} the EU's Earth-observation programme and the European Space Agency. \textbf{MARIDA /
MADOS} the Marine Debris Archive and its successor with oil spills, labelled Sentinel-2 pixels. \textbf{Plastic
Litter Project} the University of the Aegean's moored plastic targets off Lesvos. \textbf{FloatingObjects} line
annotations of floating objects on Sentinel-2 scenes. \textbf{CMR / Earthdata login / OB.DAAC / PO.DAAC / LP DAAC
/ GES DISC} NASA's catalogue, its free user account, and its ocean-biology, ocean, land and Earth-science data
centres. \textbf{OPeNDAP} a protocol for reading subsets of scientific files over HTTP. \textbf{SFREFL / \code{rhos}}
PACE's surface-reflectance product and its reflectance variable. \textbf{1,732\,nm absorption} the wavelength at
which plastic absorbs light distinctively. \textbf{CYGNSS / mean-square slope} NASA's Cyclone Global Navigation
Satellite System, small satellites that measure sea roughness from reflected GPS signals; the roughness they
measure. \textbf{Surfactants} natural films that smooth the sea surface. \textbf{FDI / FAI / NDVI / PI} the floating
debris, floating algae, normalised difference vegetation and plastic indices of \S5. \textbf{Float16} a two-byte
number. \textbf{Percentile} the value below which a given share of the data lies. \textbf{$k$ nearest neighbours}
a forecast that averages the $k$ closest observations. \textbf{Training / test split} the labelled data a method is
tuned on, and the part kept back to score it. \textbf{F1} a score combining missed detections and false alarms.
\textbf{TB / GB / MB} tera-, giga- and megabytes.
