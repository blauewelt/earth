{\LARGE\bfseries Family 1.gf in two pages\par}
\vspace{2pt}{\large the world's ocean and air, measured finer than 10\,km and 5 days\par}
\vspace{2pt}{\small blauewelt/earth --- two-page summary of the design note, 20 September 2026. A glossary of every term follows the two pages.\par}
\vspace{4pt}

\textbf{What it is.} Family 1.gf is a proposed set of datasets (``stores'') that gives the programme's
forecasting model the whole globe's ocean and atmosphere as measured at \textbf{10\,km or finer, every 5 days or
finer}: ship reports back to 1662, every non-Argo ocean profile since 1772, ship bottle chemistry, chemistry floats,
open-ocean moorings, and satellite fields kept at their own resolution --- ocean colour, sea-surface temperature
(SST), sea ice, swath sea level, wave height, cloud-top temperature and column CO$_2$. \textbf{What it is for:} the
programme's four prediction goals --- El Ni\~no, the ocean carbon sink, the Atlantic overturning circulation
(AMOC) and SST --- each of which has a mechanism at these scales (wind bursts, fronts, blooms, the ice edge,
boundary currents); every store must lower the model's error on one of them or be dropped. \textbf{What it is
not:} not a land dataset (that is family 1.0.tf --- marine species records ride its \code{gbif} store, which is global); not a replacement for the coarser global tensor (family 7.2,
$0.25^\circ$), which stays unchanged; and not gap-filled analyses --- raw measurements first, a derived product
only where it carries measurements nothing else does. Three sources slightly coarser than the rule are admitted as
named exceptions (E1--E3). A source whose licence forbids republishing is used privately and never redistributed.

\textbf{How it is stored.} One registry, \code{tensors/family1\_gf/family1gf.json} on the project's public Hugging
Face repository, lists every store with its tier, location, channels, checksums, licence and track. \emph{Point
stores} (tier P) are nine column arrays sorted by five-day bin, with an index of where each bin starts; a record
older than 1914 gets a 64-bit time column (schema 3). \emph{Fine grids} (tier G-fine) are one compressed file per
five-day bin, holding one frame per day (or per three hours), each cut into $256 \times 256$-pixel tiles with an
index of byte positions, so a read fetches only the few tiles around the place being predicted and an empty tile
costs nothing. Every value reaches the model as a token carrying its own footprint --- the area and time span it
averages --- so nothing coarse is copied into fine cells. Private stores use the same format in a private repository
and are listed in the public registry. Credentials stay on GitHub's build machines; rented machines hold none.
