{\LARGE\bfseries Family 0.9.tf in two pages\par}
\vspace{2pt}{\large family 1.0.tf with its raw imagery replaced by Google's AlphaEarth embedding\par}
\vspace{2pt}{\small blauewelt/earth --- two-page summary of the design note, 20 September 2026. A glossary of every term follows the two pages.\par}
\vspace{4pt}

\textbf{What it is.} Family 0.9.tf is family 1.0.tf (the land-and-coast stores) with \textbf{one substitution}:
instead of the list of raw satellite and aerial images --- and the small learned encoder that would turn them into
tokens --- it uses Google DeepMind's published \textbf{AlphaEarth Satellite Embedding}: 64 numbers describing each
10\,m of land, once a year, 2017--2025, computed from Sentinel-1, Sentinel-2 and Landsat and trained against radar,
LiDAR, climate and land-cover sources; free under CC BY 4.0, 577\,TB in all. \textbf{Everything else is inherited
from 1.0.tf by reference} --- the stations and gauges, the LiDAR, the daily fields, the prediction targets, the
fixed maps, and the whole of 1.0.tf's biosphere (canopy height and the canopy catalogues, fluorescence, leaf area,
the leaf calendar, the species records) --- exactly as family 10.2 inherits family 10.1: the registry lists them
with their 1.0.tf locations and checksums, not one byte is rebuilt, and this family builds nothing new for the
living surface. \textbf{What it is for:} testing land prediction now, and measuring
exactly whether the programme's own image encoder is worth its compute --- the two families differ only in the
imagery. The canopy is where that measurement is sharpest: the AlphaEarth embedding was itself trained against
GEDI, so canopy height at held-out laser footprints is where this family's cache and the programme's own tile
codec meet with everything else held fixed. \textbf{What it is not:} not sub-annual (one vector per year, so a five-day event is averaged away), not
extensible to new sensors (NISAR) or to years before 2017, and not a copy of Google's 577\,TB, which is read in place.

\textbf{How it is stored.} The registry \code{tensors/family09\_tf/family09tf.json} carries an \code{inherits}
block naming family 1.0.tf's registry; each inherited store keeps its 1.0.tf path and checksums (the four private
ones stay in the private repository), and only the four AlphaEarth groups live under \code{family09\_tf}. The
pooled stores (\code{aef1k}, \code{aef100r}) average the unit-length 10\,m vectors in each cell, renormalise, and
keep the average's length as a 65th \emph{coherence} channel (1 = the cell is one thing, near 0 = many), all in one
byte per number, as compressed tiles; \code{aef\_dots} keeps raw vectors as point rows; \code{aef10} points at
Google's files. A year-$Y$ vector is filed in the first five-day bin after 31 December $Y$, so a prediction inside
year $Y$ never sees that year's summary.
