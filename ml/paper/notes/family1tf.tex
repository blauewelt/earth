\begin{abstract}
Family 1.0.tf (``tf'' for \emph{terrestrial, fine}) is the land-and-coast \emph{family} --- a numbered set of datasets built to one storage design and read by the programme's forecasting model --- of fine-granularity observation
\emph{stores} (a store is one dataset, published as a small set of arrays): measurements at \textbf{ten kilometres or finer and five days or
finer} over the continents and a coastal band, on family 10's (the programme's granularity-aware dataset set) registry (the JSON file that lists every store),
tiers (gridded fields G, point measurements P, image tiles T) and token (the unit the model reads: a value with its offset in space and time from the place being predicted and its footprint, the area and time span it averages over). It is designed for what a model of the land surface has to
predict --- where forest will be cleared, how much carbon a canopy holds and
how fast that changes, where water will stand, what burns, what is planted,
where cities grow --- as well as for the forcing (the inputs that drive a system) the four ocean goals (El Ni\~no, the ocean carbon sink, the Atlantic overturning circulation and sea-surface temperature) need
from the land that borders the sea. The \textbf{land biosphere is the fifth
read-out} beside those four, and it is scored on two held-out measurements:
the carbon flux of flux towers (masts measuring the CO$_2$ a landscape takes
up and gives back) the model was not trained on, and canopy height at laser
footprints the model was not trained on. Three properties of the land record set
the design. The fine record is mostly \emph{points} (38,870 hourly and
132,437 daily weather stations, 2,932 radiosonde (weather-balloon) sites, 6,474 tide-gauge
records, coastal buoys and radars, gliders (autonomous underwater profilers), flux towers (masts that measure the CO$_2$, water and heat exchanged between land and air), half a billion fire
detections, and the LiDAR (laser ranging from orbit) footprints that measure vegetation in three
dimensions), and they fit tier P (the point tier) with one widening of the time column. The
daily \emph{fields} at 0.05$^\circ$ are 20--30\,\% valid and need the
sharded, tiled, compressed layout family 1.gf (the global sibling at the same scale) introduces. And the
\emph{imagery} --- optical at 10--30\,m across forty years and four
instruments on six satellites, radar at C and L band (wavelengths of about 5.5 and 24\,cm) in dual and quad
polarisation (two or four transmit--receive combinations), sub-metre orthophotos (map-corrected aerial photographs) where a country publishes them --- is
0.01--3 petabytes a year per sensor and can
never be pixels: it enters as a scene catalogue (a list of every image with its time, place and cloud cover, without the pixels) now and as cached
local-codec tokens (compact vectors computed from the pixels by a small learned encoder) later, per the tier T (image tiles) of E-078, the programme's multi-granularity design. Every candidate was checked
against its producer on 2026-09-16; a licence rule sorts each store onto a
public track (the public Hub repository --- the project's Hugging Face dataset store --- readable by anyone) or a private
track (built and held for this project's own use, never republished), so a
source's terms decide where it lives and not whether it is used; and the
note gives the stores --- including the living surface: the lasers' canopy
structure, fluorescence and tower carbon as photosynthesis, leaf area and
its calendar, biomass maps, and three billion species records --- the
arithmetic, a three-phase
ladder (phase A $\approx 0.7$\,TB, and $\approx 1$\,TB$^\dagger$ more for the
biosphere wave), the falsifiers (the tests that can show a store is not worth keeping), and the decisions left to
Chris.
\end{abstract}

\section{Purpose --- what the land family is for}

The programme builds a forward model of the Earth's surface state: encode
what is observed at a place into a compact vector, predict it forward, read
out anything (Chris, 2026-08-19). Families 7.2 (the global $0.25^\circ$, five-day input tensor) and 10 gave that model the
ocean at $0.25^\circ$ and the ocean's point measurements; family 1.gf gives
it the global fine scale. Nothing at ten kilometres or finer over land exists
in any family yet, and the land is where the finest and most consequential
predictions are asked for. This family exists for three uses, in this order.

\paragraph{Land change as a prediction target.} Deforestation and forest
degradation (where the next clearing happens, at 10--30\,m, weeks ahead);
the carbon a canopy holds and how it changes (biomass and canopy height, and
the rate at which a forest can plausibly regrow after loss); surface water and
floods; burned area; crop type and condition; urban growth. Each has an
observed read-out today --- alert systems at 10--30\,m, annual loss maps back
to 1990, LiDAR biomass, water-extent maps --- and each is a fine-scale,
land-only quantity that no ocean family can supply. Together they make the
\emph{land biosphere} the programme's fifth read-out beside the four ocean
goals, scored on two held-out measurements: the carbon flux of flux towers
the model was not trained on, and canopy height at laser footprints the model
was not trained on (\S4.3).

\paragraph{The land as forcing for the ocean goals.} The atmosphere over the
continents that border the Atlantic and the Pacific was fitted by the
reanalyses the model has seen so far; the stations, balloons, snow and
land-temperature fields below are what those reanalyses were fitted to, and a
model that has only seen the fit cannot learn where the fit fails (family
1.gf's argument for ship reports, on land).

\paragraph{Coastal sea level and currents as read-outs of the AMOC and
El Ni\~no.} Tide gauges since 1846 along the US east coast see the Florida
Current; Pacific island gauges see Kelvin waves (equatorial waves carrying a sea-level bump eastward) weeks before Ni\~no-3.4 (the standard El Ni\~no index, the SST of a central Pacific box)
moves; high-frequency radar measures the surface current at 6\,km hourly
where the boundary currents meet the shelf.

\section{The rule, the two domains, and the two tracks --- which sources qualify, land versus coast, and public versus private storage}

\paragraph{The rule} is family 1.gf's: an observation with spatial support
$\le 10$\,km (a point counts) and temporal support $\le 5$ days; a derived
product only where it carries raw observations otherwise inaccessible;
coarser cadence only where critical, and named as an exception. Read-out
\emph{targets} are held to the spatial rule and not the temporal one --- an
annual loss map is what the model is asked to predict, not what it is shown.
The biosphere wave (\S4.3) names one exception beside E4--E6: \textbf{E7},
the eight-day leaf-area composite of MODIS and VIIRS (the imaging
radiometers on NASA's Terra and Aqua and on Suomi-NPP), stored as
\code{lai500}. Eight days is three days coarser than the rule; it is
admitted because leaf area and the fraction of sunlight a canopy absorbs
have no finer global record, and the seasonal cycle they describe is what a
forecast at weeks has to get right. Its 46 composites a year are carried as
a \code{frame\_table} (a list of each frame's own dates), exactly as E4's
pentads are.

\paragraph{The two tracks.} A source's licence decides where its store
lives, not whether it is built. A store whose terms allow redistribution
(\code{licence.redistribution} of \code{yes} or \code{attribution}) goes on
the \emph{public track}: the public Hugging Face dataset repository, readable
by anyone and by every agent that works on this project. A store whose terms
forbid it (\code{no}) goes on the \emph{private track}: the same nine arrays
and the same registry entry, held in a private repository that only this
project's own jobs can read, used for training and evaluation here as a
single licensee and never republished, forwarded or mirrored. The registry
field \code{distribution} (\code{public} or \code{private}) records the
track, and family 1.gf \S4 gives the mechanism: where the private repository
is, how a rented box reads it for one job, and what may be published from a
model trained on it. Until 2026-09-16 the design had a licence \emph{gate}
that refused such sources outright, because every store was assumed to be
republished; Chris removed the assumption the same day (``we can manage to
build private datasets, and train on them without re-distributing''), and
the five sources the gate had refused are re-sorted here. Two are set aside
for reasons the rule itself gives: \textbf{HadISD} (the Met Office's hourly station set; Non-Commercial Government
Licence) is a quality-controlled derivative of the hourly reports
\code{ghcnh} (this family's hourly weather-station store) carries raw under CC0 (public domain), and \textbf{MSWEP} (a merged global precipitation product; CC BY-NC, non-commercial) is a
merged product whose gauges are reachable through \code{ghcnd} (the daily weather-station store). Three become
private-track stores: \textbf{ISMN} (the International Soil Moisture Network: 3,300 soil-moisture stations ---
``re-export or transfer of the original data to a third party is
prohibited'') as \code{ismn}, the soil-moisture station store; \textbf{GRDC} (the Global Runoff Data Centre: 10,000 river gauges, no
internet redistribution) as \code{river\_grdc}, a private river-discharge store, beside the public
\code{river}, whose CC BY 4.0 (free with attribution) Caravan subset --- an open collection of catchment data --- of 5,356 catchments stays
public; and \textbf{ECA\&D} (the European Climate Assessment \& Dataset: 25,919 European stations, non-commercial, no
redistribution) as \code{ecad}, a private European station store, phase C and only for the series GHCN-Daily
lacks. The same sorting applies inside a store: the research-only
sources of \code{tide} (the tide-gauge store) within GESLA (the global tide-gauge compilation) --- CMEMS, CV and UZ, three contributors' codes --- and the South African gauges are no longer
dropped by a filter but written by the same build to a private sibling,
\code{tide\_private}; and the non-commercial tropical basemaps of Planet-NICFI (Planet's monthly tropical mosaics, funded by Norway's International Climate and Forests Initiative)
join \code{cat\_submetre} (the sub-metre orthophoto catalogue, \S4.5) on the private track once their new-phase terms
are read. One source stays out, on access rather than licence:
\textbf{Dynamic World}, Google's 10\,m per-scene land-cover classification
(CC BY 4.0), exists only inside Earth Engine, and an export at Sentinel-2's
scene volume would be a catalogue of a derived product.

\paragraph{Two domains.} \emph{Terrestrial}: the continents, inland water
and ice sheets. \emph{Coastal}: a band of $\pm 100$\,km around the GSHHG
shoreline (the Global Self-consistent Hierarchical High-resolution Geography
set, public domain), rasterised once at 0.05$^\circ$ into a static
\code{dist\_coast} (distance-to-coast) channel. For a gridded group ``coastal'' means the tiles
whose minimum distance to coast is under 100\,km; a point store holds every
row it has and the consumer bounds by distance. The band is a build
parameter recorded in the registry, not a property of the data.

\paragraph{What is held already and not repeated.} Family 7.2's land
channels at $1^\circ$ and its statics; family 10.1's tropical moorings and
along-track sea level; family 10.2's daily 0.1$^\circ$ fishing effort
(E-081, the experiment that built it), densest on the shelves.

\section{Time axis, footprint and token --- how every value is dated, sized and handed to the model}

Time is family 10's five-day bin (a \emph{pentad}) from 1982-01-01, with the exact second in
\code{time\_s}; every row or pixel becomes the E-078 token --- value, offset
from the anchor in kilometres and days, and the two footprint fields
$\log_2 f_p = \log_2(\text{support km}/27.83)$ and
$\log_2 f_t = \log_2(\text{support days}/5)$. Family 1.gf's conventions
apply unchanged: $-4$ is the \emph{label} for a point instrument with no
areal support, a real support carries its true value even below $-4$ (a
30\,m pixel is $-9.9$, a 25\,m LiDAR footprint $-10.1$, an hour $-6.9$), and
a coarse value is one token at its own centre, never copied into finer
cells. Two land-specific points. A LiDAR footprint's \emph{vertical} axis is
not a footprint field but a channel axis (\S4.2). And an annual target map
(a loss year, a biomass map) carries $\log_2 f_t = \log_2(365/5) = +6.2$ and
is filed under the bin of its period's end, so that a target is never read
as an input from inside its own year.

\subsection{The biosphere, tiers G, P and T --- photosynthesis, leaf area and its calendar, canopy height and biomass maps, species occurrences, radar biomass}

``Predict the biosphere'' means, for this model, predicting what a named
instrument would read at a place, a time and a footprint, and five things are
asked of it. \emph{Canopy structure} --- how tall a forest is, how dense, how
much carbon it holds --- is measured directly only by the two orbital lasers
of \S4.2, and every canopy-height map below is a fit to them.
\emph{Photosynthesis}, the work the vegetation is doing while the satellite
passes, is seen as solar-induced chlorophyll fluorescence (the faint red
light a leaf re-emits while photosynthesising) from orbit and as carbon flux
at the towers of \code{flux} (\S4.1); the fluorescence is emitted by the
process itself, and the towers are the long in-situ record, the biosphere's
equivalent of the moored overturning arrays. \emph{Leaf state and its
calendar} --- how much leaf there is, and the dates of green-up and of
senescence --- are the seasonal cycle a forecast at weeks has to get right.
\emph{Disturbance} --- where forest is cleared, what burns --- is already
carried by \code{lossyear}, \code{alerts}, \code{fire} and \code{burned500}.
And \emph{where life is} is the dated, georeferenced species records of GBIF
(the Global Biodiversity Information Facility): the biosphere's \code{icoads}
(family 1.gf's archive of ship weather reports, which carries the
pre-satellite ocean) --- sparse, biased towards birds and towards wealthy
countries, and the only global record of species there is. None of it
advects: leaf area, canopy height and species are where they are, so these
channels' drift is zero and their drivers are the shared atmospheric and soil
channels, while their memory --- weeks to months for leaf area, years to
decades for canopy height --- is longer than the model's two-year cone. A
slow channel is therefore read as a \emph{state} and not as a history: last
year's map (canopy height, biomass, phenology, loss) enters as an input token
at a lag of at least one year and the target is next year's map at the same
footprint, which is \S3's annual time footprint $\log_2 f_t = +6.2$ and not a
new mechanism.

\section{Missing values and quality --- how gaps and bad values are recorded}

A channel not measured is NaN (not-a-number); a value outside its physical bound is NaN and
counted, never clipped; the source's own quality flag is kept in \code{qc}
and nothing is homogenised. Fields carry their quality layers as channels
(\code{lst05}'s QC day and night; \code{refl05}'s state QA) rather than as a
mask, so the consumer decides the threshold. Absence is information: a
station that stopped, a LiDAR track that was not flown, a cloudy day --- all
read as $n_R = 0$ (no readings in the token) or a NaN pixel, and the model is told so.

\section{Design decisions --- the choices made, and why}

\paragraph{Licence decides where a store lives, not whether it exists.} The
first draft of this note refused four of the best land networks because
every store was to be republished, and accepted having no soil-moisture
store as the price. The two-track rule of \S2 removes the price: a source
that may not be redistributed is built once, held privately, used here as a
single licensee, and never leaves. The cost that remains is named in family
1.gf \S4 --- a private store is invisible to the other agents and to anyone
reproducing the work --- so a result that depends on one says so, and the
public track stays the default wherever a licence allows it.

\paragraph{Vertical structure is stored natively, as the LiDAR's own
profile.} A canopy is a three-dimensional object and imagery cannot see
into it. \code{gedi} therefore keeps the footprint's relative-height profile
(RH25 to RH98), cover and plant-area index as channels, and \code{icesat2}
its terrain and canopy heights per segment --- the vertical axis in $C$, as
depth is for Argo. A single ``canopy height'' derived product would discard
exactly what the instrument was flown for.

\paragraph{State and event are both stored, so that a rate is measurable.}
A forest can be cleared in a day and takes decades to regrow; biomass and
height change slowly and within physical limits. The family keeps the
\emph{state} (height and biomass from LiDAR, annual biomass and land-cover
maps) and the \emph{event} (dated alerts, loss years, burned-area dates) as
separate stores with their own timestamps, so that a model's predicted
change can be checked against what growth and loss can physically do in the
interval. Whether such bounds are enforced in the model or only measured is
the model's decision; the data design makes the measurement possible.

\paragraph{One catalogue across forty years of sensors, never blended.}
\code{cat\_landsat} carries Landsat 4, 5, 7, 8 and 9 from 1982 with a sensor
code per row, and \code{cat\_hls} carries NASA's harmonised sample of the
recent part; the two coexist. The catalogue does not blend or calibrate
across sensors --- that is the local codec's job, conditioned on the sensor
code and geometry (E-078 \S4), and the falsifier in \S9 asks whether it
learned the shift. Radar is stored the same way: C-band dual-pol from
Sentinel-1 (there is no quad-pol mode), L-band dual-pol from PALSAR and
dual- and quad-pol from NISAR, each under its own code.

\paragraph{The catalogue before the codec.} Ten catalogues at 7\,GB turn
the tile tier's scene counts into measured numbers before a single token is
computed, and are the only way to size E-078c against reality; they are
phase A even though the tokens wait.

\paragraph{The coastal band is a build parameter.} $\pm 100$\,km covers the
shelf break almost everywhere and the radars' 200\,km reach; it is recorded
in the registry so a rebuild at another width is a rebuild, not a redesign.

\paragraph{Station elevation lives in the platform table.} Hashing a station
identifier would lose the one number that makes a 2\,m temperature
comparable with a 0.05$^\circ$ skin temperature; \code{platforms.json} (the per-station metadata file) keeps
it, with the source's own metadata, beside the nine arrays.

\paragraph{Annual products are exceptions, and named.} E4 CHIRPS (pentad,
derived, gauge-bearing, CC BY 4.0) is the one derived field admitted as an
input; E5 PALSAR mosaics (annual, the L-band record before NISAR) and E6
regional sub-metre orthophotos (multi-year cycles) are admitted to the
catalogue only; E7 the eight-day MODIS and VIIRS leaf-area composite
(\code{lai500}, three days coarser than the rule, the only global record of
how much leaf there is) is admitted as an input and carries its composites'
own dates in a \code{frame\_table}, as E4 does. Targets are outside the
temporal rule by construction.

\paragraph{What the imagery is for.} The catalogue lists optical at 10--30\,m
across six sensors, radar in two polarisations at two bands, LiDAR footprints
and sub-metre orthophotos where they exist --- the raw inputs a land
representation must be learned from. Family 0.9.tf (this family with its imagery replaced by Google's AlphaEarth embedding) is the alternative that
takes a published embedding --- computed from the same optical and C-band
imagery and trained against the same LiDAR, L-band and climate sources ---
in place of the pixels, and inherits every other store of this family by reference; the two families are meant to be compared, not
merged.

\sloppy Every array's sha256 (file checksum) against \code{store.json}; $N$ from the file sizes;
\code{bin} monotone and equal to \code{floor\_divide(time\_s, 432000)}; the
CSR (compressed-sparse-row bin) index reproduced; for sharded grids, every index entry decompressing to a

to the producer's listing; for catalogues, scene counts per day against the
producer's own catalogue; for \code{tide}, the station-years sorted to \code{tide\_private}
reported. A day absent upstream is a zero-length frame
with a counter, never a silent gap; a fetch lane refuses an empty listing or
a short download and records any relaxation in \code{degraded} (the build's list of relaxed checks).\par\fussy

\paragraph{The biosphere wave, and what it builds first.} Every store of
\S4.3 is new, so nothing in it is dispatched before its own one-month probe
has replaced this note's estimates. Phase A then builds, in this order: the
stores that are whole and cheap --- \code{canopy30} (one year, 261 measured
tiles), \code{pheno500} (the whole 2001--2025 calendar), \code{gbif} (the
public records of one monthly snapshot), \code{sif} from TROPOMI, the two
catalogues \code{canopy\_ref} and \code{cat\_biomass\_esa}, and, in family
1.gf, the whole of \code{pace4k} (PACE's daily 4\,km ocean colour); then
\emph{one year} of each store whose record is a terabyte --- \code{gedi},
\code{icesat2}, \code{lai500} (Terra 2020--2024) and \code{biomass100}
(2020, with the whole GEDI L4B group beside it). The year is 2022 for the
lasers because both flew it in full. What phase B buys is then a decision
made on measured rows, bytes and fetch rates rather than on an estimate.

\paragraph{The probe (a one-month test fetch through the real code).} One month per source through the real adapter on a
hosted runner: rows per day and distinct platforms; valid fraction and
compressed bytes per tile; scene counts per day against the producer's
catalogue; throughput; and for \code{tide} the station-years the licence sort
sends to \code{tide\_private}.

\paragraph{Two falsifiers.} For the \emph{forcing and coastal} stores,
family 1.gf's test: at anchors where the store has coverage, its tokens in
the cone (the past measurements the model reads around the predicted place, the anchor) must lower the held-out masked-token loss (the model's error at predicting hidden tokens) on the goal's read-out
against the same anchors without them, three seeds (three independent training runs); parity means the
reanalysis and the coarse tensor already carried it. For the \emph{land
targets} the test is reversed and is the family's main one: the embedding
trained on this family's inputs must predict each target at its own
footprint better than climatology (the long-term average for that place and season) and better than the same read-out from
family 7.2 alone --- next year's alerts from this year's state, biomass from
imagery plus LiDAR, burned area from temperature and reflectance --- and, for
the forty-year record, the codec's tokens of the same place from Landsat 5
and Landsat 8 must agree with each other more than with a different place,
which is the test that the sensor shift was learned rather than memorised.
