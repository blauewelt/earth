\begin{abstract}
Family 1.tf is the land-and-coast sibling of family 1.gf: observation stores
at \textbf{ten kilometres or finer and five days or finer} over the
continents and a coastal band, built on family 10's registry, tiers and
token. Land and coast differ from the open ocean in three ways that shape the
design. Most of the fine record is \emph{points} --- 38,870 hourly weather
stations, 132,437 daily ones, 2,932 radiosonde sites, 6,474 tide-gauge
records, 1,345 buoys, hourly coastal current maps from high-frequency radar,
3,660 glider deployments, 700 flux towers, and half a billion satellite fire
detections --- and they fit tier P with one widening of the time column, at
about 0.65\,TB for everything that may be published. The daily \emph{fields} at 0.05$^\circ$ (MODIS and
VIIRS land-surface temperature, reflectance and snow; IMS 1\,km snow; CHIRPS
0.05$^\circ$ pentad rainfall) are 20--30\,\% valid over land and need the
sharded, tiled, compressed gridded layout family 1.gf introduces. And the
finest imagery --- Sentinel-1 and -2, Landsat and HLS, NISAR, 300\,m OLCI,
375\,m VIIRS --- is 0.05--3\,petabytes a year per sensor and can never be
pixels: it enters as a \emph{catalogue} store now (one row per scene-date,
its footprint, time, cloud and validity) and as cached local-codec tokens
later, per E-078's tier T, with the first token build coastal (E-078c). The
research also drew a licence line that the ocean family did not need: the
soil-moisture network (ISMN), the river-discharge archive (GRDC, except its
open Caravan subset), the European station set (ECA\&D) and HadISD forbid
redistribution and are therefore not in this family however good they are.
Every candidate was checked against its producer on 2026-09-16; three sources
changed under the design in the last year (ISD frozen and replaced by GHCNh;
Suomi-NPP ending 2026-11-01; Sentinel-1A retired), and the family is built
from the successors. The note gives the ledger, the coastal-band definition,
the per-store arithmetic, a three-phase ladder (phase A $\approx 0.6$\,TB,
eleven stores counting the eight scene catalogues as one, fetched without an
account, with an Earthdata login on a hosted runner, or with a Copernicus
account for the Sentinel catalogues), what each store is \emph{for} against the four goals --- mostly
forcing, coastal sea level, and the read-out side of ``predict everything''
--- and the decisions left to Chris.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope: the rule, the two domains, and the coastal band}

\paragraph{The rule} is family 1.gf's: an observation with spatial support
$\le 10$\,km (a point counts) and temporal support $\le 5$ days; a derived
product only where it carries raw observations otherwise inaccessible;
monthly only where critical, and named as an exception. One rule is added
here because it decides more than the science does: \textbf{a source whose
terms forbid redistribution is not built}, whatever its value, because every
store is published on a public Hugging Face dataset and read by external
agents (family 10.1 handover). The registry's \code{licence.redistribution}
field carries \code{yes}, \code{attribution} or \code{no} per group, and a
\code{no} never reaches the Hub.

\paragraph{Two domains.} \emph{Terrestrial}: the continents, including
inland water and ice sheets. \emph{Coastal}: a band of $\pm 100$\,km around
the coastline, both sides --- the shelf seas where the boundary currents run
and the coastal strip where the tide gauges, radars and buoys are. The
coastline is the GSHHG shoreline (the Global Self-consistent Hierarchical
High-resolution Geography set, public domain, full resolution), rasterised once at 0.05$^\circ$ into a static
\code{dist\_coast} channel; ``coastal'' for a gridded group means the tiles
whose distance-to-coast minimum is under 100\,km, and for a point store means
nothing at all --- a point store simply holds every row it has, and the
consumer bounds by distance. The band is a parameter of the build, recorded
in the registry, not a property of the data.

\paragraph{What is held already and not repeated.} Family 7.2's land
channels at $1^\circ$ (NCEP's 2\,m air, wind, pressure, precipitation, snow,
soil moisture and temperature, heat fluxes, skin temperature) and its
statics; family 10.1's tropical moorings and along-track sea level, which run
into the coastal band; family 10.2's fishing effort (E-081, daily 0.1$^\circ$
per vessel, 2012--2024), which is densest on the shelves. Nothing at
$\le 10$\,km over land exists in any family yet.

\section{Storage}

Everything of family 1.gf \S4 applies: the registry at
\code{tensors/\allowbreak family1\_tf/\allowbreak family1tf.json} (schema 2,
the tier letters G, P, T with \code{layout} distinguishing bin-major,
sharded and static grids, the new per-group fields \code{licence},
\code{frames\_per\_bin}, \code{frame\_seconds}, \code{dtype} and
\code{verdict}, and the \code{siblings} line in family 10.2's registry, the current family-10 registry that inherits 10.1's stores and adds E-081's \code{fishing}); tier
P from family 10.1 with 1.gf's one widening (\textbf{schema 3}, int64
\code{time\_s}, $31 + 2C$ bytes a row, for every store whose record starts
before 1914 --- here \code{ghcnh}, \code{ghcnd}, \code{igra}, \code{tide},
\code{river}); the sharded tiled zstd layout for grids finer than
$0.25^\circ$; two-machine builds with credentials only on hosted runners; and
no silent-skip path. Three things are specific to land and coast.

\paragraph{Station stores are wide rows keyed by platform, and the platform
table is part of the store.} A station's row is one observation time with
$C$ channels (\code{ghcnh}: temperature, dew point, sea-level pressure, wind
$u$ and $v$, precipitation, snow depth, cloud fraction --- $C = 8$;
\code{ghcnd}: TMAX, TMIN, PRCP, SNOW, SNWD --- $C = 5$), \code{platform} the
station's numeric hash, and the store ships one file beyond family 10.1's
ten, \code{platforms.json}: for each hash the source identifier, elevation, and
the source's own station metadata, because a station's elevation is what makes
its temperature comparable to a $0.05^\circ$ skin temperature and it must
not be lost in the hash. \code{qc} carries the source's own flag; nothing is
homogenised. GHCN-Daily's long format (one row per station, day, element) is
pivoted to one row per station-day; \code{ghcnh}'s synoptic reports keep their
own minute in \code{time\_s}.

\paragraph{Profiles and soundings put the vertical axis in $C$.} \code{igra}
holds one row per sounding with $C = 80$: temperature, dew-point depression,
$u$, $v$ and geopotential height at the 16 mandatory pressure levels
(1000 to 10\,hPa), NaN above the balloon's burst; significant levels are not
kept in phase A. \code{gliders} holds one row per profile with the 16
Roemmich--Gilson pressures for T, S, O$_2$ and chlorophyll ($C = 64$), the
same convention as family 8 and family 1.gf's \code{wod}.

\paragraph{The tile catalogue is a tier-P store with nothing in
\code{values} but quality.} One row per scene-date: \code{bin},
\code{time\_s} (acquisition), \code{lat}, \code{lon} (footprint centre),
\code{platform} the hashed scene identifier, \code{qc} the producer's
processing-baseline flag, \code{fp} the footprint (a 110\,km Sentinel-2 tile
is $\log_2(110/27.83) = +2.0$; a 30\,m pixel is $-9.9$ --- the catalogue
carries the \emph{tile's}, the token cache will carry the token's), and
$C = 4$: cloud fraction, valid-pixel fraction, sun zenith (or radar incidence
angle), and the footprint's area in km$^2$. A sidecar \code{assets.parquet}
maps the hash to the STAC identifier and the asset URLs, so the token builder
of E-078c can fetch exactly the scenes a coastal cell needs. The catalogue
for all eight sensors is $\approx 150$\,M rows over 2013--2026, 6\,GB ---
cheap enough to build in phase A and the only way to plan the token build
against measured scene counts rather than the producers' round numbers. The
token cache itself (E-078 \S4: $k = 4$--8 tokens of 64 dimensions per
tile-date, from a local codec conditioned on sensor metadata) is a later
tier-P store, first over the $\approx 300$ coastal $0.25^\circ$ cells of
E-078c; its size is measured, not designed, by that experiment.

\paragraph{Grids that are not lat/lon.} \code{ims1k} is on a polar
stereographic $24024 \times 24024$ grid; like family 1.gf's sea ice it is
stored on its native grid with the projection in \code{tile\_grid.json} and
the tile corners in latitude and longitude, so the reader's cone search
needs no reprojection --- a tile is found by its corners, a pixel's position
by the projection formula the registry names.

\section{The build ladder, the probe, and the falsifier}

\paragraph{Phase A --- eleven stores, $\approx 0.6$\,TB.} \code{ghcnd},
\code{igra}, \code{tide}, \code{ndbc}, \code{gliders}, \code{flux},
\code{fire}, \code{lst05} (Terra), \code{snow05} (Terra), the eight
\code{cat\_*} catalogues (one store per sensor, one adapter each, counted as
one here), \code{static\_fine}, and the registry. Seven of the eleven fetch
without any account (\code{ghcnd}, \code{igra}, \code{tide}, \code{ndbc},
\code{gliders}, \code{flux}, \code{static\_fine}); \code{lst05},
\code{snow05}, \code{fire} (the NOAA-20/21 archive) and the NASA catalogues
need an Earthdata login on a hosted runner; the Sentinel catalogues need a
CDSE account for STAC, and the Landsat one a USGS login or a requester-pays
AWS read. Cost from the 10.1 precedent: the
point stores \$0 on hosted runners; \code{lst05} one verified box for a few
hours to assemble 350\,GB of shards, \$2--5; under \$10 for the phase.

\paragraph{Phase B --- $\approx 2$\,TB.} \code{ghcnh}, \code{hfr},
\code{river}, \code{alticap}, \code{refl05}, \code{ims1k}, \code{chirps05},
and the Aqua and VIIRS continuations of \code{lst05} and \code{snow05}.
\$20--30 in box time; the Hub conversation about terabyte repositories
before it, as for family 1.gf.

\paragraph{Phase C --- after E-078c.} Tile tokens over the coastal cells;
\code{oc300\_coast}; the coastal SST band if 1.gf's global store is not built.

\paragraph{The probe.} One month per source through the real adapter on a
hosted runner: rows per day and distinct platforms (point stores); valid
fraction and compressed bytes per tile (G-fine); scene counts per day against
the producer's catalogue (the \code{cat\_*} stores); throughput. For
\code{tide}, the probe also runs the per-source licence filter and reports
how many station-years it removes, because that number, not the archive's,
is what the store will hold.

\paragraph{The falsifier.} Two, because the family has two kinds of store.
For the \emph{forcing and coastal} stores, family 1.gf's test applies
unchanged: at anchors where the store has coverage, its tokens in the cone
must lower the held-out masked-token loss on the goal's read-out against the
same anchors without them, three seeds; parity means the reanalysis and the
coarse tensor already carried it. For the \emph{read-out} stores
(\code{fire}, \code{flux}, \code{river}, \code{refl05}, the tokens), the
test is the other way round: the store is a \emph{target} of E-078 \S5's
footprint-queried decoder, and it earns its place if the embedding trained
without it predicts it better than climatology at its own footprint --- the
E-076b form of the question (optimal interpolation from the same
observations is the bar). A read-out store that cannot be predicted from the
embedding says the embedding is not yet generic; it is not evidence against
the store.

\section{Decisions for Chris}

\begin{enumerate}\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{The name} --- as given; if ``1'' means ``10'', it is a prefix
change. Pick: as given.
\item \textbf{The licence gate}: never publish ISMN, GRDC (except Caravan),
ECA\&D, HadISD, MSWEP; filter GESLA per source. Pick: yes --- and the
consequence, that the family has no soil-moisture store, is accepted rather
than patched with a 36\,km product.
\item \textbf{The coastal band}: $\pm 100$\,km. Pick: 100\,km; the shelf
break is inside it almost everywhere and the radars reach 200\,km at most.
\item \textbf{Daily fields at 0.05$^\circ$}: Terra first, Aqua and NOAA-20/21
in phase B; MODIS Collection 7 replaces v061 when it is published. Pick: yes.
\item \textbf{\code{ghcnh} in phase B}, after \code{ghcnd}'s verdict. Pick:
yes.
\item \textbf{E4 (CHIRPS)}: admit. Pick: admit; it is the one derived
\emph{field} admitted as an input (IMS is an analyst's chart and the DSWx
and WorldCover classifications are read-outs or statics), and it carries
gauges.
\item \textbf{The catalogue before the codec}: build all eight \code{cat\_*}
stores in phase A even though the tokens wait on E-078c. Pick: yes --- 6\,GB
that turns the tile tier's scene counts into measured numbers.
\item \textbf{Tide gauges}: GESLA-4.1 through the UHSLC ERDDAP, filtered, or
UHSLC's own two sets only (325 + 563 gauges, licence clean). Pick: GESLA
filtered; the pick reverses to UHSLC-only if the filter cannot be applied per
station.
\end{enumerate}
