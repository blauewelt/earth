\begin{abstract}
Family 7.2 (build recipe \code{f7l2}, file stem
\code{family7\_global025\_pentad\_l2}) is the published form of the
programme's global input tensor: the Earth's surface and near-surface state on
a $0.25^\circ$ latitude--longitude grid from pole to pole, in fixed five-day
bins from 1982 to the end of 2024, in 56 channels across four
native-resolution groups, plus two static fields and two truth series. It
supersedes two earlier builds. Family 7 (\code{f7l0}) carried three groups and
two defects: an entire calendar year of observed sea-surface temperature and
sea ice was absent, and two logarithmic channels were computed in a precision
that does not reproduce. Family 7.1 (\code{f7l1}) added the fourth group ---
observed surface chlorophyll-a from the European Space Agency's Ocean Colour
Climate Change Initiative --- and filled the missing year, but published an elevation static
in which none of the 1,038,240 cells carried a number. Family 7.2 inherits
three groups byte-for-byte from 7.1, rebuilds the fourth and both statics, and
was afterwards rebuilt from the sources in an empty directory with nothing
inherited: four of its five files came back byte-identical to the published
bytes and the fifth is identical in every array value. This note states what
is in the files, how to read them, what the per-channel coverage actually is,
what the build refuses to do, and what it still cannot promise. A final
section is a self-contained ingestion handover for an external, read-only
agent.
\end{abstract}

\section{Purpose, and what changed from 7 to 7.1 to 7.2}

The programme builds a forward model of the Earth's surface state: encode what
is observed at each grid cell into a compact vector, predict that vector
forward in time, and read out quantities of interest from the predicted state.
The input to that model is a tensor, and the tensor described here is the third
published generation of the global one. Its predecessors are not superseded by
a change of science; they are superseded by three defects, each of which made
the tensor either wrong or --- more seriously --- impossible to re-derive.

Family 7 (\code{f7l0}, 4 September 2026) is the first tensor in the programme
to cover the whole planet rather than a North Atlantic window. Family 7.1
(\code{f7l1}, 14 September 2026) adds a fourth channel group, \code{oc025},
carrying the observed surface chlorophyll-a concentration from the European
Space Agency's Ocean Colour Climate Change Initiative (OC-CCI --- a
bias-corrected merge of the SeaWiFS, MERIS, MODIS-Aqua, VIIRS and OLCI
satellite records) at 4\,km, September 1997 to December 2024. Family 7.2
(\code{f7l2}, the same day) is the corrected build.

\paragraph{Defect 3, the one that is not about a number.} Thirteen of the
fifteen channels of the $1^\circ$ atmosphere-and-land group are byte-identical
across all three builds. The two that are not, \code{log\_prate} and
\code{log\_swe}, are the only ones whose transform contains a transcendental
function, and the builder was evaluating $\log(1+x)$ on a float32 array.
Float32 $\log(1+x)$ is not correctly rounded: the result depends on the NumPy
build and on which vector loop the processor dispatches to. Measured on a
realistic precipitation sample, 8.1\,\% of values differ by one or two units in
the last place between the float32 loop and float64-then-cast, and about one in
22,000 of those flips the float16 that is finally stored. \code{f7l2}
evaluates both channels in float64 and casts once, which is reproducible on any
machine. The magnitude of the correction is scientifically nil --- one float16
step is about $4\times10^{-5}$\,mm\,day$^{-1}$ of precipitation --- and the
property it buys is not: a tensor that does not reproduce cannot be re-derived
and checked.

\paragraph{Which recipe to use.} \code{f7l2}. It is the only build whose
elevation static carries data, the only one whose atmosphere group reproduces,
and the only one that has been rebuilt from scratch and compared with itself
(\S9). \code{f7l0} remains published as the historical original; \code{f7l1}
is superseded in every respect and is scheduled for deletion (\S12).

\section{Grid, time axis and bins}

The horizontal grid is \emph{point-aligned} at $0.25^\circ$: the grid points
are the multiples of the step, with no half-cell offset, and the rows run
\emph{south first}. Latitudes are $-90 + 0.25\,i$ for $i = 0,\dots,720$ (721
rows) and longitudes $-180 + 0.25\,j$ for $j = 0,\dots,1439$ (1,440 columns),
giving 1,038,240 cells; the arrays \code{lats} and \code{lons} in the small
file state them. Longitude wraps: column 1439 ($179.75^\circ$\,E) is adjacent
to column 0. A coarse $1^\circ$ grid of $181\times360$ points (\code{lat1},
\code{lon1}) is defined the same way, and a coarse channel is read at a fine
position $(y,x)$ by
\begin{equation}
y_1 = \min\!\big(\lfloor y/4 + \tfrac12\rfloor,\,180\big),\qquad
x_1 = \lfloor x/4 + \tfrac12\rfloor \bmod 360 ,
\end{equation}
so the two fine points on either side of a coarse point round to it and the
last two columns wrap to $-180^\circ$.

Time is a fixed five-day bin, a \emph{pentad}, counted from the epoch
1982-01-01 (npz keys \code{epoch}, \code{cadence}, \code{pentad\_days}): bin
$b$ covers days $[5b,\,5b+5)$ after the epoch. There are 3,142 bins
(\code{bin\_index}), the first opening 1982-01-01 and the last, bin 3141,
opening 2024-12-31 --- which is why that bin holds one day of source data
rather than five, and is empty or short in every group (\S8). Bins are
calendar-free: they do not align with months or years, and a year holds 73 of
them. A bin is an averaging window, not a date.

Two groups do not have one row per bin. The monthly ocean-interior group
\code{rg100}, built from the Argo array of profiling floats, has 252 rows, one per month from 2004-01 to 2024-12, each assigned to the
pentad containing that month's fifteenth; \code{rg\_bin\_index} gives the
absolute bin of every row (1609 to 3137) and \code{rg\_months} the month
label. The colour group \code{oc025} has 1,997 rows on an \emph{offset} axis:
row $r$ holds bin $1145 + r$, with \code{oc\_bin\_first} = 1145 the bin
containing 1997-09-04, and \code{oc025\_bin\_index} listing 1145 to 3141
contiguously.

\section{The four channel groups}

Channels are stored in four groups, each an array of shape $[T, H, W, C]$ in
little-endian float16, C (row-major) order, with a 128-byte NumPy header
(measured on all four published files). The array is bin-major, so one time
step of one group is a contiguous byte range (\S7). Storing every channel on
the fine grid instead would mean writing each $1.9^\circ$ reanalysis value
about sixty times.

Every channel is standardised before the float16 write, $x' = (x-\mu)/\sigma$,
with $(\mu,\sigma)$ computed over all finite values in all bins and stored per
group as \code{norm\_<group>}. \textbf{A stored value is therefore in standard
deviations, not in the channel's unit.} The $\mu$ and $\sigma$ columns of the
four tables below are read directly out of those arrays; nothing is retyped.
Float16 resolves a standardised value near 1 to about $10^{-3}\,\sigma$, which
is $0.012^\circ$C for sea-surface temperature and
$2\times10^{-4}$\,m\,s$^{-1}$ for currents. Anomalies are not stored: the
training code derives them at load time from a training-years-only climatology.

\section{Missing values}

\code{NaN} --- the floating-point ``not a number'' value --- means ``not
observed here at this time'' and is information rather
than damage. The pattern is systematic. Ocean channels are absent over land;
the GLORYS12 channels are absent south of $80^\circ$\,S and before 1993; soil
channels are absent over sea; the Argo group is absent outside 2004--2024,
outside its latitude band, and in any bin not on its monthly list; the colour
group has no rows at all before bin 1145 --- which is the period before any of
those satellites flew, not a gap.

Two cases are easy to misread. \textbf{Sea-ice concentration is absent
wherever it is below 15\,\%}, because that is how the source reports ice, so a
consumer wanting zeros on open water sets the channel to zero where
sea-surface temperature is finite and ice is not. And \textbf{\code{chl\_cov}
is \code{NaN} exactly where \code{log\_chl} is, never 0}: a block that saw
nothing has no coverage to report, and a stored zero would be
indistinguishable from ``observed, and the coverage was zero''. The tensor's
token design rests on that distinction.

A flat 56-channel view of a bin below 1145 has the two colour channels
\emph{absent} rather than \code{NaN}, and those are different states: absent
means no satellite yet, \code{NaN} means the satellite looked and saw nothing.
A consumer whose token design has no missing-versus-unobserved split should
use \code{NaN}, which is the conservative choice.

Counting the finite values in the whole tensor by streaming every file (\S8):
$1.0757\times10^{10}$ in \code{g025}, $2.8169\times10^{9}$ in \code{g100},
$2.5953\times10^{8}$ in \code{rg100} and $1.7115\times10^{9}$ in
\code{oc025} --- $1.554\times10^{10}$ observed values in total. The middle two
figures agree exactly with the counts the builder's own normalisation stage
reports, which is a check on the scan rather than a coincidence.

\section{The statics}

Two static fields ship inside the small file, both $[721, 1440]$ and both
complete: the manifest and the npz agree that \code{static\_n\_finite} is
\code{\{"elev": 1038240, "sphere": 1038240\}}. That key exists because the
previous build published an elevation static with no data in it and nothing
anywhere said so.

\code{sphere} is an \code{int8} surface class per cell: 0 ocean (702,642
cells), 1 land (226,495), 2 ice sheet or glacier (107,074, from Natural
Earth's glaciated-area polygons \cite{ne}), 3 inland water (2,029, from its
lake polygons). Ice takes precedence over ocean where both apply, because the
surface an instrument sees on an ice shelf is ice. The histogram is identical
in all three builds and the array is bit-identical across them.

\code{elev} is float32 elevation in metres from ETOPO 2022 \cite{etopo},
\emph{averaged over the cell}, negative under the sea, from $-9{,}687.95$\,m
to $+6{,}004.33$\,m. The convention matters when reading the maximum: a
quarter-degree box in the Himalaya is about $28\times25$\,km and averages a
summit with its valleys, so the highest cell on Earth reads 6,004\,m rather
than Everest's 8,849\,m. The array is bit-identical to \code{f7l0}'s, compared
cell by cell.

Two truth series are carried as (bin, value) pairs: \code{truth\_rapid}, the
overturning transport of the Atlantic at $26.5^\circ$\,N in Sverdrups as
measured by the RAPID moored array, which has watched that section
continuously since 2004 --- 1,459 pentads from bin 1626 to bin 3084; and \code{truth\_fc}, the Florida
Current cable transport, 2,490 pentads from bin 15 to bin 3141. Both are
Atlantic; they exist so that a global model is scored on the same instrument
as its predecessors.

\section{The ocean-colour group}

Colour is the only routine global observation of the ocean biosphere, and it
is one of the read-outs the programme has committed to. It enters as a
\emph{target} and probe before it enters as an input: the question the group
is built to answer is whether the physical state the model already carries ---
temperature, mixed layer, currents, wind, light through the calendar ---
predicts the biosphere a season ahead.

\paragraph{The block.} A point of the $0.25^\circ$ grid sits at a multiple of
$0.25^\circ = 6/24^\circ$, which is a cell boundary of the source's
$1/24^\circ$ grid; the six source cells whose centres lie within
$\pm0.125^\circ$ of the point on each axis are therefore exactly three on
either side. The block wraps in longitude; at the two pole rows it is
truncated to the eighteen cells that exist, and the coverage denominator
counts the cells that exist, so a fully observed pole block still reads 1.0.

\paragraph{Log space.} Chlorophyll is log-normal over four orders of
magnitude. The arithmetic mean of a block containing one bloom cell at
30\,mg\,m$^{-3}$ and thirty-five at 0.05 describes neither; the mean of
$\log_{10}$ does, and the programme's read-out for colour is scored in log

\section{Layout, and reading one bin over HTTP}

Each group file is a plain NumPy \code{.npy}: a 128-byte header followed by
little-endian float16 data in C order. Because the array is bin-major, one
time step of one group is one contiguous range, and the public dataset
repository answers HTTP \code{Range:} requests with status 206. The arithmetic
is
\begin{equation}
\text{offset} = 128 + (b - b_{\text{first}})\cdot H\cdot W\cdot C\cdot 2,
\qquad \text{length} = H\cdot W\cdot C\cdot 2 ,
\end{equation}
where $b_{\text{first}}$ is 0 for \code{g025} and \code{g100}, 1145 for
\code{oc025} (or, equivalently, the row given by \code{oc025\_bin\_index}),
and for \code{rg100} the row is the position of $b$ in \code{rg\_bin\_index}
rather than an offset. The slab lengths are 14,535,360 bytes for \code{g025},
1,954,800 for \code{g100}, 4,170,240 for \code{rg100} and 4,152,960 for
\code{oc025}.

\begin{verbatim}
import numpy as np, urllib.request

STEM = "family7_global025_pentad_l2"
BASE = ("https://huggingface.co/datasets/chfrank/earth-tensors/"
        "resolve/main/tensors/" + STEM + "/")
SHAPE = {"g025": (721, 1440, 7), "g100": (181, 360, 15),
         "rg100": (181, 360, 32), "oc025": (721, 1440, 2)}
FIRST = {"g025": 0, "g100": 0, "oc025": 1145}       # rg100: use rg_bin_index

def read_bin(group, b, header=128):
    H, W, C = SHAPE[group]
    slab = H * W * C * 2                            # float16
    row = b - FIRST[group]
    assert row >= 0, "that bin has no row in this group"
    lo = header + row * slab
    req = urllib.request.Request(
        BASE + f"{STEM}_X_{group}.npy",
        headers={"Range": f"bytes={lo}-{lo + slab - 1}"})
    with urllib.request.urlopen(req) as r:
        assert r.status == 206, "the host ignored Range - do not read on"
        buf = r.read()
    return np.frombuffer(buf, "<f2").reshape(H, W, C).astype(np.float32)

m = np.load(STEM + ".npz", allow_pickle=True)       # the 5.4 MB small file
b = (np.datetime64("2015-01-03") - np.datetime64("1982-01-01")).astype(int) // 5
frame = read_bin("g025", b)                         # -> 2411, 14.5 MB, HTTP 206
c = list(m["chan_g025"]).index("sst")
sst_C = frame[..., c] * m["norm_g025"][c, 1] + m["norm_g025"][c, 0]   # degrees C
\end{verbatim}

The assertion on status 206 is load-bearing: a 200 means the host ignored the
range header and is about to send 45.67\,GB. The programme's own web front end
paints a global frame this way, one range read per pentad.

\section{The build, its guards, and the reproducibility result}

The tensor is built by one resumable job, stage by stage, with a
specification digest per stage so that a changed stage discards only its own
state. \code{f7l2}'s stages are \code{glorys}, \code{sst}, \code{ncep},
\code{rg}, \code{occci}, \code{static}, \code{truth}, \code{norm},
\code{meta}, \code{publish}, and an opt-in \code{verify}.

\paragraph{The silent-skip audit.} Every defect in \S1 has the same mechanism:
a source could not be read, one warning went into a log nobody reads after a
green tick, and the build carried on and published a tensor with a hole in it
that nothing downstream could see. Worse, the marker that says ``this item is
done'' was written anyway, so a resume trusted it and the hole became
permanent. Six such sites were found and closed in one pass, and the rule they
now all obey is that \textbf{a marker may only under-claim}: the stage does not
mark an item it could not read, and refuses at the end rather than marking
itself done. The six were the monthly ocean-reanalysis chunk loader (whose
month list was a filesystem glob, so a month the mirror did not hold was never
even named); the reanalysis land mask (whose absence sent the two soil channels
into the tensor \emph{unmasked} over 71\,\% of the planet, finite, plausibly
scaled and indistinguishable from a masked tensor); the statics stage's ocean
mask; the Argo extension cubes (a bare skip left a \emph{live} monthly row
carrying nothing but \code{NaN}, which tells a model the subsurface was
observed); and two paths in the truth stage, including an empty truth file that
passed both existing guards.

\paragraph{The refusal flags.} A degraded tensor now has to be asked for by
name, and each flag records what it gave up: \code{--allow-empty-statics} (the
stage may write an all-\code{NaN} elevation; the metadata and publish stages
still refuse it, so the flag lets the stage finish and does not let the tensor
ship), \code{--allow-missing-years} (a fill stage may mark itself done over a
source year, a monthly chunk or the land mask), \code{--allow-empty-rg} (a
zero-row subsurface group), and \code{--allow-drift} (the verification stage
may report a difference and still exit 0 --- without it a difference is a
failed job, and the report is written either way, so the finding is never
lost). Two guards are unconditional: the elevation fetch floor is sized from
the 933\,MB file it guards (0.10\,MB\,s$^{-1}$ over a 180-second probe) rather
than from the 45\,GB streams it was originally written for, and the metadata
and publish stages refuse an empty static independently of any marker.

\paragraph{Reproducibility.} A seeded build proves nothing about the builder.
A hard link shows that the file system copied an inode; it says nothing about
the code that wrote the bytes, which is exactly how an all-\code{NaN}
elevation shipped green through a re-publish that verified the restore of the
file it had been handed. So the tensor was rebuilt with nothing inherited: an
empty work directory, no seed, the stage list
\code{glorys,sst,ncep,rg,occci,static,truth,norm,meta,verify} and no publish
stage at all, on the same rented box, 3\,h 23\,m for about \$1.1 at
\$0.31\,h$^{-1}$.

\code{g025} \code{1cb1e1af\dots}, \code{g100} \code{79c8075e\dots},

\code{np.savez} writes a ZIP archive and a ZIP stores a per-member timestamp,
so two archives of identical arrays cannot hash equally. The verification
stage therefore opens both and compares key by key: 41 against 41, none
only-local, none only-published, and the three that differ are the build's own
signature --- \code{builder\_git\_sha}, \code{built\_at} and \code{sources}.

have streamed about 400\,GB from an archive the same job had just measured at
1.20\,MB\,s$^{-1}$, which is 92.6\,h of transfer against a 24\,h job timeout.

would never have reached verification at all.

\section{Verification}

Every item below was checked against the published bytes: the small file
downloaded whole, the group files by HTTP range read, the manifest by its own
URL.

\begin{enumerate}\itemsep1pt
\item \code{static\_n\_finite} reads \code{\{"elev": 1038240, "sphere":
1038240\}} in both the manifest and the npz, and \code{sources} carries the
ETOPO and Natural Earth entries. The elevation fetch took three attempts and
4,025\,s at a probed 2.99\,MB\,s$^{-1}$; two attempts died on an incomplete
read, and the re-sized floor let the third finish instead of aborting it.
\item \code{sphere}'s histogram is \code{f7l1}'s exactly --- 702,642 / 226,495
/ 107,074 / 2,029 --- and the array is bit-identical to both predecessors'.
\item \code{same\_as\_base} is \code{true} for \code{g025}, \code{rg100} and
\code{oc025}, with each hash equal to \code{f7l1}'s, and \code{false} for
\code{g100}, whose \code{base\_sha256} (\code{ba59ed7c\dots}) is recorded
beside this build's own (\code{79c8075e\dots}); the top-level
\code{inherited\_groups} and \code{rebuilt\_groups} lists agree with that.
\item \textbf{\code{norm\_g100} did not move, against the prediction that it
would.} All four \code{norm\_*} arrays are bit-identical to \code{f7l1}'s. The
arithmetic that makes the prediction wrong is the same arithmetic that made
the defect invisible: the rebuild moves about ten float16 cells per bin out of
the 2,816,949,312 observed values the statistic is taken over --- four parts in
a billion --- which cannot move a float32 mean or standard deviation. A moved
\code{norm\_g100} would have meant something else had changed. In total only
five npz keys differ from \code{f7l1}'s: \code{recipe},
\code{builder\_git\_sha}, \code{built\_at}, \code{sources} and \code{elev},
plus the new \code{static\_n\_finite}.
\item The \code{g100} difference, range-read on five bins (200, 1500, 2411,
3000, 3141): thirteen of fifteen channels byte-identical to \emph{both}
predecessors in every bin; \code{log\_prate} differing from \code{f7l1} in 9,
9, 9, 12 and 0 cells of 65,160 and \code{log\_swe} in 1, 0, 2, 0 and 0; every
difference exactly one float16 step; no \code{NaN}-payload and no signed-zero
differences; bin 3141 identical in every channel. Note the direction:
\code{f7l0} and \code{f7l1} differ from each other in only 1--3
\code{log\_prate} cells per bin, so the float64 correction moves about four
times as many cells as the drift it removes. That is expected --- the two
float32 builds were both wandering near the correctly rounded value and this
one is at it.
\item The derived static grids the programme's front end reads were
regenerated at recipe \code{f7l2}: the elevation grid carries 1,038,240
non-null values where it carried 1,038,240 nulls, and the surface-class grid
is byte-unchanged. The browser test that would have noticed the empty grid ---
a non-zero and above-90\,\% non-null count on the committed file --- was added
and checked to fail on the old file and pass on the new one.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Limitations}

\paragraph{Colour reproduces from the partials, and is untested from the
source.} The colour reduction takes a float32 $\log_{10}$ on a
$4320\times8640$ raster about ten thousand times, deliberately: a float64
working copy of that raster is 300\,MB per temporary. Float32 $\log_{10}$ is
not correctly rounded, so this is the same class of defect as \code{g100}'s
two channels, and it was recorded as a known limitation rather than repaired.
The unseeded rebuild narrows it considerably: the float32 $\log_{10}$ runs
\emph{once}, on the hosted runner that reduces a calendar year into its
partial, and the box's fold over those stored accumulators is exact
arithmetic. So the claim is not ``the colour group does not reproduce'' but
the strictly smaller ``a colour year \emph{re-reduced from the daily 4\,km
files} on a different machine is not expected to reproduce'' --- a path
nothing has yet exercised, because every build so far has folded the same 28
partials. A chunked float64 $\log_{10}$ would close it at roughly zero extra
peak memory whenever the group is next rebuilt for another reason.

\paragraph{The atmosphere group differs from its predecessors at the last
bit.} \code{g100} is the correctly rounded build and the two before it are
not, but any of the three is scientifically usable: one float16 step is about
$4\times10^{-5}$\,mm\,day$^{-1}$ of precipitation and $1.7\times10^{-3}$ of a
snow-water-equivalent unit, in about ten of 65,160 cells per bin, in two of
fifteen channels. A consumer that keys a cache on a file hash must refresh;
a consumer that does not may keep the older file.

\paragraph{The elevation maximum is a box mean.} 6,004.33\,m is the highest
\emph{cell average} of ETOPO 2022 over a $0.25^\circ$ box, not the highest
point on Earth. Anything that compares this static against a summit elevation
is comparing two different quantities.

\paragraph{Standing limitations inherited from family 7.} The atmosphere and
land channels are a $1.9^\circ$ reanalysis, the coarsest source in the tensor,
and a stand-in for ERA5, which needs an account nobody in the programme yet
holds; the layout is designed so that replacing the source changes no shape.
Subsurface ocean structure is a monthly objective analysis at sixteen levels,
present only from 2004 and only inside the Argo latitude band. The colour
averaging discards 35 of every 36 source pixels; \code{chl\_cov} records the
loss but does not undo it, and the channel is clear-sky and daylight only, so
it loses most of its high-latitude bins and all of the polar winter. And the
record is 43 years, of which colour covers 27: no source shortens that
constraint, and it is the reason the programme's models are small.

\paragraph{Deprecation.} \code{family7\_global025\_pentad\_l1} will be
\textbf{deleted from the Hugging Face dataset repository on 2026-10-15}. It is
superseded in every respect by \code{f7l2}, whose three inherited files are
the same bytes; nothing in it is unique. \code{family7\_global025\_pentad\_l0}
\textbf{stays} as the published original, because its hashes are cited
elsewhere --- but it carries the 1989 hole and the
non-reproducing atmosphere group, so it is a historical record rather than a
recommendation. Pin \code{f7l2} by its hashes and re-read the manifest before
a re-ingest.

\code{github.com/blauewelt/earth} --- a read-only agent can still open one,

the bin and the check that failed. If issues are unavailable to you, report to
Chris.
