\title{Predicting any observed quantity of the Earth:\\
\large a design for a global ocean--land--atmosphere model\\
that reads the observational record through a learnable space--time cone}
\author{blauewelt/earth --- design paper}
\date{22 September 2026}

\begin{abstract}
We specify a two-stage model that predicts any observed quantity of the Earth
system --- a grid cell, a station, a float profile, a satellite pixel --- at
any place, time and footprint, from the whole observational record of ocean,
land and atmosphere. The design rests on one sampling prior: a quantity at a
place and time is most informed by a cone in space--time whose reach grows
with the speed of the fastest signal carrying information about it and ends
with that signal's memory, and it informs the mirror of that cone into the
future. Both stages sample this hourglass, supplemented by an ungated global
far field. Stage~1 reads raw values over the last thirty days through a waist
of several cells and writes one embedding per cell and pentad. Stage~2 reads
those embeddings over two years at log-spaced lags and writes the embeddings
of the next year as a residual on an asymptotically stable linear propagator
fitted as a linear inverse model. Every value enters through one token ---
value, observed flag, offset from the anchor, footprint, source --- so dense
$0.25^\circ$ grids, kilometre-scale fields, tile catalogues and point
observations share one encoder without resampling to a common analysis grid;
every prediction leaves through one query-based decoder that takes the same
coordinates and footprint. The cone's shape --- a drift toward the source, a
waist ellipse, a growth ellipse, a scale and a memory --- is learned per
$1^\circ$ cell and per channel; what the model reads is learned, what it is
asked to predict is drawn from a fixed distribution that the learned geometry
cannot move. The first experiments address initialised forecasts of observed
Earth-system quantities over five days to one year; long-term forced responses
and scenario projections are outside the scope and remain unvalidated. We
state the data, the geometry, both stages, the training ladder, the evaluation
contract --- which periods are development, retrospective evaluation and
prospective test, and what each source was available to know at issue time
--- the null ladder and the comparisons that decide each choice, beginning
with a fixed-target forecasting benchmark that asks whether the
representation improves a forecast beyond cheap models given the same
information. Verdict rules are fixed before any run. A results section is
reserved with the tables it will hold.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

We specify a model that, given the observational record up to time $t$,
returns the value an instrument would read at place $\mathbf{x}$, time $t+\ell$,
depth or height, and footprint (the area and span the reading averages), for
any channel the record has contained. A transport through a mooring section,
an El Ni\~no index, a coastal pixel's chlorophyll, a station's temperature,
a gauge's sea level, a forest's canopy height at a laser footprint and a
tower's carbon flux are instances of one query, answered by one
representation and one decoder. The Atlantic overturning, the ocean carbon
sink, El Ni\~no, sea-surface temperature and the land biosphere are the five
read-outs tracked, because each has a long truth series; they are properties
of the instruments, not of the target.

The design rests on one sampling prior, stated in \S\ref{sec:problem}: what
bears most on a value at a place and time lies in a cone in space--time, and
the value bears on the mirror of that cone (Figure~\ref{fig:hourglass}). The
prior is transport-inspired and is not a claim about causation; the model
also reads an ungated far field, and what its learned geometry represents is
predictive relevance. The record at
$0.25^\circ$ and five-day cadence is of order $10^9$ values a year, the kilometre tier a thousand times
more, and point observations are irregular in every coordinate; no
transformer attends over that directly. Stage~1, the codec, reads raw values
in a small cone --- thirty days, hundreds to a few thousand kilometres ---
and writes one embedding per cell and pentad; it is the only stage that sees
a raw value. Stage~2, the forecaster, reads embeddings in a large cone ---
35 days to two years, up to the planetary scale --- and writes the
embeddings of the future cone; it is what is rolled forward and scored.
Stage~1's hourglass is the small solid at the centre of stage~2's. The split
is in time, not distance.

\paragraph{Premises.} (i) At one basin's data volume the forecast is
insensitive to width and its held-out optimum arrives early. That is
consistent with a data limit and also with a limit in the representation,
the objective, the optimisation or the target noise; the design grows data
before parameters and measures which it is (\S\ref{sec:scale}), each rung of
the ladder being bought on the one below. (ii) A linear inverse model and
damped persistence are strong beyond two weeks, so the learned forecaster is
a residual on a linear propagator fitted as a linear inverse model and is
reported against both. (iii) A read-out with few effective degrees of freedom
cannot resolve a design choice; comparisons are decided on the dense field,
with a fixed panel of decoded physical quantities beside it. (iv) The
evaluation contract separates development years, a retrospective evaluation
block that has informed design decisions and is labelled as such, and a
prospective period that no statistic, model or decision has touched; the
exclusion of held-out bins from every window the forward pass can see is
certified before training, and every source carries the time at which it
became available. A technical report on the North Atlantic pilot
\cite{techreport} is the dated record behind these premises; this paper does
not repeat it.

\paragraph{Scope.} The first experiments address initialised forecasts of
observed Earth-system quantities over five days to one year from the
observational record up to the issue time. Multi-year rollout is a mode of
the forecaster, not its objective, and nothing in this design supports a
claim about scenario-conditioned change, tipping times or century-scale
behaviour; a scenario-conditioned projection needs a forcing pathway as an
input and an evaluation of the response under changed forcing, which a longer
rollout of an observation-conditioned model is not. The query interface is
broad; demonstrated predictability is whatever \S\ref{sec:results} will
show.

Section~\ref{sec:method} states the method: the problem, the token every
observation becomes, the cone and its learnable geometry, the two stages and
the query decoder. Section~\ref{sec:data} describes the data,
Section~\ref{sec:scale} the training ladder, and
Section~\ref{sec:eval} the evaluation protocol and the comparisons that
decide each design choice, with verdict rules fixed before any run.
Section~\ref{sec:results} reserves the results; Section~\ref{sec:related}
places the design among existing models.

\section{Method}\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Problem formulation}\label{sec:problem}

\paragraph{Queries.} Let $\mathcal{O}_{\le t}$ be every observation made up
to time $t$: gridded fields, point and profile measurements, image tiles,
each a value with a place, a time, a depth or height and a footprint. A
query $q=(\mathbf{x},\,t+\ell,\,z,\,c,\,f_p,\,f_t)$ names a place, a lead
$\ell\ge0$, a depth or height, a channel $c$ and a footprint in space and
time. The model returns a predictive distribution $p(y_q\mid\mathcal{O}_{\le
t})$ --- a mean and a variance in the channel's transformed space
(\S\ref{sec:stage1}) --- for any query whose channel and footprint occur
among the training targets. Lead zero with the value hidden is a nowcast.
Every stage below serves this one function.

\paragraph{The dependency cone as a sampling prior.} A driver moving at speed
$v$ reaches $\mathbf{x}$ at time $t$ from within $v\,\Delta t$ at
$t-\Delta t$. The points that can carry a signal to $(\mathbf{x},t)$ form a
cone opening into the past, its reach set by the fastest mechanism carrying
that channel and cut off by the channel's memory: wind stress forgets in
days, sea-surface temperature anomalies in months, a deep water mass in
years. Each channel has a family $(v,\tau)$. Run forward, the same argument
gives the points $(\mathbf{x},t)$ can carry a signal to: the point mirror of
the past cone. Past cone, waist and future cone are an hourglass
(Figure~\ref{fig:hourglass}), and advection is what the mirror looks like:
water came from upstream and goes downstream. This is a prior on where to
sample, not a law the model recovers. Predictive relevance is not causal
influence: a remote observation can reveal a shared driver or a hidden state
without any parcel travelling to the target, coupled pathways and
spatially varying flow break the one-channel--one-drift picture, and a
dissipative forward system does not imply a point-mirrored statistical
dependency. So the design tests a transport-inspired space--time sampling
prior, supplemented by a global far field; the geometry it learns represents
where the predictive information was found and is read as a diagnostic, never
as a recovered transport law.

\subsection{One token per observation}\label{sec:data-token}

The encoder attends over places at times, each carrying every channel there:
\begin{equation}
\text{token}=\big(\mathbf{v}[C],\ \mathbf{m}[C],\ \Delta x,\Delta y,\Delta z,
\Delta t,\ \log_2 f_p,\ \log_2 f_t,\ n_R,\ s,\ \text{sphere},\ \text{season}\big).
\end{equation}
$\mathbf{v}$: standardised anomalies of the group's channels; $\mathbf{m}$:
observed flags; offsets from the anchor in km, m and days; footprint
$\log_2 f_p=\log_2(\text{km}/27.83)$ and $\log_2 f_t=\log_2(\text{days}/5)$,
clamped to $\pm12$ (a Sentinel-2 pixel $(-11.4,-4)$, a laser footprint
$(-10.1,-12)$, a 30\,m annual map pixel $(-9.9,+6.2)$, an Argo profile
$(-4,-4)$, a 4\,km daily cell $(-2.8,-2.3)$, an OISST pentad cell $(0,0)$, a
reanalysis cell at its true support $(+2.9,0)$, a monthly $1^\circ$ Argo
field $(+2,+2.6)$); $n_R$: how many such observations were within reach;
$s$: a learned source embedding; sphere code; day-of-year harmonics. One
token per location and lag, not per channel: adding a channel adds a vector
entry, not a token. Footprint is constant per group for dense sources and
per row for sparse ones; nothing else depends on the storage tier (\S\ref{sec:data-fine}), so the encoder
never learns which file a value came from. The two footprint scalars are a
first approximation: they encode the support's scale, not its shape, and a
swath cell, a laser footprint and a monthly objective analysis have supports
a scalar cannot describe. Each source therefore carries its documented support
assumption in the registry (\S\ref{sec:data-fine}), and consistency between a
fine and a coarse reading of the same quantity is tested only where the
coarse measurement is an average of the fine one; the decoder is never asked
to invent fine-scale information from a coordinate alone. A $1^\circ$ value
is one token at the cell centre, seen from sixteen offsets by the sixteen
anchors in it. Point observations enter where and when they were made: the
$k$ nearest in space--time within the cone, $k$ small (three to eight per
depth group), one-sided in time. Three absences are kept apart. A slot the
nearest-neighbour search cannot fill is a whole padded token, excluded from
attention by the key mask; it is bookkeeping, not evidence. A channel entry
the source never measured (a land cell's SST, a month without Argo) is a
learned \emph{never-observed} entry inside an attended token, with its
observed flag off. A channel entry the encoder may not see during training
is a learned \emph{hidden} entry, distinct from never-observed, so that
``this is being withheld'' and ``this was never measured'' are different
facts to the model.

\subsection{Sampling the space--time cone}\label{sec:geometry}

\paragraph{Waist.} The present is a disc of radius two cells (${\approx}$13
cells, a small sunflower), read and reconstructed; the embedding $z$ is
written for its centre. Gradients need neighbours: thermal wind is a density
gradient, geostrophy a height slope, and neither exists in one cell. The
waist carries the cone's shape at lag zero, so a jet can have an elongated
waist.

\paragraph{Past cone.} Lags 1--6 pentads, every pentad: five days is the
tensor's cadence and thirty days is the codec's job. At each lag the sampling
set is a 24-point Vogel sunflower $\mathbf{u}_i$ mapped through a Gaussian
footprint,
\begin{equation}
\Sigma(\ell)=\Sigma_0+\ell^2\Sigma_v,\qquad
p(\ell,i)=\mathbf{x}_{\text{anchor}}+\ell\,\mathbf{d}+\Sigma(\ell)^{1/2}\mathbf{u}_i ,
\end{equation}
with drift $\mathbf{d}$ per pentad toward the source, waist ellipse
$\Sigma_0$ and growth ellipse $\Sigma_v$ (standard deviation linear in lag).
The sunflower's phase advances by the golden angle per lag, so the six slices
interleave. The anchor column is sampled at every lag.

\paragraph{Far ring.} Every lag also keeps a fixed ring of 24 points at
log-spaced radii from 28\,km to the antipode, read at full weight and never
gated. The design speed is the fastest measured mechanism of the family
times $1.5$ (the ocean: 3.6\,m\,s$^{-1}$, so 5.4\,m\,s$^{-1}$ and 2{,}333\,km
per pentad; wind stress: the atmosphere, global within a pentad). The
ellipse gives direction and density; the ring keeps a sparse, ungated reach
to the whole globe at every lag, so that a remote driver is not excluded by
the cone's shape. Twenty-four points are not exhaustive coverage of the far
field, and the ring is not a guarantee: a compact coarse global summary of
equal token budget is the comparator that tests it
(\S\ref{sec:eval-compare}).

\paragraph{Prediction cone.} The past cone point-mirrored: centre
$-\ell\,\mathbf{d}$, same $\Sigma(\ell)$, leads $+1$ to $+6$, sampled the
same way, read by nobody. Each point is a decoder query and a target. The
anchor column at every lead is always a target. The target positions are
drawn from the \emph{fixed} hourglass of the initial geometry
(\S\ref{sec:geometry-learn}) and are shared across arms and seeds: the model
learns where to read, and what it is asked to predict does not move.

\subsection{Learnable per-channel geometry}\label{sec:geometry-learn}

\paragraph{Per-channel geometry.} What moves a signal differs by channel
even inside one file group: currents follow the flow; sea-surface height
anomalies move west with eddies and Rossby waves whatever the current does
\cite{chelton}; sea ice moves with the wind at 1--2\% of its speed and to its
right \cite{thorndike}; sea-surface temperature follows the wind at short
lags and the current at long ones \cite{sutton}; below ${\sim}$800\,m at the
western boundary the deep current runs opposite to the upper limb
\cite{meinen}. So every geometry number is per channel: $\mathbf{d}_c$,
$\Sigma_{0,c}$, $\Sigma_{v,c}$, a log-scale $s_c$ and a memory $\tau_c$
(two scales for the L-shaped channels, lags 0--1 and 2--6). They are stored
hierarchically --- a group value plus a per-channel residual under L2
shrinkage --- so a channel that shares its group's carrier costs nothing and
one that does not pays for its departure with evidence. Initial values come
from a table set by physics: wind stress 500\,km with a ten-day memory;
currents, height and profiles 130\,km per pentad with a long memory; SST and
mixed layer $\max(\text{that},500\,\text{km})$ at lags ${\le}$1 then the
ocean's growth; land 400\,km flat, no drift; $\mathbf{d}=0$ everywhere; the
waist ellipse at the measured flow anisotropy $0.71$. Step zero is the fixed
hourglass. The biosphere channels are land channels: no carrier of their
own, an aperture that opens over the atmospheric and soil tokens, fire the
one exception with the wind as its carrier at 5--30\,km a day. Their memories
--- weeks for leaf area, decades for canopy height --- exceed the cone, so a
slow channel is read as a state: the previous annual map is an input token
at lag ${\ge}$73 pentads and next year's map at the same footprint is the
target; the same hourglass at the annual time footprint.

\paragraph{Aperture, at zero extra tokens.} A token carries all channels of
its group, so each channel reads the shared points through its own weight
\begin{equation}
w_{c,i,\ell}=\exp\!\big(-\tfrac12\,(p_i-\ell\mathbf{d}_c)^{\top}\Sigma_c(\ell)^{-1}(p_i-\ell\mathbf{d}_c)\big)\,
e^{-\ell\Delta t/\tau_c},
\end{equation}
applied to that channel's value and flag entries before projection. A point
outside channel $c$'s aperture in space or memory contributes ${\approx}$0
for $c$ and full weight for the others (400\,km at lag~1: currents 0.30, SST
0.73 at the initial apertures). The weight is smooth in every geometry
number, so the loss reaches all of them through every point at once. The same
weight applies to tier-P tokens, whose offsets and depths are known: each
pressure-level channel gets its own direction over the same $k$ nearest
profiles, with no sampling ellipse for profiles. The ring is not gated.

\paragraph{Parameterisation.} $\mathbf{d}=\tanh(\mathbf{x})\,v_{\text{design}}
\Delta t$, capped by construction. Ellipses as matrix logarithms,
$S=\log\Sigma=m\mathbf{I}+k\begin{pmatrix}\cos2\theta&\sin2\theta\\
\sin2\theta&-\cos2\theta\end{pmatrix}$: no kilometres, no angle to wrap, a
circle is zero, and an Adam step moves every radius by the same fraction of
itself \cite{adam}. Bounds are softplus offsets in log space, not clips, and
they are of three kinds: a \emph{minimum radius} --- the waist ellipse is
floored at one cell so a gradient always has a neighbour; a \emph{maximum
reach} --- the drift is capped at $v_{\text{design}}\Delta t$ by the $\tanh$
above and the growth ellipse's standard deviation at lag $\ell$ is capped so
that the cone at the longest lag stays inside the design reach; and a
\emph{default growth rate} --- the initial $\Sigma_v$, 130\,km per pentad for
the ocean's carried channels, which is a starting value and not a bound. The
design speed is a ceiling on how fast the cone may spread, never a floor on
how fast it must. The numbers live on a $1^\circ$ map
per channel (64{,}800 cells; bilinear blend at the anchor; ${\approx}$34\,M
nominal, half live, no compute), because the currents that carry the
overturning are ${\sim}$100\,km wide and a $10^\circ$ cell would learn a
drift near zero. Each map is a coarse $10^\circ$ map plus a $1^\circ$
residual under L2 and Laplacian priors, so a data-poor cell inherits its
region. A first-harmonic seasonal drift is a second-phase option for monsoon
regimes.

\paragraph{Learning: gate first, harden later.} In phase one the candidate
points are fixed --- a log-radial set of 36 per lag to the design reach plus
the anchor column --- so reads are regular and independent of live weights,
and the geometry learns through the aperture alone. Every 2{,}000 steps the
dense table per region is re-baked, the 24 points per lag drawn from the
group's mixture of channel ellipses, and swapped at an epoch boundary.
Geometry parameters get ten times the codec's learning rate
\cite{deformable}. Reading at continuous positions was rejected: it ties the
loader to live weights and its gradient sees four cells.

\paragraph{Guards.} A movable question moves to easy water, and a
stop-gradient on target positions does not stop the target
\emph{distribution} from changing when the targets are re-drawn from a
learned cone; nor can a variance statistic detect a move toward cleaner or
denser observations. So the input geometry is learned and the target
geometry is not: target queries --- the mirrored future cone, the hidden
waist, the fill-in cells and points --- are drawn from the fixed hourglass of
the initial table in every phase, including after each re-bake, and the
same target set serves every arm and seed of a comparison. The mirror of the
\emph{learned} cone is not a training target; whether a learned mirrored
target set buys skill is a separate, later comparison with its own guard.
The remaining guards are: the aperture never enters the loss weights; the
ring stays ungated; the evaluation query set is frozen and identical across
arms (\S\ref{sec:eval-protocol}); and the learned geometry is reported as a
diagnostic --- agreement between a learned drift and a known current is
descriptive, since a disagreement can be useful information routing and an
agreement can be redundant source structure, and neither establishes a
causal reading.

\subsection{Stage 1: encoding observations}\label{sec:stage1}

\paragraph{Tasks.} Each example is one anchor at one bin and draws one task
(Table~\ref{tab:tasks}). Dropout patterns fire independently: one channel
hidden at the waist; lags ${\le}\ell_0\in\{1,2,3\}$ hidden; a $90^\circ$
bearing wedge hidden; points hidden at random ($p=0.3$); for the nowcast,
the whole waist. The task is not signalled by a token; hidden markers and
the lead in each query suffice. Point observations --- a profile, a laser
footprint, a fluorescence sounding, a tower's half-hour --- are hidden and
predicted like cells; an annual map is a T1 or T4 target at its own
footprint and year, with the previous year's map visible. Three rules are
not knobs: in the main tasks a target's channel must be visible somewhere in
the input (the sampler counts violations, required zero), which keeps a copy
or persistence control inside every example; the waist family scores hidden
channels only; and every (task, family, lead) is reported against the score
of predicting the mean, and is called learned only where the ratio is below
one held out. The first rule also bars the cross-variable inference a
completion model is often wanted for --- a subsurface profile from the
surface alone, chlorophyll from physics alone --- so a fourth, small task,
\textbf{T4c}, scores exactly that: the whole target channel is absent from
the input, on a fixed list of (target channel, visible channels) pairs, with
its own predict-the-mean bar. A claim of general observation completion is
made only from T4c.

\paragraph{Model.} A masked autoencoder \cite{mae} over tokens that exist:
the encoder attends only to tokens that carry at least one observed or
hidden entry, padded slots are masked out of attention, and within an
attended token the hidden and never-observed entries are the learned markers
of \S\ref{sec:data-token} --- so the masking is at the level of entries
inside a location token, not of whole tokens as in an image MAE. It is a
transformer over the example's tokens --- about 900 in the gate phase and
1{,}200 after hardening (six lags $\times$ column, ellipse and ring $\times$
four groups, plus waist and profiles) --- with Perceiver-style
cross-attention into a fixed latent set \cite{perceiver}, writing a
deterministic $z$ of width 128--256. The bottleneck is the token count, not
the width. The decoder reads $z$ alone: a query (offset, channel, sphere,
depth, lead, footprint) cross-attends to $z$ and emits a mean and
log-variance. A decoder that could read the encoder's latents would let $z$
carry nothing; that path is closed, and its weight is zero rather than
small. At the larger rungs the feed-forward block is a mixture of experts
routed by sphere and channel group \cite{switch}.

\paragraph{Objective.} A heteroscedastic Gaussian per channel group in its
transformed space, decoupled:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}=\|\mu-y\|^2+\tfrac12 e^{-s}(y-\mathrm{sg}[\mu])^2+\tfrac12 s,
\qquad s=\log\sigma^2 ,
\end{equation}
so the mean trains by squared error and the variance by the likelihood with
the mean detached, which removes the variance head's habit of explaining a
hard target away. This is the faithful heteroscedastic regression of
\cite{faithful} (the mean's likelihood gradient replaced by its squared-error
gradient, the variance keeping the likelihood's), with the variance gradient
also stopped from reaching the trunk the two heads share; it is not the
$\beta$-NLL of \cite{betanll}, which re-weights the likelihood by a detached
power of the variance and never detaches the mean. $s\in[-8,8]$; the first 2{,}000 steps run at
$\sigma=1$; Gaussian in log space for log-normal channels, a hurdle
(wet/dry $\times$ log-Gaussian) for rain, logit-Gaussian for fractions.
Coverage of the $\pm1\sigma$, $\pm2\sigma$ bands is the calibration check.

\paragraph{Weighting across targets.} A plain sum over targets whose residual
errors differ by orders of magnitude lets the hardest dominate.
Standardising weights by marginal variance conflates \emph{varies a lot}
with \emph{hard to predict}. The scale used is a reference error --- the
held-out error of a reference model on that class, which is not the
irreducible error but a fixed, measurable stand-in for it:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_\mu=\sum_k w_k\,\mathrm{MSE}_k,\qquad w_k=1/\mathrm{MSE}_k^{\text{ref}},
\qquad\text{$w_k$ fixed} ,
\end{equation}
over classes $k=$ (channel, family, lead). With fixed weights this is a
Gaussian likelihood with one fixed variance per class; it equals the
gradient of $\sum_k\log\mathrm{MSE}_k$ only where the reference equals the
current error, and the objective actually minimised is the weighted sum as
written. The weights are fitted once, on a calibration fold of training
years (never a held-out year and never the run's own final training error),
frozen for the run, and capped at $100\times$ the median weight so that no
class dominates through a small reference; at the first run of a tier the
reference is the predict-the-mean error, and at later runs the previous
run's held-out error. A run is repeated once if a re-fit after it moves any
weight by more than $2\times$. Heavy-tailed classes (rain, chlorophyll) use a
robust scale or a Huber error. Logged per class: $w_k\mathrm{MSE}_k$ (near
one at convergence) and $1-\mathrm{MSE}_k/\mathrm{var}_k$. An explicit
multiplier for a class a use cares about sits on top, kept separate.

\paragraph{Stability.} Collapse is invisible to reconstruction loss, so the
effective rank of $z$ on a fixed held-out batch and a scale-invariant probe
correlation are logged each evaluation; two sub-threshold readings abort. No
quantisation from random initialisation. Gradient clip $1.0$; AdamW,
$\beta_2=0.95$, weight decay $0.1$, 2{,}000-step warm-up, cosine to 10\%;
bf16 with fp32 master weights; stop at the held-out minimum, at most four
epochs \cite{datacon}.

\subsection{Stage 2: forecasting embeddings}\label{sec:stage2}

\paragraph{Inputs.} Tokens are the codec's embeddings: the deterministic
$z$, the count of observations that entered it, offset, statics and season
at each sampled cell and lag. The past cone reads eight slices --- lag 0,
whose embedding already summarises the raw record over lags 0--6, and lags
$7,13,21,34,55,89,143$ pentads, log-spaced from the first pentad outside the
codec's window to the edge of the two-year window --- each with the anchor
column, a 24-point footprint whose reach grows at 130\,km per pentad
(${\approx}$1{,}000\,km at lag~7, the 4{,}444\,km cap from lag 33) and a
24-point ring to the antipode: $8\times49\approx400$ tokens against twenty
thousand for a dense window, which is the ablation. An embedding token has
no channel axis, so stage~2's geometry is per sphere rather than per channel:
one drift, waist, growth and memory per $1^\circ$ cell and sphere, on the
same maps, under the same fixed-target guard; the per-channel aperture is a
stage-1 device.

\paragraph{Linear backbone.} For the anchor column,
\begin{equation}
\hat z_{t+\ell}=\mathbf{G}_\ell z_t+f_\theta(\text{cone},\ell),\qquad
\mathbf{G}_\ell=\exp(\ell\,\mathbf{L}),\quad
\mathbf{L}=\tfrac{1}{\tau_0}\log\!\big(\mathbf{C}(\tau_0)\,\mathbf{C}(0)^{-1}\big) ,
\end{equation}
the propagator of a linear inverse model \cite{penland,newman} fitted by
regularised least squares on training-year embeddings at lag
$\tau_0$ of one pentad, retained unchanged, plus a transformer $f_\theta$
with a zero-initialised output projection. At step zero the forecaster
\emph{is} the embedding-space LIM, without projection or approximation, and
it must earn its distance from it held out; fine-tuning $\mathbf{L}$ is a
separate arm. $\mathbf{L}$ is a general real matrix: its eigenvalues come in
complex pairs, so the backbone carries damped oscillatory modes, and it is
non-normal, so finite-lead amplification $\|\mathbf{G}_\ell\|_2>1$ is
possible while every mode decays --- the optimal growth of \cite{penland}.
The backbone is therefore asymptotically stable (spectral radius of
$\mathbf{G}_1$ below one, checked at fit time) and is not called contractive;
a parameterisation restricted to a real decay spectrum
$\mathbf{U}\,\mathrm{diag}(e^{-\ell/\tau_j})\,\mathbf{U}^{-1}$ would drop the
oscillations and, with non-orthogonal $\mathbf{U}$, would still not bound the
Euclidean norm. Stability of $\mathbf{G}$ says nothing about the residual
model or its multi-year roll, which are scored on their own. Reported before
the residual is trained: the backbone's decoded skill per lead, its
finite-lead amplification $\max_\ell\|\mathbf{G}_\ell\|_2$, and after
training whether the residual improved it. Damped persistence is the diagonal
case and its own null.

\paragraph{Direct multi-lead targets.} The mirrored future cone at leads
$1,2,3,5,8,13,21,34,55,73$ pentads, each a query to the same head, predicted
directly, not by chaining: no compounding, amplitude set by the data at
that lead, targets where the correlation still changes. Anchor column weight
1, footprint $0.25$; the footprint targets make the prediction a coherent
field. Beyond a year the predicted anchor embeddings feed back as the recent
past: the multi-year roll is a mode, not the objective.

\paragraph{Objective.} The decoupled Gaussian on each target embedding,
weighted per class by the rule of \S\ref{sec:stage1} starting from the
predict-the-mean bar; plus a decoded-space squared error through the frozen
codec decoder at the leads the protocol scores (\S\ref{sec:eval-protocol}),
because the embedding metric depends on the codec's coordinates, the decoder
is nonlinear so the decoded mean latent is not the mean decoded forecast,
and the scored metric is the decoded one; plus, for the multi-year mode, a
two-step pushforward with a stop-gradient on the first step. Two mismatches
between training and use are measured on the complete pipeline rather than
assumed away: the size of the latent-versus-decoded gap per lead, and the
shift a read-out trained on true embeddings meets when fed forecast
embeddings (every read-out is scored both ways). If either dominates the
error, a larger decoded loss at the scored leads is preferred to a larger
model. Under a squared-error mean the amplitude shrinks with correlation, so
calibration is a property of the held-out minimum; Murphy's decomposition
\cite{murphy}
$\mathrm{MSSS}_{\text{clim}}=1-\big(1+a^2-2a\,\mathrm{ACC}+b^2\big)$, with
$a$ the amplitude ratio and $b$ the mean bias in units of the verifying
standard deviation, is logged per lead as the check --- ACC, $a$, $b$ and
MSSS computed under one set of area weights, the reference being the
training-year climatology so the identity is approximate to the extent that
reference differs from the verifying sample's --- and a scaling fitted on
training-year starts is a diagnostic only.

\paragraph{Regularisation.} The globe supplies every regime; the log-spaced
cone cuts parameters per example seventyfold; the free part is a residual on
a backbone the data pin down. Then: Gaussian noise on input embeddings at
$0.7$ of the persistence scale; dropout of past points; weight decay; early
stopping.

\paragraph{Uncertainty.} Three sources are kept apart and never counted
twice. The decoder's variance head gives a per-query predictive variance in
the channel's transformed space. A seed ensemble of three to five forecasters
measures seed sensitivity --- fitting variability, not the conditional
variability of the Earth system --- and is reported under that name. The
cheap probabilistic baseline is residual calibration: the distribution of
decoded residuals by lead and region, fitted on a calibration split of
training years and applied to the deterministic forecast, evaluated by
coverage and by proper scores in physical units (or under the channel's
stated transform, applied consistently; for a transformed channel the
transformed mean, the physical median and the physical mean are three
different numbers and each is named). A forecast distribution that beats
that baseline has earned its machinery. Once the deterministic level exists,
a shared low-dimensional noise vector conditions the head so samples cohere
across cells and leads, scored by the fair CRPS of an $M$-member ensemble
\cite{ferro2008,ferro2014,leutbecher},
$\frac1M\sum_i|x_i-y|-\frac{1}{2M(M-1)}\sum_{i\ne j}|x_i-x_j|$, which is
unbiased in $M$ and so comparable across unequal ensembles. Good marginal
scores do not identify a joint distribution: an ensemble trained on
marginals reproduced joint spatial structure for one weather architecture
\cite{fgn}, which is a reason to try the approach and no guarantee for this
one. The joint structure is therefore scored directly --- the CRPS of an
area average over $10^\circ$ blocks and of a temporal accumulation or event
duration, beside the marginal CRPS --- and the diagnostic that shows the
aggregate scores can see the problem is to permute ensemble members
independently across cells, which preserves every marginal and destroys the
dependence. Categorical marginals over a quantised embedding are the
alternative where the conditional is two-humped, as an El Ni\~no index at
6--24 months may be.

\subsection{Decoding queries}\label{sec:readout}

A prediction is a query in the token's own coordinates: offset in space,
depth, time; channel; sphere; source; footprint. A $0.25^\circ$ cell over a
pentad, a 300\,m pixel on a day, a station at an hour, a float at its
depths, a canopy's height at a 25\,m footprint and a 30\,m pixel's loss year
are one decoder, because it was trained on targets with those coordinates
and footprints, each scored against the observation that has that support.
The domain is what the training targets contained, at the footprints they
came with, and the model's behaviour outside that support is unconstrained
--- a variance head never trained on a region has no reason to be large
there. So support is a flag, not an inference: a query for a channel where
the source never covered (SST over land) is \emph{invalid} and is refused;
a query for a valid channel at a footprint, depth or place outside the
trained range carries an \emph{extrapolation} flag with the answer; and the
flags are computed from the training-target registry, not from the model.
A functional of many cells is decoded in the way its definition allows. A
quantity with a known definition --- Ni\~no-3.4, an area-mean SST, a heat
content over a stated volume --- is the exact area-weighted integral of the
decoded field, never a learned read-out. A quantity that is an instrument's
own aggregate with no closed form in the model's fields --- the transport an
array measures across its section --- is an attention head over the
predicted embeddings of its cells, trained on training-year true
embeddings, scored on forecast embeddings as well, and never on a spatial
mean (geostrophic transport is the contrast across a section, and a mean
cancels it). Read-outs are the cheap end of the system and choose between no
models. A point store's own record verifies its forecast; where a licence
forbids redistribution, the score is published and the values are not.

\section{Data}\label{sec:data}

No source is resampled to a common analysis grid. Each source is stored at
its own resolution and cadence in one of three tiers; where a source is
aggregated at build time --- the pentad mean of a daily field, the block mean
of 4\,km colour under a $0.25^\circ$ point --- the aggregation is stated with
the group and is the footprint its token carries; the sampler turns all of
them into one token at read time; the decoder is queried at a location and a
footprint.

\subsection{The $0.25^\circ$ pentad grid}\label{sec:data-tensor}

\paragraph{Grid and time.} A point-aligned $0.25^\circ$ grid, $721\times
1{,}440$ cells, rows south first, longitude wrapping; a five-day bin (pentad)
from 1982-01-01, bin $b$ covering days $[5b,5b+5)$; 3{,}142 bins, the last
opening 2024-12-31 and holding one day of source data rather than five.
A bin is an averaging window, not a date. A coarse $1^\circ$ grid
($181\times360$) is defined the same way; a coarse channel is read at its
nearest coarse point, so sixteen fine cells see one number. That is the
model's honest view and is not interpolated.

\paragraph{Groups.} Each group is an array $[T,H,W,C]$ in float16, bin-major,
at its native grid and cadence, so one bin of one group is one contiguous
byte range of one file (14.5\,MB for the ocean surface, 2.0\,MB for the
atmosphere) and a reader fetches a pentad with a single HTTP range request.
Four groups carry 56 channels:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{ocean surface} (\code{g025}, seven channels), $0.25^\circ$, every pentad: current speed and
its two components, sea-surface height and log mixed-layer depth (GLORYS,
1993--), sea-surface temperature and sea-ice concentration (OISST, 1982--);
\item \textbf{atmosphere and land} (\code{g100}, fifteen), $1^\circ$, every pentad, every surface:
wind stress and its within-pentad standard deviation (four), 2\,m air
temperature, 10\,m wind (two), surface pressure, log precipitation rate, log
snow water equivalent, soil moisture and temperature (0--10\,cm), latent and
sensible heat flux, skin temperature (NCEP; ERA5 is the intended
replacement);
\item \textbf{ocean interior} (\code{rg100}, thirty-two), $1^\circ$, monthly: temperature and salinity
at sixteen pressure levels, 10--1{,}900\,dbar (Roemmich--Gilson Argo,
2004--), each month stored in the pentad holding its fifteenth, 252 rows.
The field is an objective analysis of every profile in the calendar month
and is released after the month ends, so its support is the whole month and
its availability is the month's end plus about a week; the mid-month bin is
where it is stored, not when it was known (\S\ref{sec:data-split});
\item \textbf{ocean colour} (\code{oc025}, two), $0.25^\circ$, 1{,}997 rows on
an offset axis from bin 1145 (1997-09-04): block mean of
$\log_{10}$ chlorophyll over the $6\times6$ OC-CCI (the European Space
Agency's Ocean Colour Climate Change Initiative) 4\,km cells under a point,
per day and then over the pentad's days, and the observed fraction of
cell-days --- the evidence behind the value, missing exactly where the value
is, never zero; 855{,}742{,}985 finite values per channel, 41.3\,\% of the
group, colour over estuaries and lagoons the land mask calls land kept and
counted;
\item \textbf{statics}: surface class (702{,}642 ocean, 226{,}495 land,
107{,}074 ice sheet, 2{,}029 inland water; ice over ocean on a shelf) and
cell-mean elevation from ETOPO 2022, both complete in all 1{,}038{,}240 cells.
\end{itemize}
No channel is masked by sphere: a land cell reads its skin and air
temperature and soil, an ocean cell its surface fields, every cell the
shared atmosphere. A channel is a quantity and an instrument, never a
sphere; observed sea-surface and land-surface temperatures are separate
channels.

\paragraph{Standardisation, transforms, missing values.} Stored values are
$x'=(x-\mu)/\sigma$ per channel. At load time every channel becomes a
standardised anomaly against a day-of-year harmonic climatology (annual and
semi-annual) fitted on training years only, over a training-year standard
deviation; no held-out year touches any statistic. Log-normal quantities
(mixed-layer depth, chlorophyll, precipitation, snow) are logged first;
bounded fractions (sea ice, soil moisture) go through a logit. A value that
does not exist --- a land cell's SST, a month without Argo, a cell under
cloud --- is not zero: every token carries an observed flag per channel, and
an unobserved entry is masked from loss and input. Holes in the grid are
holes in the sources, counted per channel and bin at build time and again by
streaming every published file: $1.554\times10^{10}$ observed values; every
channel full over its own record, except where a source is empty --- the
reanalysis ocean before bin 804 (1993-01-03), sea ice in bins 433--439 (a
change of microwave sensor), 2846 and 3090, colour in bin 1153 --- and the
one-day last bin, empty in the two groups that require three days of a
pentad.

\subsection{Finer sources in three storage tiers}\label{sec:data-fine}

Three facts decide how finer sources enter. A coarse value copied into fine
cells is a lie: nine copies are not nine measurements. A fine value averaged
into a coarse cell discards what the source was bought for: 4\,km colour
into $0.25^\circ$ keeps one part in thirty-six. And missing is not damage,
nor is far away: the nearest profile's value depends on its distance, which
a grid cell hides. Sources are therefore stored by density, in three tiers
under one registry:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{G, gridded dense} --- fields at ${\lesssim}$5\,km, daily to
monthly, near-complete: colour at 4\,km, SST at 5.6\,km per sensor and
2.2\,km merged, sea ice at 6.25\,km, 4\,km cloud-top temperature,
$0.1^\circ$ daily rain, the daily $0.05^\circ$ land temperature, snow and
reflectance, 500\,m leaf area every eight days, and the annual maps ---
forest loss, canopy height and biomass, the leaf calendar --- at 30--500\,m.
A group finer than $0.25^\circ$ is sharded: one file per group and
pentad holding $F$ frames (one per day, or three hours, or one per year), each
frame cut into $256\times256$-pixel tiles of the native grid, each tile
compressed on its own with zstandard; beside the shard an index of (byte
offset, length) per frame and tile, where an empty tile has length zero and is
never stored and a frame absent from the source has offset $-1$, so a missing
day is recorded, not silently skipped. Tiles are written frame by frame
without gaps, so a frame is two range reads --- its slab of the index, then
one contiguous span of the shard --- and a cone decodes only the tiles it
touches (578 tiles a frame for the 4\,km colour grid, 11.6\,\% of its
pixel-days valid). Footprint is a constant per group.
\item \textbf{P, points, profiles, tracks} --- Argo and BGC-Argo, every
non-Argo profile since 1772, ship and buoy reports since 1662, bottle
chemistry, drifters, tropical and open-ocean moorings, the overturning
arrays' instruments, surface CO$_2$, along-track altimetry at 7\,km and SWOT
swath cells at 2\,km, column CO$_2$ soundings, wave height, scatterometer
wind cells; on land, stations, balloon soundings, tide gauges, buoys,
gliders, flux towers, fire detections, rivers, soil probes, laser
footprints of the canopy, fluorescence soundings, species occurrences. A
store is a set of columns --- time in integer seconds, latitude,
longitude, values in raw units, mask, quality, hashed platform, per-row
footprint --- sorted by (bin, time) with a compressed-sparse-row index of
row offsets per bin, so the $k$ nearest observations in space--time are one
bounded scan that stops at the anchor's own bin and never reads the future.
The bin is derived in integers, $b=\lfloor t_s/432{,}000\rfloor$ with $t_s$
the seconds since 1982-01-01T00:00\,UTC, floored toward minus infinity, so
pre-1982 rows keep negative bins (drifters from 1979, carbon from 1957, ship
reports from 1662) and the loader, not the store, clips to a tensor's axis;
each store's index runs over its own first bin, which is not zero. The
seconds are int32 (1913 to 2050), or int64 in a store whose record begins
before 1914 (schema 3 against schema 2). They replaced float32 days, which
resolve 21\,s in 1993 and 84\,s by 2015 --- up to 316 consecutive one-second
altimeter samples shared one timestamp, 550\,km of track inside which
$\Delta t$ ordered nothing, and 908 carbon rows within 84\,s of a pentad
boundary were filed one bin late; integer seconds cost the same four bytes.
\item \textbf{T, tiles} --- imagery at ${\le}$1\,km (Sentinel-1/2, Landsat,
NISAR, OLCI, SLSTR, VIIRS, MODIS, the L- and P-band radar mosaics) and the
10\,m and 1\,m canopy-height maps are not stored as values. The store is a
scene-date catalogue (footprint, sensor, time, cloud fraction, location) and
a token cache: a local codec turns one tile-date into a few 64-dimensional
vectors, stored as a tier-P store whose values are those vectors, footprint
the tile and the day. AlphaEarth's published embedding \cite{alphaearth}
--- 64 numbers per 10\,m of land per year --- enters by the same path as a
ready-made cache, so the programme's own tile codec can be measured against
it with everything else fixed.
\end{itemize}
One time axis (the pentad bin; tier-P rows keep their exact time, so
$\Delta t$ is fractional); one registry per family (tier, layout, cadence,
channels, footprint constants, first bin, checksums, sources, licence,
builder commit --- a consumer dispatches on the tier), in which every group
also carries its \emph{source contract}: \code{support\_start} and
\code{support\_end} (the interval of measurements a value summarises,
relative to its stamp --- zero width for an instantaneous reading, the
calendar month for a monthly analysis, the fit period for an annual map),
\code{available\_at} (the earliest time the value could have been used,
from the producer's release latency; equal to the stamp for a real-time
source), \code{product\_version}, and \code{lineage} (the measurements a
derived product was fitted to or assimilated, by store name --- the
Roemmich--Gilson field to the Argo profiles, the reanalysis to the profiles
and the altimeter, a canopy map to the laser footprints, a tile cache to the
imagery and to whatever supervised it). Annual maps, retrospective analyses,
climatological backgrounds and pre-trained embedding caches carry these
fields like any other source, since an anomaly climatology recomputed on
training years does not remove future information already inside an
upstream product; one sampler contract
(\code{gather(anchor, family)} returns tokens per group under that group's
cone family, with a fixed slot count so batches stay rectangular). Licences
decide the track. Each group's licence block records \code{redistribution}
(yes, with attribution, or no) and \code{derived\_works} (free,
non-commercial or restricted) from the licence's own clause, and
\code{distribution} is public by default and private whenever redistribution
is not granted: a private store keeps the same arrays and layout in a private
repository, is listed in the public registry with its sizes and checks so a
reader without access skips it by the field rather than by a failed fetch,
and is pulled for a job and never kept on a rented machine. What may leave the
private track is a trained model and its scores where \code{derived\_works}
allows, never the values, and a result resting on a private store names it. A
non-commercial source whose licence permits redistribution stays public and
carries the restriction in its fields.

The global fine-granularity family (1.gf, Table~\ref{tab:gf}) is the instance of
tiers G and P over the ocean and air at ${\le}$10\,km and ${\le}$5 days; a
land-and-coast family (1.0.tf) holds the station networks, gauges, towers, fires,
rivers, the $0.05^\circ$ land fields, the imagery catalogue, the
land-change targets and the biosphere stores of Table~\ref{tab:bio}; a
third (0.9.tf) is that family with the catalogue replaced by the AlphaEarth
cache (\S\ref{sec:data-built} gives what is built).
Admission: raw measurements first, a derived product only where it carries
measurements nothing else does or is itself a target a user will ask for
(the annual maps), no gap-filled analyses; four named exceptions slightly
coarser than the rule (12.5\,km scatterometer wind, 11\,km rain, one
10.5\,km CO$_2$ sounder, eight-day leaf area); and every store is scored on
the read-out it was admitted for, at equal token budget with and without it,
with its incremental cost recorded. The verdict has three values, not two:
\emph{useful} (it lowers the error beyond the paired interval), \emph{not
useful at this budget}, and \emph{inconclusive} (the read-out lacked the
power to say). A store that is not useful at this budget or inconclusive is
deferred --- its registry entry, provenance and cost stay --- and is not
called uninformative, because an absence of measured benefit on a low-power
read-out is not an absence of information. An observation count is not
independent information: a long altimeter track or a dense buoy array can
dominate a loss or a nearest-neighbour set without adding a regime, so
sampling and verification of tier-P stores are platform- and track-aware,
and a store's benefit is inspected conditional on the companion channels
that make it useful.

\paragraph{The local codec.} A $64\times64$ tile at 10\,m is 4{,}096 pixels
of 13 bands and a $0.25^\circ$ cell holds ${\sim}$7{,}000 of them; they
cannot be sampling points. The local codec is a masked autoencoder over one
tile-date, conditioned on sensor metadata (resolution, incidence or sun
angle) with a learned source embedding so one codec serves optical and
radar. A same-place--different-date contrastive term would make the token
invariant to exactly the changes a forecast must retain, so it is not
switched on by default: the token's sensitivity to real change (a
clearing, a burn, a green-up) is measured first, on the loss-year and
phenology targets, and any invariance term is added only with augmentations
that preserve the state of interest. It emits $k=4$--$8$ tokens of 64 dimensions,
cached once, reused by every anchor whose cone holds the tile; it sees one
date, and the cone codec supplies time. Tile size is a knob: 640\,m tiles at
seventy clear dates a year are ${\sim}$20\,TB of tokens a year globally,
2.56\,km tiles sixteen times less. Imagery is streamed once to train and once
to encode, never kept.

\subsection{Biosphere observations}\label{sec:data-bio}

The living surface is observed by few
instruments and fitted by many maps. The measurements are the laser
footprints (GEDI \cite{gedi} to $\pm51.6^\circ$, ICESat-2 \cite{atl08}
beyond), which alone see a canopy in three dimensions; fluorescence
soundings \cite{tropomisif}, emitted by photosynthesis itself; the towers'
eddy-covariance carbon flux \cite{fluxnet}, the long in-situ record; leaf
area every eight days; fire detections; and 3.7 billion dated, located
species occurrences \cite{gbif} --- sparse and biased, and the only global record of
where life is. The annual maps --- forest loss \cite{hansen}, canopy height
\cite{potapov,lang,tolan}, biomass \cite{ccibiomass}, the leaf calendar ---
are each fitted to one of those measurements and are admitted as targets:
what a user asks for, at the map's own footprint and year. The first model
assumes the dominant predictors of a biosphere channel are the local
atmosphere and soil, which every token already carries, and gives land no
drift; that is an assumption about the first model, not about land ---
seed dispersal, river transport, fire spread, hydrology, disturbance and
management all move signals across land, and fire already has the wind as
its carrier. Species occurrences enter as a channel only once a model of
observation effort exists: a record says a species was seen, its absence
says nothing about whether it was there, and a generic Gaussian channel
cannot tell absence from lack of reporting; until then \code{gbif} is
stored and not read.

\subsection{The evaluation contract: periods, availability,
lineage}\label{sec:data-split}

\paragraph{Periods.} The record is divided into three kinds of period, and
every learned component --- codecs, tile caches, the linear inverse model,
read-outs, loss weights, calibration --- obeys the same division.
\emph{Training} is 1982--2020 less the \emph{development} years 2009 and
2017, which are held out of training, climatology and every statistic and
are where architecture, sources and training choices are compared.
\emph{Retrospective evaluation} is 2021--2024: no model is trained on it and
no statistic is fitted on it, but these years have informed design decisions
of this programme --- probes and reconstructions were scored on them, and
2023 served as a development year in the pilot --- so a score on them is
labelled retrospective and is evidence about skill conditional on the
reconstructed state, never a first look. \emph{Prospective test} is
2025-01-01 onward: no statistic, model or decision has touched it, the
tensors do not yet contain it, and it is scored as issued --- forecasts for
it are produced and archived with their issue time before the verifying
observations are ingested, and each is scored once. The prospective record
grows by one year each year; nothing is promised about a pristine period
that has already been seen.

\paragraph{Two evaluations, kept apart.} A \emph{retrospective state
forecast} may read every declared retrospective analysis product at its
stored bin, with the product's support audited, and establishes skill
conditional on that reconstructed state. A \emph{forecast as issued at $t$}
reads only rows with $\code{available\_at}\le t$ and establishes deployable
skill. Every score names which of the two it is. The difference is not
small: the monthly Argo analysis (\S\ref{sec:data-tensor}) is stored
mid-month, summarises the whole month and is released a week after it, so a
retrospective forecast issued mid-month reads a month of the future's
profiles that an as-issued forecast does not; the reanalysis ocean
assimilates observations from after the bin it is stamped with; an annual
map summarises a year. The size of each such gap on the scored metrics is
measured on the complete pipeline, not assumed.

\paragraph{Lineage holdouts.} Holding out a measurement does not hold out
the products fitted to it: a hidden Argo profile still shapes the gridded
Argo analysis and the assimilating reanalysis of the same month, a hidden
laser footprint may have supervised a canopy map or a tile cache. For a
forecast of later independent observations this is not a leak; for a claim
of independent reconstruction or of generalisation across sources it is.
Such a claim is therefore scored under a lineage holdout --- the
\code{lineage} field groups a measurement with every product derived from it
and the group is withheld together --- and is not made otherwise.

\paragraph{Admissibility and its check.} An example is admissible only if
every bin its forward pass can see --- every past token, every future
target, every point observation's exact time --- lies in the training
period. The check is deterministic, not sampled: for each anchor bin the
earliest input lag and the latest target lead are compared with the period
boundaries, and the representative boundary cases (an anchor whose two-year
window crosses a development year, whose target year crosses the
retrospective boundary, whose nearest profile falls in a later bin) are
enumerated and refused before a run. A random draw of anchors is a
diagnostic that runs beside it, never the certificate.

\paragraph{Longitude holes.} Meridional blocks are withheld from targets and
from every fitted statistic; inputs inside them are not hidden, and the
underlying observations of derived products are not touched. What a
longitude hole measures is therefore the forecast head's generalisation to
places whose targets it never fitted while it could still read their
inputs --- one kind of generalisation, named as such and scored apart from
interpolation --- not source generalisation and not independent
reconstruction, which are the lineage holdout's.

\paragraph{The manifest.} One machine-readable split manifest per run
records the period boundaries, the source-contract fields of every group
read, the lineage groups withheld, the evaluation mode (retrospective or
as-issued) of every reported score, and the outcome of the admissibility
check; a result without its manifest is not reported.

\section{Training and scaling}\label{sec:scale}

Data is priced in observed values and their independence, not bytes. A
$0.25^\circ$ cell is correlated over hundreds of kilometres and, at the
surface, months, so a basin's $10^9$ values are $10^6$--$10^7$ independent
ones --- the regime in which a small model equals a large one. The globe adds
regimes, the spheres add physics, daily cadence is mostly the same sample
five times, the kilometre tier adds new variance. Order: globe and spheres,
then footprint, cadence last (Table~\ref{tab:ladder}).

Each rung is bought on the previous rung's read-out under the frozen
protocol with the null ladder beside it. The test that a rung is
over-parameterised is not a step count --- a held-out minimum ``inside
2{,}000 steps'' depends on batch size and learning-rate schedule and does
not travel between runs --- but a learning curve: the held-out loss against
examples processed, and, before any move from millions to billions of
parameters, a $2\times2$ of two data extents at fixed size and two sizes at
fixed data and compute, with examples and tokens processed recorded per
cell. A rung whose larger cell does not beat its smaller one at equal data,
and whose larger-data cell does beat its smaller one at equal size, is
data-limited and the next data rung is bought; the reverse buys parameters;
neither pattern points at the representation, the objective, the
optimisation or the target noise rather than at scale, and those are tested
before either is bought. L1: a 7--125\,M codec on the $0.25^\circ$ pentad
grid, fixed against learned geometry. L2: 0.3--1\,B codec and forecaster
with the tier-P stores; the width ladder is re-measured here, the one test
that separates data-limited from compute-limited. L3: 1--4.5\,B,
sphere-routed experts, all spheres: do land and atmosphere improve the
ocean's rolled skill and vice versa. L4: daily cadence, the first
compute-bound rung. L5: the kilometre tier, after L4 is positive.

\paragraph{Cost.} The forward cost of the codec is not $6N$ per tensor token,
because a cone codec reads each cell--pentad once per anchor whose cone
contains it. The unit is the \emph{anchor}: an example of ${\approx}$900
input tokens cross-attended into a latent set of 64--256 vectors. The
measured anchor is a 7\,M-parameter cone codec of 748 tokens per anchor at
$20{,}000$ steps $\times$ 256 anchors, 58 minutes on one RTX~4090, or
$1.1\times10^6$ input tokens a second; at that rate the input projection and
cross-attention, not the latent stack, set the time. Scaling the latent
stack to $10^9$ parameters at the same anchor budget ($5.1\times10^6$
anchors, $4.6\times10^9$ input tokens) costs of order 10--130 RTX-4090-hours
depending on where the parameters sit --- the lower figure if the latent
stack alone grows, the upper if $6N$ is spent on every input token --- and a
sustained 66\,TFLOP\,s$^{-1}$ (40\% of the card's dense bf16 peak) is the
throughput assumed. That budget touches about $0.3\%$ of the
${\approx}$$1.8\times10^9$ ocean cell--pentads as anchors; a pass that uses
every one of them once is $10^4$--$10^5$ RTX-4090-hours and is not planned.
``Epoch'' below therefore means a pass over the anchor budget, and the
four-epoch rule \cite{datacon} --- a language-model heuristic for repeated
data --- is not the binding constraint; the number of distinct observed
values the anchor budget has seen is, and it is logged. Every stated cost is
replaced by measured anchor throughput, encoder and decoder token counts,
loader time and cache-construction time before the rung is bought. The
aperture and ring cost one multiply per token. The gather is host-bound and
batched by latitude row. Every artefact is written incrementally to durable
storage while a run lives. A configuration is a named recipe; a resumed run
derives its architecture from its checkpoint. One seed is a direction, never
a level; the first result at a new tier buys its replication, and a
probe-scored claim needs three seeds.

\section{Evaluation methodology}\label{sec:eval}

\subsection{Protocol and baselines}\label{sec:eval-protocol}

Periods, evaluation modes and lineage as in \S\ref{sec:data-split}; every
score is labelled retrospective or as-issued. \textbf{Starts.} Forecasts are
issued from every pentad of an evaluation year where inference cost permits,
and from no fewer than one start a month; three starts a year under-sample
the seasons and the dependence on the forecast origin. Overlapping forecasts
are not independent: intervals come from a block bootstrap over starts with
blocks that preserve the temporal dependence of the lead being scored
\cite{goddard}, reported with their sensitivity to block length and with the
number of distinct years and events behind them, and a paired comparison is
always paired by start and seed. A few years support no claim about
multi-year timescales however many pixels they contain, and training seeds
add optimisation variability, not climate histories. \textbf{Scores.} Per
lead and channel, under one explicit set of physical area weights and fixed
channel and region weights: mean-squared skill against climatology, raw and
damped persistence; anomaly correlation; amplitude ratio and mean bias;
per scope (trained and held-out longitudes). Beside the dense field, a fixed
\emph{decoded panel} that the dense score cannot stand in for, because more
pixels are not more independent El Ni\~no events or overturning transitions:
area-mean SST over named regions (Ni\~no-3.4, the North Atlantic subpolar
gyre, the tropical Indian Ocean), upper-ocean heat content to 700\,dbar
where Argo coverage supports it, the Ni\~no-3.4 index as an exact
area-weighted integral, each at leads of one, three, six and twelve months;
and for one supported event class (an El Ni\~no onset, defined by a
threshold on the index) the probability of exceeding the threshold, the
event's duration and its accumulated anomaly. The panel is fixed here and
not extended before the first result. \textbf{Slow background.} For
seasonal and interannual leads every score is reported twice: against a
training-only evolving climatology (a linear trend plus the harmonic
seasonal cycle, fitted through the issue time) and against the stationary
climatology, so trend reproduction and internal-variability prediction are
visible separately; the trend is part of the target and is not removed.
\textbf{Proxies.} A score is named for what it measures: a surface-CO$_2$
error is not an air--sea flux skill, a velocity or a transport at one
section is not the overturning, a canopy height at a footprint is not a
vegetation change; each is scored as the precursor or proxy it is unless the
named quantity is scored with its own observation and aggregation
assumptions stated. \textbf{The null ladder} beside every read-out:
climatology, persistence, damped persistence (per-cell AR(1) on training
years), the LIM in data and in embedding space \cite{penland,newman}, a
direct multi-lead regression on compact features, an analogue forecast, the
previous rung. \textbf{Decision rule.} Each comparison names one primary
score --- the area-weighted mean over the scored leads and channels of the
mean-squared skill against damped persistence, unless stated otherwise ---
with a practical improvement threshold and an equivalence margin fixed in
advance, and a short list of non-degradation checks on the panel. A
difference inside the equivalence margin at the paired interval is
``equivalent''; a difference the interval cannot resolve is
``inconclusive'', which is not evidence of equality; an inconclusive
comparison keeps the simpler arm operationally and reports the uncertainty.
Requiring a win at every lead and every seed is not a rule here: it is
brittle and biased to the shortest leads. \textbf{Evaluation inputs.} The
query and target set of an evaluation is frozen and identical across arms;
each trained model reads it through its own intended, admissible input
sampler, so a model with a learned cone is evaluated with the cone it
learned. Forcing every arm through the fixed sampler tests behaviour under
an input change, not the intended deployment, and is reported as a separate
diagnostic where it is run. \textbf{External baseline.} On the atmospheric
channels at leads up to fifteen days the external baseline is FGN
\cite{fgn}, a data-driven ensemble trained on ERA5 and fine-tuned on the
ECMWF operational analysis, whose released checkpoints (WeatherNext~2) are
run here over 2023 from the analysis they were fine-tuned on; its 6-hourly
$0.25^\circ$ members are averaged to the pentad bins and the $1^\circ$ cells
the atmospheric group holds, so both models are scored on the same output
support. Equal output support is not equal input information: FGN starts
from an instantaneous global analysis, this model from a pentad observation
stream, so the comparison is a \emph{system benchmark}, not a matched
comparison, and it is labelled one. Its conventions are fixed: the issue
time is the end of the input averaging window; each forecast's valid
interval is the pentad it means; precipitation is the pentad accumulation
of each system's own rate, averaged the same way. Scores: fair CRPS of the
ensemble, ensemble-mean RMSE and spread--skill ratio, per channel and lead,
and CRPS pooled over $10^\circ$ blocks. Configurations are compared on the
dense field and the panel; the five read-outs are checks with limited power,
kept without letting an inconclusive read-out veto a representation. A
winner is replicated with a second seed before it is written up; a negative
result is reported like a positive one.

\subsection{Pre-registered comparisons}\label{sec:eval-compare}

\textbf{(0) The forecasting benchmark}, first, before any geometry is
learned or any store admitted, because a reconstruction that works, a
sampling pattern that is physically plausible and billions of measurements
do not by themselves show that the representation improves a forecast of
the quantities of interest beyond cheap models given the same information,
and the pilot found a current-state read-out from the codec at parity with
the raw fields and a linear model stronger beyond two weeks
\cite{techreport}. Existing data, the fixed hourglass, the smallest
practical model, one common information set --- the same forecast origins,
variables, history, targets and preprocessing --- and four arms:
\textbf{B1} damped persistence and a data-space LIM on compressed
(EOF) features, the skill already available cheaply; \textbf{B2} a direct
multi-lead regularised regression on compact raw or EOF features over
selected lags, which says whether reading history and fitting each lead
directly explains any gain before it is credited to nonlinear dynamics or
to cone geometry; \textbf{B3} the fixed codec plus an embedding-space LIM,
which says whether the representation is a better compression for
forecasting; \textbf{B4} B3 plus a small nonlinear residual, which says
whether there is predictable nonlinear signal beyond it. Scored in decoded
physical space, on SST at one, three and six months and one subsurface
quantity (heat content to 700\,dbar), with the analogue baseline where
affordable and ENSO and transport as secondary checks; one land task is
added only where a record long enough exists. Runtime and paired
uncertainty are part of the table. The decision this buys: if B3 does not
beat B2 on the primary score, a smaller codec and a modest decoded forecast
supervision are tried before any source is imported or any geometry
learned; if B4 does not beat B3, the residual is not grown. One error
decomposition accompanies the table --- by lead, season, region, slow
background against anomaly, and on one scientific aggregate --- to say
whether the limiting factor is the representation, the dynamics, the
calibration or the data coverage.

\textbf{(1) Hourglass against snapshot codec}, both frozen, same forecaster:
one-step ratio, future-cone families against their bars, the T1$-$T2 gap.

\textbf{(2) Geometry}, run only if (0) shows a limitation that geometry
can address, at equal token and compute budget, same tensor, codec size and
steps, three seeds each, the target set fixed and shared: \textbf{A0} fixed
(the control); \textbf{A1} learned per channel, hierarchical, from A0's
initialisation; \textbf{A1g} learned per group, which A1 must beat to earn
its extra estimation burden; \textbf{A2} as A1 with the ocean-surface drift
primed at $-\mathbf{u}_{\text{clim}}\Delta t$. Read-outs, fixed now:
\textbf{R1} the primary score of \S\ref{sec:eval-protocol} on T1, with the
per-lead losses against their bars as the non-degradation checks;
\textbf{R2} the T1$-$T2 gap; \textbf{R3}, descriptive only, per-channel drift
against its expected carrier over the world ocean where the carrier exceeds
$0.2$\,m\,s$^{-1}$ --- currents against $-\mathbf{u}_{\text{clim}}$,
height's zonal drift positive (source to the east), ice against the reversed
wind turned $5$--$30^\circ$, SST against the reversed wind at lags 0--1,
deep levels against the reversed reanalysis velocity at their depth;
\textbf{R4}, descriptive only, at an anchor north of the $26.5^\circ$N array,
surface drift south-west up the Florida Current and the drift of the
${\ge}1{,}000$\,dbar channels north up the deep western boundary current,
with attention on deep profiles north and shallow south. Verdict: A1 is
adopted if it beats A0 on R1 by more than the practical threshold at the
paired interval with no check degraded; A1 within the equivalence margin of
A1g keeps the per-group form as the cheaper equal; A2 beating A1 with A1 at
A0 sets the geometry from climatology and is read as a statement about
optimisation, regularisation and initialisation, not as proof that gradients
cannot find upstream; none winning keeps A0. R3 and R4 are reported whatever
the verdict and decide nothing. First ablations after: A1 without the ring,
and A1 with the ring replaced by a coarse global summary of equal token
budget.

\textbf{(3) Forecaster}: the LIM backbone alone (its decoded skill and
finite-lead amplification reported first), backbone plus zero-initialised
residual, the residual with a fine-tuned $\mathbf{L}$, and the transformer
alone; direct multi-lead against rolled one-step; log-spaced against dense
lags. A constrained propagator (a real decay spectrum, or damped $2\times2$
rotation blocks) is an arm only if the unconstrained backbone shows
transient growth that costs skill, with the trade-off stated: suppressing
growth or oscillation may remove predictability.

\textbf{(4) Footprint and tiers}: coarse groups as offset tokens with true
footprints against nearest-cell copies; the stores of Table~\ref{tab:gf} and
the point families with against without, per read-out --- El Ni\~no by
Ni\~no-3.4 at six months against warm-water-volume persistence; carbon by
held-out surface CO$_2$ against a climatological null; overturning by the
velocity loss with drifter tokens against without; SST by the level-3 cell
loss with drifter and ship tokens against without; the land biosphere by
held-out tower carbon flux against a per-site day-of-year climatology and
damped persistence, and by canopy height at held-out laser footprints
against the 2020 map and the footprint's previous value --- under the
lineage holdout of \S\ref{sec:data-split}, since the map and the AlphaEarth
cache were fitted to the same laser --- with fluorescence at held-out
soundings as the dense check --- and per store on the read-out
it was admitted for; the coastal tile codec with against without tile
tokens, three seeds; AlphaEarth's cache in place of the tile codec, scored
on the canopy read-out as well, since that embedding was fitted to the same
laser.

\textbf{(5) Scale}: the width ladder at L2; sphere-routed experts against a
dense model of equal active parameters at L3.

\textbf{(6) Weather}: the shared atmospheric channels --- 2\,m temperature,
10\,m wind, surface pressure, precipitation --- at leads of one, two and
three pentads against FGN on 2023 (a retrospective year here and FGN's own
test year), with damped persistence and climatology beside both. The forecaster is not a weather model: it reads pentad means
and predicts pentad means, and FGN is scored on the same means. The
comparison says how much of the medium-range atmosphere a model built for
the coupled system at pentad cadence gives up, and whether the ocean, land
and biosphere stores buy any of it back (arms with and without them).

\textbf{(7) Completion}: T4c on its fixed pair list against its
predict-the-mean bar, the one comparison that licenses a claim of
cross-variable inference.

Build order: the evaluation contract and its manifest
(\S\ref{sec:data-split}); the forecasting benchmark (0) with the fixed
hourglass and the error decomposition; the forecaster's backbone and
residual (3); then, and only where (0) has located a limitation they
address, geometry (2), task mix and aperture; then one source family chosen
against a documented error, with its lineage-safe holdout and its
incremental cost (4); replication of any promising effect before scale (5);
tiers and the external benchmark (6) last. A comparison's outcome changes
what is built next; the contract does not change.

\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}

Global data-driven weather models \cite{pangu,graphcast,aifs,fgn} and their
hybrids with a dynamical core \cite{neuralgcm}, atmospheric foundation
models \cite{aurora,climax}, end-to-end systems reading observations
\cite{aardvark}, ocean emulators \cite{samudra,oceannet} and coupled
emulators \cite{ace2} read one grid, one variable set and one reach. This
design reads every source at its own footprint through one token, samples a
per-channel cone, and predicts through a query carrying the footprint of
the answer. Speed times time as a receptive field has precedents: an ocean
emulator drops lateral boundaries because eddies travel a bounded distance
over the horizon \cite{oceannet}; a neural semi-Lagrangian layer gathers at
$\mathbf{x}-\Delta t\,\mathbf{u}$ with one velocity shared across
transported modes \cite{paradis}; deformable attention learns offsets per
query and head with a learning-rate multiplier \cite{deformable}. Truncation
by memory, a cone per channel, and a learnable ellipse with a hierarchical
per-channel drift are, to the authors' knowledge, not stated as a principle
elsewhere; per-variable tokenisation \cite{aurora,climax} changes what a
variable is embedded as, not where it is read from. The architecture is a
masked autoencoder \cite{mae} with Perceiver cross-attention
\cite{perceiver}; AlphaEarth Foundations \cite{alphaearth} is the closest
embedding-field model, over land at annual cadence, and enters here as a
token cache; geospatial tile models \cite{prithvi} embed imagery. Global
canopy-height maps \cite{potapov,lang,tolan} regress one year of laser
footprints from imagery, one map per year; here the footprints are targets
in their own coordinates and the maps are one more target, so the forecast
of a canopy is scored against the laser, not against a map. Frozen
backbones with shallow read-outs \cite{lehmann} are the evaluation stance,
judged by what a forecaster predicts from the representation rather than by
a current-state probe. The LIM \cite{penland,newman} and the
interannual-to-decadal verification framework \cite{goddard} are the nulls
and scores, with Murphy's decomposition \cite{murphy} as the calibration
check; the decoupled objective is the faithful heteroscedastic regression of
\cite{faithful}, distinct from $\beta$-NLL \cite{betanll}; the fair CRPS is
Ferro's estimator \cite{ferro2008,ferro2014,leutbecher} within the proper
scoring rules of \cite{crps}; the four-epoch rule \cite{datacon} and the
compute-optimal ratio \cite{chinchilla} are planning heuristics; sparse
experts \cite{switch}. The physics of the per-channel carriers is from
\cite{chelton,thorndike,sutton,meinen}; the overturning array
\cite{mccarthy2015} and Argo \cite{argo} enter as observations in their own
coordinates.
