# Response to the 22 September 2026 review of the design paper

**Reviewed document:** *Predicting any observed quantity of the Earth*, 22 September 2026, 16 pages, SHA-256 `78ca125e…180a9` (the build at commit `20640e3`).

## Overall verdict

The review is valuable and, on the points that matter most, understated rather than overstated. Of its ten sections and the work order, nine are accepted in full, one (data admission and scaling) is accepted with one modification, and none is rejected. The three findings with the largest consequence are the evaluation contract (§1), the cost arithmetic (§10, §4 of the paper) and the order of work (§2); the mathematical corrections (§4, §10) are all confirmed. Several claims in the paper turned out to be wrong in ways the reviewer did not see and the fact-check did; they are listed at the end and are corrected in the same revision.

## 1. Evaluation contract — accepted; the situation is worse than the review says

The reviewer's claim was that 2023 sits in both the development set and the "once-only" terminal test, so the terminal test is no longer pristine. Confirmed: paper §3.5 held out 2009, 2017, 2023 for development and reserved 2021–2024 for the terminal test, and §5.1 ran the external benchmark on 2023. The fact-check found more. The years 2021–2024 were not merely adjacent to development: E-076a (the nearest-observation Argo experiment) reported probe correlations on "terminal years 2021–2024" for three runs (`ml/EXPERIMENTS.md:1395–1403`) and judged its build-or-not falsifier on them, E-076b tabulated optimal-interpolation scores on the same split, and every model before E-068 trained on 2021, 2022 and 2024. `REBOOT_PLAN.md:171` asked for 2021–2024 "untouched by any development decision"; that condition has not held since E-076.

The reviewer's second claim, that the bin-level exclusion is insufficient because the monthly Roemmich–Gilson Argo field is stamped mid-month while summarising the whole month and being released after it, is confirmed from the code (`build_family7.py:2349`, `build_family4.py:313–322`) and from the product's own documentation (each monthly map is an objective analysis of that calendar month's profiles, released typically 2–10 days after month end, sometimes months later). Nothing in the repository records availability times, and no registry field carries `support_start`, `support_end`, `available_at`, product version or lineage (the family-1 and family-10 registry writers were read line by line). The reviewer's third claim, that longitude holes should say what they exclude, is confirmed: `train.py` removes held-out longitudes from targets and from the fitted statistics only, never from inputs, and derived products are untouched. And the paper's "brute-force count over a large anchor draw" is, in `train_cone.py:1379` and `cone_sampler.certify`, a uniform random draw of at most 4,096 anchors that checks time bins only — a diagnostic, exactly as the reviewer suspected, not a certificate.

What changed in the paper: §3.5 is rewritten as an evaluation contract. Training is 1982–2020 less development years 2009 and 2017; 2021–2024 is labelled *retrospective evaluation* and the paper states that these years have informed design decisions; the *prospective test* begins 2025-01-01, which no statistic, model or decision has touched and which the tensors do not yet contain, scored as issued with forecasts archived before verification data are ingested. Two evaluation modes (retrospective state forecast; forecast as issued at *t*) are defined and every score must carry one. The registry gains a source contract — `support_start`, `support_end`, `available_at`, `product_version`, `lineage` — and §3.1 states the Argo field's true support and availability. Lineage holdouts are defined and required for any claim of independent reconstruction or source generalisation; the canopy read-out in comparison (4) is put under one. The admissibility check is specified as deterministic boundary enumeration with the random draw demoted to a diagnostic. Longitude holes are defined as what they measure. A machine-readable split manifest is the acceptance condition. The abstract and premise (iv) are aligned.

## 2. The simplest forecasting experiment first — accepted

The reviewer argues that the paper fixes geometry, task mix, aperture and maps before showing that the representation improves a forecast beyond cheap models with the same information, and that the pilot's own results (current-state read-out at parity with raw fields; linear inverse model stronger beyond ten days) make this the sharp question. Both facts are in the archived report and premise (ii) of the paper already concedes the second. The four-arm design (damped persistence and data-space LIM; direct multi-lead regression on compact features with history; fixed codec plus embedding-space LIM; the same plus a small nonlinear residual) is a clean attribution ladder, and the second arm is the one the paper lacked: a history-reading model needs a history-reading linear comparator before a gain is credited to nonlinear dynamics or to cone geometry.

What changed: comparison (0), the forecasting benchmark, is added ahead of (1) with the four arms, decoded-space scoring on SST at one, three and six months and heat content to 700 dbar, runtime and paired uncertainty in the table, a pre-registered decision (if B3 does not beat B2, a smaller codec and modest decoded supervision are tried before any source is imported or geometry learned), and one error decomposition. The build order is rewritten: contract and manifest, benchmark, backbone and residual, then geometry only where the benchmark locates a limitation geometry addresses, then one source family against a documented error, replication before scale, tiers and the external benchmark last. The sentence "no comparison changes it" is replaced by "a comparison's outcome changes what is built next; the contract does not change." The paper remains a design paper and keeps the learnable geometry as its design; what moves is the order in which the design is tested.

## 3. The cone as a sampling prior; fixed training targets — accepted

The reviewer is right that §2.1 presented the cone and its mirror as a physical fact and that predictive relevance is not causal influence. The reworded §2.1 states the cone as a transport-inspired sampling prior supplemented by an ungated far field, and says the learned geometry is read as a diagnostic, never as a recovered transport law. The ring's "guarantees no driver is excluded" is withdrawn; a coarse global summary of equal token budget is added as the comparator (an ablation in comparison (2)).

On targets the fact-check adds a nuance: the E-080 plan already fixes the target set in its first phase, but after every re-bake in the hardening phase the mirrored targets are redrawn from the learned ellipses, so the target distribution does move (`E080_hourglass_cone.md:214, 278`), and the paper's Guards paragraph did not mention the fixed phase at all. The reviewer's fix is adopted outright: target queries are drawn from the fixed hourglass in every phase and shared across arms and seeds; the mirror of the learned cone is not a training target; whether a learned mirrored target set buys skill becomes a later, separately guarded comparison. The target-variance flag R5 is dropped because it no longer has anything to detect. R3 and R4 become descriptive; the verdict rule that read A2 beating A1 as "the gradient cannot find upstream" is rewritten as a statement about optimisation, regularisation and initialisation.

## 4. The linear backbone — accepted in full

Both mathematical points are confirmed. A propagator `U diag(exp(−ℓ/τ_j)) U⁻¹` with real positive time constants has a real decay spectrum only, so it cannot represent a general linear inverse model, whose operator is a real non-normal matrix with complex-conjugate eigenvalue pairs (damped oscillatory modes); and with non-orthogonal `U` it does not bound the Euclidean norm, so "contractive" was not established — transient amplification while every mode decays is the "optimal growth" of Penland and Sardeshmukh's title. The pilot's LIM (`lim_baseline.py`) was fitted as `C(τ)C(0)⁻¹` on principal components and recorded only |λ|, so the paper's "at step zero the forecaster is the embedding-space LIM" was a claim about a restricted fit, not the LIM. None of the backbone exists in code, so the correction is free.

What changed: the equation now defines the backbone as the fitted, regularised LIM propagator `exp(ℓL)` itself, retained unchanged, plus the zero-initialised residual, so the step-zero statement is exact. It is called asymptotically stable (spectral radius below one, checked at fit time), not contractive; the paper says why, and says that stability of `G` says nothing about the residual or the multi-year roll. The backbone's decoded skill per lead and its finite-lead amplification are reported before the residual is trained; fine-tuning `L` is a separate arm; a constrained parameterisation (real decay spectrum or damped 2×2 rotation blocks) is an arm only if the unconstrained backbone shows growth that costs skill, with the trade-off stated. The abstract's "contractive" is replaced.

## 5. Objective and primary metrics — accepted

Accepted as written: explicit physical area weights and fixed channel and region weights; a fixed decoded panel (area-mean SST over named regions, upper-ocean heat content where Argo covers, Niño-3.4 as an exact area-weighted integral, one event class with threshold probability, duration and accumulated anomaly), fixed now and not extended before the first result; proxies named as proxies (surface CO₂ is not air–sea flux, a velocity or a section transport is not the overturning, canopy height is not vegetation change); a decoded loss at the scored leads rather than at three arbitrary ones, with the two mismatches the reviewer names — decoded mean of a latent versus mean of decoded forecasts through a nonlinear decoder, and the shift a read-out trained on true embeddings meets on forecast embeddings — measured on the complete pipeline and, if dominant, addressed with more decoded loss rather than more model; known integrals computed exactly, the attention read-out kept only for instrument aggregates with no closed form (the array transport). The read-outs are kept as limited-power checks that cannot veto a representation on an inconclusive result.

## 6. Uncertainty — accepted

The paper's sentence that an unsupported query (SST over land) "returns a variance that says so, because the variance head was never rewarded for confidence there" was a non sequitur, as the reviewer says: behaviour outside support is unconstrained. Replaced by support flags computed from the training-target registry — invalid queries refused, extrapolations flagged. Seed spread is renamed seed sensitivity; lead- and region-conditioned residual calibration on a calibration split is the probabilistic baseline that any forecast distribution must beat; the fair CRPS estimator is written out with its citations; the FGN claim is scoped to "one weather architecture, a reason to try, no guarantee"; joint structure is scored directly (area-average and duration distributions) with the member-permutation diagnostic; the three uncertainty sources are kept apart and never counted twice; transformed mean, physical median and physical mean are distinguished.

## 7. Initialised prediction versus projection — accepted

A scope paragraph is added to the introduction and a scope sentence to the abstract, in the reviewer's sense: initialised forecasts over five days to one year; multi-year roll a mode not the objective; no claim about scenario-conditioned change, tipping times or century-scale behaviour; a projection needs a forcing pathway and an evaluation under changed forcing, which a longer roll is not. Every seasonal and interannual score is reported against a training-only evolving climatology (trend plus seasonal cycle) as well as the stationary one, with the trend kept as part of the target.

## 8. Using the verification record — accepted

Starts move from three a year to every pentad where inference cost permits and never fewer than monthly; overlapping forecasts are not counted as independent; block bootstrap with block-length sensitivity and the count of distinct years and events; paired by start and seed. Each comparison names one primary score with a practical threshold and an equivalence margin, plus non-degradation checks, and distinguishes "equivalent" from "inconclusive"; the "every lead ≤ 3 at every seed" rule is withdrawn as brittle and short-lead-biased. The evaluation query set is frozen across arms and each model reads it through its own intended sampler; forcing all arms through the fixed sampler becomes a separate diagnostic. This overturns the paper's earlier "evaluation geometry is frozen to the fixed hourglass for every arm", and the reviewer's reasoning is right: that tested behaviour under an input change, not the intended deployment. The FGN comparison is labelled a system benchmark with unmatched input information, its issue time, valid interval and precipitation rule fixed. The fact-check also corrected the paper's description of FGN: the released WeatherNext 2 checkpoints are fine-tuned on the ECMWF operational analysis, not ERA5, and evaluate at 56 members (four seeds × 14), not 64.

## 9. Data admission and scaling — accepted, with one modification

Accepted: the 2,000-step over-parameterisation test is replaced by a learning-curve criterion and, before any move to billions of parameters, a 2×2 of two data extents at fixed size and two sizes at fixed data, with examples and tokens processed recorded; the compute-optimal ratio and the four-epoch rule are labelled planning heuristics, not scaling laws; observation counts are not independent information, so tier-P sampling and verification become platform- and track-aware; the admission verdict becomes three-valued (useful; not useful at this budget; inconclusive) and a deferred store keeps its provenance; the target-channel-visible rule is kept as a copy control and a small scored task (T4c) with the target channel absent is added, the only comparison that licenses a claim of cross-variable inference; the tile codec's same-place–different-date contrastive term is switched off by default pending a change-sensitivity test; GBIF is stored but not read until an observation-effort model exists.

The modification: the reviewer's "before moving from millions to billions" test is adopted as written, but the paper adds that neither pattern of the 2×2 points at the representation, objective, optimisation or target noise, so those are tested before either scale is bought — which is the reviewer's own premise (i) point turned into a rule.

## 10. Implementation-facing ambiguities — all confirmed, all corrected

Figure 1 versus text: the figure drew ±143 pentads symmetrically from a lag list `[7,12,18,24,33,72,143]` that appears in no plan, the text listed twelve past slices including lags inside the codec's own window and a lag of 144 that exceeds the window, and the caption said "7–143 each way" while targets stop at 73. One authoritative list is now stated in §2.6 (past: lag 0 and 7, 13, 21, 34, 55, 89, 143; future: 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 21, 34, 55, 73), the figure is redrawn asymmetrically to it (`ml/plans/E080_build/figures.py` gained keyword arguments; the deck's own images are byte-identical), and the caption matches.

Growth floor: the plan text did say Σ_v is floored at the design speed while initialising it eighteen times lower; the design speed is a cap. §2.4 now distinguishes a minimum radius (waist, one cell), a maximum reach (drift and spread capped at the design reach) and a default growth rate (130 km per pentad, a starting value).

Placeholders: the paper's "strict MAE, never a placeholder" is contradicted by the E-069 codec, which uses two learned, attended placeholder embeddings in BERT style (`cone_codec.py:214–215, 352–354`), and by the paper's own miss tokens. §2.2 and §2.5 now state the three cases exactly: unfilled point slots are whole padded tokens masked from attention; never-observed entries and hidden entries are learned markers inside attended tokens.

Stochastic latent: `z` is deterministic in code and in every plan (`cone_codec.py:238`); stage 2's "mean and log-variance of z" is removed and the tokens carry `z` plus the count of observations behind it.

Per-channel geometry in stage 2: undefined anywhere; the paper now says stage 2's geometry is per sphere (an embedding token has no channel axis) and derives the token budget (8 slices × 49 ≈ 400, replacing the unsourced 600).

Eq. 5: the reference error is renamed as such (not the irreducible error); the weights are fitted on a training calibration fold, frozen and capped at 100× the median; the "stop-gradient form of Σ log MSE" claim is qualified to the case where reference equals current error and the objective actually minimised is stated.

MSSS identity: rewritten with Murphy's bias term and a common set of area weights for ACC, amplitude, bias and MSSS, and the reference climatology named.

Footprint scalar: stated as a first approximation with documented per-source support assumptions and the fine-to-coarse consistency test restricted to genuine averages.

"Nothing is resampled" and "nothing on land advects": both rewritten as the reviewer suggests.

Cost: confirmed and material. The paper's "6NT per epoch, ~30 RTX-4090-hours for a 1 B codec at four epochs" counted each tensor token once. A cone codec reads each cell–pentad once per anchor whose cone contains it; the measured anchor (E-069 run #539: 7 M parameters, 748 tokens per anchor, 20,000 steps × 256 anchors, 58 minutes on one RTX 4090) processes 3.8 billion input tokens, about 96 times the North Atlantic tensor's token count. At the same anchor budget a 1 B codec costs of order 10–130 RTX-4090-hours depending on where the parameters sit; a pass using every ocean cell–pentad as an anchor (≈1.8 billion) costs 10⁴–10⁵ and is not planned. §4 now says so, defines "epoch" as a pass over the anchor budget, notes that the four-epoch rule is therefore not binding, and requires measured throughput, token counts and loader and cache times before a rung is bought. Table 3's caption says its token column bounds distinct information, not compute.

References: reference [1] now carries both dates; Eq. 4 is attributed to Stirn et al. (2023), *Faithful heteroscedastic regression with neural networks*, which is exactly the mean-by-squared-error, variance-by-NLL-with-detached-mean form, and the paper says why it is not β-NLL (Seitzer et al. re-weight the likelihood by a detached power of the variance and never detach the mean); the fair CRPS is cited to Ferro, Richardson and Weigel (2008), Ferro (2014) and Leutbecher (2019), with Gneiting and Raftery kept for proper scoring rules generally; Murphy (1988) is added.

## Work order — accepted

The reviewer's five deliverables are the paper's new build order. The first (amend scope, contract, availability rules and mathematical claims, preserving results and distinguishing retrospective from prospective) is this revision. The second to fifth are experiments, not text, and are pre-registered in §5.2 as comparisons (0), (3), (2) and (4) in that order.

## Findings beyond the review, corrected in the same revision

The fact-check found four things the review did not raise. The E-069 codec keeps the decoder-reads-encoder-latents path open at weight 0.25 (`cone_codec.py:397–423`, `train_cone.py:149`) where the paper said "that path is closed"; the paper now says the design closes it at weight zero, and the build must follow. The paper's admissibility check as described does not exist in that form (random, time-only, and the cone trainer has no longitude holdout at all). The paper's FGN description was wrong on initial conditions and ensemble size. And the paper contradicted itself on 2023. All four are fixed in the text; the first two are also work items for the E-080 build (`E080_HANDOVER.md`).

## What this revision does not do

It does not run anything. The reviewer's central recommendation is an experiment, comparison (0), and the paper now puts it first. It does not change the design's architecture: the hourglass, the learnable geometry, the query decoder, the tiers and the linear backbone stand; what changed is what is fixed (targets), what is claimed (a prior, not a law; stable, not contractive; a system benchmark, not a matched one), what is measured first, and what the evaluation contract promises. Two decisions are Chris's and are stated in the paper as defaults that can be reversed: dropping 2023 as a development year in favour of 2009 and 2017 alone, and declaring 2025 onward the prospective period, which requires extending the tensors past 2024-12-31 before the first as-issued score exists.
